*[Transcript, Driftline orientation broadcast, courier band, all hands encouraged. Sound cues in brackets. Performed by whoever wasn't riding.]*

## Act One: Three Riders

**ANNOUNCER:** New couriers! Gather at the fire or the radio, whichever is nearer. Tonight's ballad is true except where it's better. It is the story of a bike. *[engine cough, laughter]* I said a *bike*.

**CHORUS OF COURIERS:** *Small frame, long heart, rust on the starboard rail — who carries the couriers? The Wren, the Wren.*

**ANNOUNCER:** The Wren was built in the third decade of the Delay for a rider named Okafor, who delivered vaccines through three walls of sand and retired owing the bike her gratitude. Second came Bellamy, the mathematician, who calculated every drift and was wrong about all of them beautifully. And third came Paloma Reyes. *[fire crackle]* Say her name back, new riders. That's the toll for the story.

**NEW RIDERS:** Paloma Reyes.

## Act Two: The Diphtheria Run

**ANNOUNCER:** Paloma rode the Wren faster than the spec sheet allowed and slower than her grandmother advised, and when the diphtheria run came up in the season's worst wall — a storm the weather desk's own instruments declined to believe — every senior rider shook their head, and Paloma said the medicine was for children, and children don't wait out weather.

**KETCH** *(played by Ketch, unconvincingly disguised):* I told her the intake seals were old. She told me the seals were exactly her age. I have never won an argument with that bike or its riders.

**ANNOUNCER:** She made the pan. The record's clear on that. Medicine delivered, children saved, wall at her heels. Coming home, the storm took the Glassroad's east shoulder — *[wind swell]* — and that is where the ballad runs out of Paloma. Eleven days later the Wren limped into Saltmouth alone, intake shredded, tank bone-dry, saddle still warm, the way some say. She parked herself outside the garage. Engines don't park themselves. Except she did.

**KETCH:** I kept her thirty years. Folks said scrap her, part her out, she's just a frame with a name. I said she's not a frame with a name. She's a *promise* with a throttle. *[beat]* Someone would come along with a debt and good hands and no bike. Promises should be kept running.

**CHORUS:** *Small frame, long heart, outruns what outlives her — who carries the couriers? The Wren, the Wren.*

**ANNOUNCER:** And that, new riders, is whose saddle you're borrowing, whoever you are tonight. Ride her kindly. Drift her well. And if the flats ever hum in her key — hum back. *[engine note, single, long fade]*

**KETCH:** Next week: "The Manifest of Errors," a comedy. Goodnight, Driftline. Any crate, any storm, any door.
