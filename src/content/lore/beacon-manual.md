*Driftline Tender's Circular 12, Glass Desert Loop revision, copy 4 of 9. Copies 1 through 3 were used for ballast, which the manual allows for in section zero ("the manual is an asset; assets serve"). Passed courier to courier. Margin notes in at least six hands, two of them famous.*

## What a beacon is

A beacon is a promise with a battery. Six metres of mast, a cage of amber cells at the top, a solar skirt that drinks all day and glows all night, and a bell — always a bell, even where nobody lives, especially where nobody lives. Two hundred and three beacons on the Loop, spaced at roughly one visible from the next, which in flat country is far and in dune country is a joke the wind is telling.

Rule one: a beacon is never off. Rule two: see rule one. Rule three is a list of acceptable excuses and it is blank.

## Spacing and reason

Spaced by sight, not distance. A new tender always asks for the distance chart and the old tender always hands them a dune. Stand on it. Can you see the next light? No? Then the chart was a lie, like most charts, and you will plant a relay mast at the crest and tell no one, because masts cost guild and lights cost us.

Bell facing downwind. Always. The wind rings them free of charge, all night, and a ringing beacon is a beacon telling the dark *we are still here and the count is unbroken.*

## Relighting procedure

Kill your engine at thirty metres. Walk in. Say the beacon's number — not a prayer, the Choir will tell you it is a prayer, it is a number, but say it like you mean it. Check the skirt for dust burial (storm season: assume burial). Check the cells for condensation. Strike the bell once with the heel of your hand. If the tone is true, the frame is sound. If the tone is flat, the frame is cracked and you ride for Saltmouth with the bad news, which travels faster than any cargo.

Relight with a Skyline flare cap, amber, never teal. Teal is the Choir's colour and the Choir does not maintain these, whatever their tracts say now.

## Spare-part etiquette

Take a cell, leave a cell. Take a flare, leave two — flares are how couriers say thank you when nobody is watching, which is the only true kind. The log cylinder in the base is for numbers, conditions, and one (1) joke. The joke is mandatory. Morale is infrastructure.

## On beacon 41

It has been reported, repeatedly, that beacon 41 on the flat south of the Cinderflats is haunted: it rings with no wind, it dims when unobserved, riders near it report a voice counting.

Ruling, after three inspections and one overnight: beacon 41 is not haunted. Beacon 41 stands farther from its neighbours than any beacon on the Loop — the flats moved, we didn't — and the wind over open salt carries the bell oddly, and the voice is the mast's own bell-wire humming against the count of the pulse relay. In other words: the beacon has been alone a long time and has learned to keep itself company. Tell it your name when you relight it. That's not in the procedure. Do it anyway.
