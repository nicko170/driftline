*Pinned behind the kettle in the Saltmouth garage, oilskin-backed card, three columns — **NAME / RIDER / STORM OF RECORD** — entries in any hand, arguments in the margin, tea ring on the lower left regarded by all parties as an official seal. Rule of the registry, written across the top in Ketch's hand: "Unofficial by long custom. Official would imply somebody governs a name. Nobody governs a name. Ask any parent, or any sandstorm."*

*The standing superstition: a bike earns its name in its **first storm**. Before that, whatever you call it is a suggestion — provisional, luckless, technically legal. The registry lists suggestions in pencil and storm-names in ink. A name may only be *ended* by weather or by a garage-keeper, which is why the red pencil hangs on a nail beside the page. Red is for endings. Somebody ends a name in red maybe twice a decade. The pencil stays sharp.*

## Entries, a selection

**PRINCIPAL** and **INTEREST** — escort skiffs of the Guild water-hauler *Second Mortgage*, Saltmouth column. Storm of record: *the note, unpaid.* Margin, three hands: "The skiffs get renamed every fiscal year and it is always this." — "The joke compounds." — "The skiffs are faster than the joke and both have held for forty seasons."

**APPROXIMATELY SAFE** — long-haul skiff, rider withheld *for legal margin*. Storm of record: year 41, the crossing afterwards known as the West Wind Inquiry. Renamed, in red, from **TOTALLY SAFE** following bipartisan review of the evidence. The rider still flinches when praised.

**F-NATURAL** — Choir long skiff, Lamp Rest. Renamed twice. The Hollow does not discuss it. The listening horn does, once a season, in precisely that note, and the crater pretends not to hear. Storm of record: *pending by unanimous lack of comment.*

**ASTERISK** — racing skiff, J. Marrows. Storm of record: *declines to confirm or deny.* Margin: "The asterisk is load-bearing. It holds the whole legend up." A second hand: "Held, past tense. Ask Varga."

**GRAVITY'S PROPERTY (ON LOAN)** — Reclaimer crawler-bike, Cinderflats yard. Storm of record: *lent, frequently.* The yard pays it hazard shares as a courtesy. It has thrown every rider it has ever tolerated, and it tolerates them all.

**[line left blank, pinned twice]** — couriers say the book keeps an entry for the missing, and that the second pin means *held open*. No name is entered until the rider is. Nobody argues with the second pin.

## The Wren page

Most-argued page in the card. Five lines for one bike:

1. *unrecorded* — the ballad claims a first rider named Okafor, vaccines, three walls of sand. The seat-pannier service book claims nobody before Pell Anyanwu, who called her nothing but kept her torque values in a greeting hand. The registry lists both and charges nothing.
2. **PLUMB** — pencil, survey tradition. Never storm-named. Pell took a desk; the suggestion expired with the posting.
3. **HALF-LAP'S ANSWER** — Mele's race name, ink, storm of record legit (Slick Mile, year of the long wind), struck through in red once, *pointedly*. Margin: "A name that boasts invites the weather to answer. Retired out of respect — K."
4. **THE WREN** — Ketch's hand, red pencil used as *ink*, which the margin calls either heresy or a promotion. Renamed after the old ballad, before anyone can remember the ballad being about *this* bike. The two documents now cite each other. The office declines comment; the office is a kettle.
5. *blank, ink-ready.* Storm of record: **pending.** "The desert is an unhurried registrar. She'll write it herself when it's earned. Good bike. Don't tell her. — K."

*Footer, in the kettle hand: "Entries open. You do not name your own bike — you witness it, like weather, like weddings. Pencil's on the nail. Bring tea. The registry runs on tea; the tea is on whoever argued in the margin last."*
