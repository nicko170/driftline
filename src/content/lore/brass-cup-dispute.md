*Minutes of the Assay Board, Guild exchange, Saltmouth. Convened to fix, once and for tariff purposes, the capacity of the official water cup under which Saltmouth bills the skydocks and half the north routes. Recording: clerk I. Vell, whose shorthand is accurate and whose sighs are considered commentary and not entered. — Exhibit filed, season of the low condensate.*

## Minute 1: The matter

Water moves north billed *by the cup*, the cup being the brass cup worn on a cord at the neck of the keeper of the Saltmouth tower, herein A., Mose. Its capacity is disputed. It has been disputed since the keeper's grandmother, who settled the last dispute by offering the assay board a drink and waiting. The board drank. The board did not finish forms that day, or that season.

## Minute 3: Testimony of A., Mose, notarised

Q. State the capacity of the cup.
A. One cup.
Q. In grams.
A. It holds a cup of water. It has been checked against the other cup, the chipped enamel one, which is for friends and is the same size on purpose.
Q. What is the enamel cup checked against?
A. The brass one. Counted twice, both ways. That is not a circle. That is a *relationship*, and it has outlasted four assay boards.
Q. For the record, were you offered a scale?
A. Weighed water is a rumour. Poured water is a promise. The tower does not deal in rumours. She condenses forty-one cups a night and I know because she *tells me.*

## Minute 5: Testimony of F., Hollis, notarised at the Top Scale

The cup was conveyed to Keel Town by courier, sealed in wax, and weighed. The scale says what it says. The scale is final per the Hang-Tenants' Agreement, and its keeper is not to be bribed with anything under a whole cake, and no cake was offered, which both parties may reflect on.

The reading was entered in the docking slate. It was not entered in the unlisted column, as the weight of a cup of water is not grief, mail, or a secret, whatever the keeper tells his tower.

*Annotation, D.S.: He weighed a cup of water. He weighed the* cup. *For the record, there is a difference, and for the record, both readings were demanded and produced.*

## Minute 9: The page both parties wish torn out

On the third afternoon the board put the same question to both witnesses separately and received the same answer, which is set down here with reluctance: that the cup's true capacity, at dawn, measured quietly, with the knots unargued, is **two hundred and fifty-seven grammes**.

There is a silence in the minutes here. The clerk has marked it *ten minutes.*

The keeper then observed that 257 was very close to what he'd been saying all along, close being a relationship, not a number. The wharf-master then observed that no measurement is official until the bell closes it, and rang the Finality Bell *on the hour coffee arrived,* which witnesses describe as tactical. Both men requested, in writing, within the same hour, that the figure be struck.

The board declined. The figure stands, attached.

## Minute 11: Finding

Capacity of the official cup: **still disputed, now with charts.** Billing continues by the cup. The charts are filed where charts go. The relationship is intact.

*Postscript, clerk Vell, off the record but in the record: what actually happened, since nobody will write it, is that the hour the number stood agreed, the Guild priced it, and by dusk there was a meter schedule for a thing that had run two hundred years on a cup and a blessing. The keeper and the wharf-master withdrew their agreement the way you withdraw a bad anchor. The dispute resumed, healthier than ever. Some arguments are load-bearing. Take away the wall and the roof remembers why it needed one.*
