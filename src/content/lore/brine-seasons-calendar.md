*Kept on parchment in the brine shed at Saltmouth, pages stiff as roof shingle, entries in a hand so broad the commas need their own weather. Ogo keeps the calendar because Ogo's grandmother kept it, and her log is bound into the back, and her mother's behind that, so that the book is less a document than a queue of women leaning over your shoulder.*

## The seasons of the pans

**Firstcrust.** The pans wake white and weak. Skim thin, walk lighter, take nothing below the second flake. Marginal note, grandmother's hand: *The pan remembers your boots longer than your face.*

**Long Bloom.** The brine colours. Each pan runs to its own private chemistry and the flats look, from the ridge, like a paint table tipped by a child of taste. Harvest the rose pans at dawn or not at all; by noon they are bragging and by dusk they are soup.

**Crackweather.** The crust splits in hexagons wide enough to swallow a hub plate. Couriers love this season, which tells you everything about couriers. Marginal note, Ogo's: *Rode the crack lines on the old bike. Do not tell the Guild. Do not tell your mother. Told your mother.*

**Stillening.** The pans go mirror-calm and the harvest is done standing, with flat shovels and conversation. This is the season the calendar exists for, and the season with the fewest entries, because everyone is busy being happy.

**Black Brine.** Rare. The deep pans turn dark and sweet and the salt that comes up cures meat like a song. Two entries in three generations. Both end with the same five words: *Ate well. Thanked the pan.*

## The teal year

Season 31 of the current binding: the Long Bloom came up **teal**, every pan at once, the same blue-green as the fused glass on the old burn road. The Guild sent an auditor, who filed a colour. The Choir filed a **jubilee** and sang at the pans for nine days. The Reclaimers offered to strip whatever machine was surely leaking into the water table, found no machine, and were honestly a little upset about it.

The salt tasted of nothing unusual. The salt has never tasted of anything unusual. The teal faded by Stillening, the harvest was ordinary and good, and the only lasting effect was that for one season, all three factions came down to the pans every evening to look at the same thing, and standing shoulder to shoulder at the rail, forgot to quarrel.

Ogo's entry for the year, whole text: *Teal. Good salt. Everyone behaved. Ask the pan.*

## Keeper's rule

The calendar's last page is blank and always has been. It is understood that the blank page is not for the future. It is for the year the pans do something none of the names cover, and someone in the queue of women will get to name it.
