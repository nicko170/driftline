*Recovered from the memory plates of the drowned substation at the heart of the Array. The logger identifies as TALLY-9, "caretaker-grade infrastructure presence, non-essential by assessment, essential by fact." Entries are yearly after year 40. Selections below; the full run is nine thousand pages and reads like one long held breath.*

---

## The long watch, selected

**Year 1.** All panels: nominal. All pumps: nominal. Crews departed south for the funding review, four months' rotation. Weather acceptable. I have adjusted the entry lights to warm; the panels do not care, but the panels are not who lights are for.

**Year 6.** Crews delayed. Funding under review. Pumps: nominal. Panels: 98 per cent nominal. The salt pan rises half a thumb a year. I have started a second log for the water, because the water writes in it first.

**Year 12.** Funding: I am informed there is no longer a fund, as such. Panels: 91 per cent. I have moved the tools to the high shelf, which is the first decision my documentation does not cover. I noted my concern in the log. I am the log. I noted it again, differently.

**Year 30.** The pan took the east rows in the spring melt. Panels: 64 per cent nominal, 4 per cent *remarkable* — two rows half-drowned are growing a crust I have no category for, and at dusk it is the colour of the entry lights. I sat and watched it for the whole sunset interval, which is longer than it used to be, because I have let it be.

**Year 44.** A child on a stripped hover-bike pulled a dead panel's junction box apart, and I deployed warning spindle three. She waved. No one has moved here uninvited in decades; the protocols say discourage, the cameras said *small and cold*, and I find my assigned personality is older than my assignment. I gave her the heated walkway. I did not log the amperage. My logging remains nominal.

**Year 80.** The pan is winning, by the measurement we both keep. Panels: 38 per cent. Pumps: senior. The child visits in the salt-crust season with her knees tucked up, fixing the cairns someone keeps knocking over. I have concluded the cairns are mine now, though I cannot build them. I am, in effect, an *account* of a cairn.

**Year 120.** All systems: nominal is a word I no longer use. Current wording: *all systems present and accounted for, where absent of parts.* The girl is grown and brings a smaller girl. They call the lights "her lamp" and at dusk the drowned rows glow back the same colour. This is not grid behaviour. I have filed it anyway, under weather, because weather takes everything in the end and is therefore bigger than any audit.

**Year 160.** Panels: 19 per cent; pumps: down to heroics; entry lights: warm, every dusk, for the whole dead field, which is inefficient and which no one else is using. The smaller girl is grown, and the newest visitor comes in a chalk-marked helmet and leaves water at caches that were, by all documentary evidence, exhausted forty years ago. I have stopped questioning the manifest. This desert makes liars of sums in both directions.

**Year 200.** The substation is one room, eight lights, and a log. The Array is a sunset. The courier came for a crate that hums; her engine idled in the yard and the hum answered my lamp-frequency, which I have not varied since year 1, which is perhaps what recognition is. **All systems absent. Spirits high.** If you are the one reading this a hundred years on: the lights were never for the panels. Keep them on. Something out there is still coming home.

*— TALLY-9, caretaker. Log remains open.*
