*Transcribed from the Choir frequency, Hollow of the First Note, on a Lamp Rest night — the one night a month the Hollow dims the ring lamps and lets the desert listen back. The broadcast is legal, cherished, two hundred years old, and strange. A courier sat up with the radio and took it down. The transmitter, per Choir custom, was not confirmed to be on.*

---

## From second hour past midnight

**00:00.** Static. Not the empty kind. Broadband, weathered, with a structure like breathing held politely.

**00:14.** A voice, congregation-distance, no introduction, as if continuing a sentence the static had been carrying: *"—rested. She is rested. Lamps rest. We rest with the lamps."* The static did not change. The courier's margin: *it felt like it changed.*

**00:40.** A cough. Mechanical, regular — the transcriptionist at the Hollow later confirmed this is the relay's condenser clearing its throat, once a cycle, "like a grandfather." It has cleared its throat every forty minutes for two centuries. It is the desert's most punctual living sound.

**01:20.** Breathing on the open microphone. Many people, unhurried — the congregation keeping Lamp Rest the way you keep a fire, by being near it. Fourteen minutes of it. The courier's margin: *I matched my breathing to strangers' and I was less lonely than I've been on any route. Noted so I remember it was the radio and not the desert doing it. Noted again: that distinction may be the Choir's entire position.*

**02:00.** "Request hour," announced by Brother Decibel's night voice, which is his day voice remembering someone. Request hour is not on any posted schedule and occurs, per the congregation, whenever it is needed. First request, from a beacon tender out past Relay 6: *"The low one. The one that sounds like a door thinking."* What followed was five minutes of the frequency's static, unaltered. The tender keyed back a single icon: a lit lamp. The courier's margin: *request fulfilled.*

**02:40.** Cough.

**03:10.** A child's voice on mic, awed, permitted: *"Can she hear the lamps?"* An elder, patient as geology: *"She heard them before we lit them. Ask a smaller question."* The child, thinking: *"Does she dream in our key?"* The elder, after a silence that weighed something: *"Litany Nine. We'll practice it till she answers."*

**03:30.** Reading hour. Little Reverb, uncharacteristically slow, reading from a document the congregation calls *the mail*: a list of cargo manifests from the harbour bulletin, read like scripture, delivery addresses pronounced with full liturgical care. The courier's margin: *they read my route back to me, parcel by parcel, like it was a name being said correctly for the first time. I have never felt so accurately freighted.*

**04:00.** "And now the part of the night that is not ours," said Brother Decibel, and the static went on — uninterrupted, unhosted, held open like a door — for the remaining hours. The courier's last margin, hand unsteady in the good way: *at dawn the ring lamps came up one by one, and the static, I swear on the creed, made room.*

*The Choir publishes nothing about the broadcast. If asked, they say: it is a rest. The lamps say otherwise, is the courier's note, and the courier has been paid by the Choir since, monthly, in exact change, for transcription services she was never asked to render.*
