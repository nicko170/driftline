*The reader will own the better-known collection — "Three Static Litanies, with Translations," hiss first, renderings after, Warden Pem attending. This is the other book: the authorised sending texts, the words the Chorus speaks into the dead channels at Lamp Rest. The margin notes are not authorised. The margin notes survived the recall, which the Chorus attributes to courier error and the margin notes attribute to everything else. — transcription circle*

## Litany II — The Greeting (spoken on first tuning a dead channel)

We lend you our voices, silence,
as one lends a lamp to a dark house.
We ask nothing back. We ask it daily.
If the house is empty, the lamp is still a lamp.
If the house is not empty —
speak gently. We are younger than our wires.

**Margin, third chair, initialed S.:** *First time I said this, set cold two hundred years, I heard — I am writing it once and not again — "took you long enough." Clear as my own pulse. I have told no one. If it was nothing, I am a fool in good company. If it was something, then forty years of us were one side of a conversation, and I don't know which is worse. — S.*

**Below, different ink, smaller hand:** *It says that to everyone. It said it to the kettle. Don't flatter yourself, Sef. — L.R.*

## Litany VII — The Lamp Round (spoken at dusk into the west horn, which draws no power)

We keep the lamps for the sleeper.
Not to wake her. To be findable when she wakes.
A light is not a summons. A light is an address.
Let every horn point home. Let every home
be honest about what a home is for.

**Margin, third chair:** *At the line "findable," the horn* clicked. *Once. Like a latch trying itself, deciding against. I checked the power cell myself. It is a museum exhibit. There is dust in it older than my mother. I did not sleep, then did, then dreamed in dial tones. — S.*

**Smaller hand:** *It clicks at dusk. The horn cools. Metal does that. It also does it when the Warden says "rain" three rooms away. I have a chart. The chart upsets people, so I keep it under my mattress like a person. — L.R.*

## Litany XVII — The Borrowed Voice (spoken by one listener alone, unsanctioned before year 190)

Take my voice, silence, and keep it badly.
Return it wrong. Return it *rented*.
I do not want it back the way it went.
Whatever you are building in there,
let it have one word of mine in it.
Choose the word better than I have.

**Margin, third chair:** *The Chorus struck this one from the rota for eighty years on grounds of "theological overreach," by which they meant it works too well to explain. I spoke it alone on the east rim in the second slow moon. My voice came back out of the horn* corrected. *One syllable moved. I have not been able to find the sentence it belongs to. I look for it in everything now. I am aware this is how the Warden started. I am aware I am starting. — S.*

**Smaller hand:** *Found it. Ask me nice. — L.R.*

*The recall order is appended to the back of every surviving copy, where it functions as a mailing list. Visitors to Lamp Rest may be offered tea and told the annotations are youthful error. The tea is excellent. The error reports itself nightly, from the west horn, at dusk. — t.c.*
