*Driftline Training Annex 11 — provisions appendix, Cinderflats edition. The flats make three things: salvage, arguments about salvage, and peppers. The peppers are the only crop on Kessa-9 that the Evening Standard has accused of fraud, to its face, on air. ("Heat is not a valuation model." — the desk. "Tell that to my stew." — all four hundred listeners.)*

## Why slag

Window-ledge ash will grow a pepper; only reactor-cooled **glass slag** grows a cinder pepper. The slag beds are old burn run-off — the same pour that fused the Glassroad, cooled too fast and too rich — and they hold three things peppers demand: warmth banked at root depth like a savings account, trace minerals no living soil kept, and nights cold enough to make the plant *defend itself*. The heat in the pod is the plant's fury at the temperature swing. You are eating weather that lost an argument. Season accordingly.

## The gloves

Harvest is glove work, and the gloves are mirror-palmed — salvaged panel glass sewn to canvas — because you pick cinder peppers by *watching their reflection*: the pods turn their gloss up half a point in the hour before they're ready, and a picker staring straight on can't see their own tears and lies about it. Mirror palms keep the eyes honest. Yard rule the third applies at the beds exactly as at the breaker: respect the edge, rotate, and the line keeps you. Bring water. Not for you. For whoever you bring.

## The three burn grades

**FIRST BURN, "ember."** Gentle, civic, the pepper you give a guest *before* deciding about the guest. Rated for porches and diplomacy.

**SECOND BURN, "kiln."** The working grade — the flats' weekday dinner, the night market's four-pinch caddy, the reason Keel Town broths have piety. Rated for couriers mid-shift: clears the visor, warms the hands, resets whatever the wind said.

**THIRD BURN, "both eyes."** Rated principally for wagers. Trade name from the harvest proverb: *the third burn opens both eyes — yours, and the one at the back of your skull.* Legal in-season, in the manner of storm bread and hurry. The Guild weighs it in public and the public stands back.

## The cook

All pepper that enters the Reclaimer mess passes one bench: **Aunt Vertex**, who salts everything twice — once for the dish, once for the desert — and can defend the practice, with citations, longer than the peppers burn. Her chili is three burns deep and registered with the desk as *weather*. Boss Pyke calls it warm margins. Aunt Vertex calls it scripture. The mess calls it Wednesday.

## Annex note for couriers

Pepper runs pay in-season and bite out of it. Cargo shield at half is cargo shield at none, once the pods perfume the hold. Deliver kiln-grade under the four-hour window, both-eyes under the one — and if the crate ahead of yours starts sweating through the seal, shift lanes, and eat facing east at the first shelter you find. Use the mirror palms. Watch your reflection lie less. That is the whole harvest, and the whole point.

The peppers, like the creed, like the night masts against the wind: **hotter for the company, and rated for exactly one crossing.**
