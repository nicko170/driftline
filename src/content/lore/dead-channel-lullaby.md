*Listening-notes, Static-Warden Pem, Hollow of the First Note. Copied by request to the Driftline creed appendix, because couriers keep asking what the humming is, out past the second dune line, when their radios drift off-band.*

## Channel 0.4 — "the cradle band"

The band has no licence because there was no one left to license it. It sits low on the dial, below the freight talk, below the moorage weather, in the slice of spectrum the old terraforming crews used for — the paperwork says *personnel morale*. It plays one piece of music, on loop, all day, all night, and has done so for a hundred and ninety years, give or take a storm.

Duration: three minutes, forty-one seconds. Instruments: something plucked, something sung, something underneath both that might be a reactor idling in sympathetic key. Words: **none survive.** The melody has the shape of words — verses, a lift where a chorus would go — and everyone who has ever hummed along (everyone) has felt the absent syllables under their tongue like teeth a child has lost.

The words died with the crews, in a dozen languages the colonists brought and the desert kept. What remains is the tune, which needs no translation, which is the problem and the gift of it.

## Transcript, a full loop, annotated

**0:00** — plucked figure, descending, like someone checking the tuning and deciding it doesn't matter.
**1:02** — the voice enters. Alto. Unhurried. The hiss underneath her is not static; it is on the original tape; it might be weather on another continent, on a world that hadn't finished yet.
**2:15** — the lift. Every child in Saltmouth hums this part in the same key without being taught. Guild researchers looked into this once. The report was one line: *stop looking into this.*
**3:41** — she holds the last note a half-breath past comfortable, and then three seconds of open band, and again.

## Custody

The transmitter sits in a canyon culvert nobody maintains. Attempts to locate it fail cheerfully — compasses swing, bearings double back. The Choir's position: the lullaby is MOTHER singing in her sleep, and the culvert is where her cradle-rocking arm reaches up out of the ground. The Reclaimers' position: it's a solar trickle-charger and a tape loop and *leave it alone*. Both positions are held sincerely and neither faction goes near the culvert, which is how you know both believe a little of the other.

The Guild once tried to bill someone for the frequency. The invoice is still open. Under *payee*, the clerk eventually wrote, in the margin where payees do not extend: *the kid, the one being sung to. Which one? All of them.*

## Keeper's rule

Night riders know the trick: drift your dial low past the freight talk when the dark gets long, and there she is. It doesn't keep you awake. It keeps you *company*, which is different, and on the flats after midnight, better.

Pem's note, last page, in pencil: *If it ever stops, come tell me first. Do not fix it alone. Do not be alone at all. Bring everyone. That's what it's for.*
