## Ode to the Condenser at Dock Four

*You drank the morning, drop by drop,*
*and gave it back as water —*
*a trick the sky gave up on*
*a hundred years too early.*
*We took your coils. We took your drum.*
*We left the drip tray standing.*
*Some habits are a kind of gravestone.*
*Keep drinking. It's allowed.*

---

## Elegy for the Long Conveyor

You ran gravel uphill for forty years
and nobody asked why, not even the gravel.
Belt gone to sling-stock. Rollers gone to wind-chimes —
yours now ring at dock two, tuneless, unimproved.
The Yards disagree about whether you retired.
The author has written *relieved* on the chassis
and will fight any machine that calls it otherwise.

---

## Bolt (haiku)

*One bolt, cross-threaded,*
*held the whole crane anyway.*
*I loved it. Don't tell.*

*Author's margin note: Solder says a cross-threaded bolt is a failure of meetings, not love. Solder has never met this bolt.*

---

## To the Winch That Will Not Stop Humming (dock nine)

If you must sing, sing in key:
the night crews set their watches by you
and you are flat, old friend, you are *flat*,
and we will strip you fondly in the spring
unless you learn the blue notes first.
(The blue notes. You know the ones.)

---

## Gate-note, pinned below the odes, author's hand

The Yards get asked why the hauler writes to dead machines. Answer, in order: the machines are not dead, they are relieved; the hauler is not writing, she is taking dictation; and your manifest is late, driver, and unlike a poem your lateness does not scan.

*Added later, different chalk, larger hand: AMEN — A.V.*
