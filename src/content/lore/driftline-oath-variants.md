*Collected by the Last Lamp society from yard walls, shed doors, ballot kiosks and one sail. Every yard insists its version is first. The society declines to rule; the society enjoys its suppers too much. Variants transcribed as found, punctuation under protest.*

## The nine, as spoken where they live

**SALTMOUTH (garage wall):** *Any crate. Any storm. Any door.* The reference text. Painted largest; argued loudest. See the wall transcription for the forty amendments.

**SKYDOCKS (ballast kiosk):** *Any crate, any storm, any door, any tide.* The addendum of a hanging town. Keel crews note that a tide is a door that arrives by itself, usually early, always enquiring about insurance.

**CINDERFLATS (crusher shed, chalked):** *Take it. Carry it. Get out of the slag.* Mostly verbs. The Reclaimer union maintains this is the creed with the poetry stripped for salvage, and considers the result an improvement, and has offered to strip the other eight for free.

**CHOIR HOLLOW (not written, hummed):** Seven words fitted to one long note; the note is the word, the words are cargo. On Lamp Rest nights the congregation sings it so slowly that couriers passing on the pan below hear it as weather. It is, the singers insist, the only version that arrives on time.

**DROWNED ARRAY (service log):** Rendered by a carekeeper mast in ticket language: CONSIGNMENT: ANY. CONDITIONS: ANY. DESTINATION: ANY. STATUS: OUTSTANDING. The ticket has been open two hundred years and is reassigned nightly to whoever is riding. *(See the mast's own index for what stamps it.)*

**WINDSPINE (gauge hut):** *Any crate. Any storm. Any do—* unfinished, by tradition. The ridge takers say the last word is the wind's to finish, and the wind finishes it *shut*, loudly, at four in the morning, as is its right.

**GLASSROAD (gate warden's manuscript, margin):** *Any crate; any storm; any door — subject to revision.* Main clause generous, margin clause honest, per house style.

**MOTHERSGATE (hermitage wall):** *Any crate, any storm—* stop there. The prior's note: some doors you do not pre-declare *any* to. You knock. See how it goes. Her lamps agree in order, left to right.

**THE OLD PAN (oldest known):** no words. A line of boot prints pressed into ancient salt crust, walking, eight strides, each stride a shade apart as if slowing to choose a door. The wind has filled and emptied them ten thousand times and has never once been permitted to claim them. Couriers touch the first print for luck and the last for direction. The middle six are the creed.

## Ruling

Unresolved, by tradition, on purpose. The creed's one formal property, per every yard consulted: *it doesn't hold you. You hold it.*

*Flat salt, full pan, following wind.*
