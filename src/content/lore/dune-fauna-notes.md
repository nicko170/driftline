*Driftline Training Annex 4, natural history division (staff: one courier, off season). The annex reminds riders that the desert was terraformed for machines and grasses; everything below the knee is an unscheduled improvement by the desert itself. Grading scale: HARMLESS / DELICIOUS / BOTH.*

## Sand-skipper *(Saltarex minor)*

**Field marks:** a fist-sized silver-grey hopper, all shin and ear, travelling the dune faces at dusk in skips of two to four metres. You will see them in your headlight spill as a scatter of punctuation.
**Habits:** drinks fog off its own ears; waits out storms buried to the nostrils, which double as tiny shovels and, per the Last Lamp kitchen, tiny handles.
**Grade: BOTH.** Harmless beyond description and, regrettably, the backbone of flats cuisine — see the cook's ledger under *skipper, salt-baked, serves whoever stopped*. Annex rule: you may cook what you can catch, and you cannot catch any of them. The rule is decorative and widely framed.

## Glass flea *(Splendidulus vitreus)*

**Field marks:** a teal-bright speck with the jump-to-body ratio of a folk hero, found only on fused glass — Glassroad shoulders, canyon floors, reactor-burn spill.
**Habits:** grazes the algal film that pearls the glass after cold nights; leaps when your shadow covers it, which means a riding line across old burn is written in rising glitter. Choir beekeepers of the Hollow insist the fleas keep time with the listening static. The fleas decline to comment.
**Grade: HARMLESS.** Too small to eat, too pretty to want to. Riding through a startled cloud of them at dusk is officially listed, in one courier's marginalia, as *the whole reason*.

## Lamp-moth, or dusk-moth *(Lucivex noctis)*

**Field marks:** bone-white wings, amber-veined, size of a ration coupon.
**Habits:** hatches on still evenings and seeks the brightest warm thing on the horizon — a beacon, a porch lamp, your headlight. A swarm in the beam reads as snow falling upward. They mean no harm and accomplish none; wipe the lens at the next shelter and consider it rent.
**Grade: HARMLESS.** Eating one is possible and has been done, once, on a dare, by a rider whose name the annex has charitably lost.

## Storm-mole *(Fossor tempestatis, alleged)*

**Field marks:** unknown. The species is documented entirely by its earthworks — a fresh mound, profile of a sleeping dog, appearing overnight on open pan. No photograph, no skull, no pelt. The Guild weather desk denounces it quarterly, in writing.
**Habits:** surfaces only in the seventy-two hours before a wall. Old riders check for mounds the way they check the gauge; the goat at the weather desk is said to stamp twice at a mound report and once at an instrument, and the goat's record speaks for itself.
**Grade: BOTH (theoretical).** Harmless — no mole has ever harmed anything but an argument. Delicious — presumed, by the perpetually hungry, on grounds that anything that clever owes the desert a flavour. *The annex formally discourages prospecting for one.*

## Parting note, unsigned

Grade every creature you meet, out loud, as you pass. It keeps the eyes up and the throttle honest, and on a long solo run it is pleasant to be one of several small lives out doing errands. — *K.*
