*Salt Guild Office of Consequence, Form AA-19, on the loss of the settlement of Bitter Flats, north-west pan, in the Wall of the Long Night. Filed three seasons after the fact — the standard interval, since Form AA-19 requires the filing clerk to have stopped dreaming about the subject. The clerk has annotated that they have not stopped dreaming about the subject, and that the form will adjust.*

## Situation

Bitter Flats: 214 souls, brine-works, one relay mast, one chapel (unaffiliated, excellent soup). Storm system classified Wall, third magnitude, arriving at night against a forecast of *clear, signed, the goat unwell*. Wind face wind speed measured at the ridge gauge, briefly, before the ridge gauge became a citizen of the storm.

## What Failed

The western pan beacon failed first and is *exonerated* — it signalled until taken. The north escape causeway failed second; it was four hundred metres short of the mesa notch, a saving of cost made by persons whose names the Office preserves in wax, ochre grade, in a drawer. The relay mast stood longest. The operator stayed at the key and her traffic — positions, headcounts, one recipe — is the only honest timetable of the night.

## What Held

At 22:40 the quarry hoist house radioed for any wheel, any skiff, anything with traction and a warm heart. What answered was a Driftline courier wagon rated for cargo and listed, in the levy book, as *fifteen persons maximum, or nine with cargo*.

**Sable Urrutta**, courier second grade, age nineteen, made seven crossings through the wall's skirt between 22:52 and 04:10. Her wagon carried forty-two people out of Bitter Flats. The final run took the brine-works' night crew, the chapel's entire bench population, two goats, and Sable's own hover-bike, which she threw out the side to make the weight work.

She put the wagon on blocks at the mesa notch, checked the headcount five times — her count and the Guild's count disagree by one, and the Office, having reviewed the relay traffic, sides with *hers* — and lay down beside the wagon and slept for nineteen hours.

## Rulings

Bitter Flats is struck from the manifest. Salvage rights *waived in perpetuity* — Form AA-19 does not normally contain the word *sacred*, but the clerk notes the form is adjusting, see above. The causeway shortfall is charged where charged. The north causeway now ends at the mesa notch. It is named **Urrutta's Forty-Second** on every chart since, and riders sound one short horn passing it, after dark, so the ground knows someone is counting.

Sable Urrutta declined promotion, pension, and a statue, in writing, accepting only a replacement bike and the right to name it. The levy book records the name: **FIFTEEN MAXIMUM**.

The Office will take no queries.
