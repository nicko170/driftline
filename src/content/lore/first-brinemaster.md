## The founding, as approved

In the first season after the money left, water was true and scales were not. Every trader on the flats owned a scale that weighed in their favour, and every caravan owned a story that weighed more. Salt was traded for water, water for flour, flour for promises, and the promises were settling into an inheritance the way sand settles into everything.

Then came a woman — the approved history does not hold her name; the approved history holds that she gave it up with the office, and her name is the first thing ever weighed and found correct — carrying a scale of old terraforming stock, mass-calibrated at the factory for a world that did not finish becoming. It weighed true. It weighed true in dust, in heat, in grief, in the presence of money. This made it, by the standards of the season, a monster.

She set it in the square and weighed, for a fee of one pinch per pan. Traders came to laugh and stayed to be weighed themselves, because — the approved history is frank on this point — a true scale is the finest entertainment a dishonest season affords. Within a month, deals were struck in its sight. Within a year, deals were only *real* in its sight. Within a generation the scale had a house, and the house had clerks, and the clerks had a doctrine, and the doctrine had stationery.

## The first lie

The approved history keeps one story above all. In the third year a factor appeared before the Brinemaster with flawless books, and the Brinemaster — who was by then old, and tired, and well-loved, and therefore dangerous — said: *the books are beautiful; weigh the factor.* And the second scale, the apprentice scale, the ugly one in the back that nobody prayed to, was brought out, and the factor's declared salt was weighed against the season's receipts, and the difference was a lie of four hundredweight, and the Guild learned its founding lesson: **no one catches the lie. The scale does. Your whole duty to it is to be standing nearby when it does.**

That is the entire theology. Everything else — the forms, the audits, the seals, the Counterweight's office, the debt doctrine — is standing nearby, elaborated.

## The auditor's footnotes (excerpted, accruing)

1. *The name was not "given up with the office." It is in the year-one ledger, crossed out in the Brinemaster's own hand, which is a different and more interesting kind of religious act.* — S.C.
2. *The "monster" scale is in the vault. It weighs true still. The Guild has tested it against itself annually for two centuries, which the auditor notes is a ritual, not a measurement.* — S.C.
3. *Four hundredweight. The approved history never says what the factor bought with the lie. The year-three grain ledger says: a well. The auditor declines to resolve this, noting only that the well still runs, and the factor's line still draws from it, free, per the ancient settlement nobody will explain.* — S.C.

*Sealed, copied, and witnessed in the ninth holding of the office, year two hundred and three of the Delay.*
