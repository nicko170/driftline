*Copied off Dusk-Marron Kell's left forearm, with permission and one small argument about spelling. Kell says paper gets wet and skin gets older and he'll take his chances with the second. The tattoo is rearranged whenever a channel changes hands; there's a bare patch on the wrist he calls* pending. *— transcription circle*

## The talking bands, south arm (the polite half)

**GUILD DISPATCH — the brass channel.** Manifest readings, tariff changes, mooring slots, and Sister Counterweight's audit summaries read in a tone that implies the weather has been filed incorrectly. Answer it on the second hail. Answering on the first is presumptuous. Never not answer it.

**THE RELAY BAND — courier chatter, ours.** Where the road actually lives. Position calls, wall warnings, who's broken down, whose engine is doing the *new sound*. Ketch hold-court happens here after dusk. Rules: keep it short, keep it warm, and if you sing, commit.

**THE STANDARD — dusk broadcast.** Vela Marlowe, off the Saltmouth water-tower antenna. Listen with one ear. She'll tell you the storm is coming an hour after Pemmy's stamped it and twenty minutes before you'll admit it.

## The listening bands, north arm (the other half)

**CHOIR HOURS.** Not chatter — *listeners*, logging the static off Windspine and the deep desert on shifts. They key open only at bell-change. What they broadcast is one clear tone. Nobody has established what answer it is waiting for, and Brother Decibel answers all of them.

**RECLAIMER WORK CHANNEL — the cutting band.** Hull counts, torch warnings, Boss Pyke telling someone the thing they're standing on is load-bearing *now*. Open to anyone in gassing range; Pyke says an informed thief wastes less of his schedule.

**THE DEAD PAIR.** Two channels with nobody on them for sixty years that still carry their old call-letters in the static if the night is dry enough. Compass says leave them be. Compass is right about one thing a year and spends it here.

## The wrist

Below the bone, in fresh ink, one frequency with the carrier open and *nobody on it*. Nobody admits to keying it. Twice a night, when the crate is within a klick, something breathes across it — not speech, the *shape* of speech, three parts, patient.

Kell's note under the ink, in his own hand, too small to be a tattoo so he carved it in the armrest: *The open band is not empty. It is a chapel with the door ajar. Knock twice. Then ride on.*
