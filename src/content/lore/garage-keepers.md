*Copied for the Lamp Rest archive with the keeper's permission, which the keeper gave by saying "if they can read, they can read it." The names above the garage door are painted one over another — nine strata of weathered lettering — and can be read in full with patience, a low sun, and one fingernail. The lines from the inner wall are transcribed verbatim.*

---

## The door, oldest to newest

**SABBATH REN, year 1 of the Delay.** Built the garage out of the drop-lander that brought her, which is why the roof curves like a stomach. Her wall line: *"Fix the ride. The rider is already fixing you."*

**ODETTA MARSH.** Hung the second door so the sand would have to queue. Famous for test-riding every repair herself, badly, on purpose: "if it survives me it survives anyone." Her line: *"Loud parts are telling the truth early."*

**PEL VO ("Two-Stroke").** Kept the garage through the lean decade when half of Saltmouth walked to the cinder fields and only the stubborn walked back. Repaired bikes for salt, for soup, once for a song (rated the song overpaid). His line: *"Charge what keeps the door open. Open is the whole business model."*

**GRANDMOTHER FUSE.** First electrical keeper; came down off Windspine Ridge with a coil of turbine wire and opinions about both. The wall notes her handwriting is the only one in ochre, because she painted over Pel Vo's line and he painted over hers and the two layers cured into the colour the Guild later adopted, which neither of them ever admitted. Her line: *"A spare part in the hand is worth three in somebody else's courier."*

**DORM HALLOEK.** The quiet one. Thirty-one years. His line is the shortest on the wall and the one new couriers photograph: *"Slow down."*

**MARI SORN.** Storm-born, storm-kept. Rebuilt the garage twice after walls of sand came through Saltmouth like invoices legally arrived. Her line: *"Shelter is a delivery you make to yourself. Signature required."*

**DOLE KESTREL.** Taught. Every courier of his era learned hover-height in his yard with a rope on the bike and Dole holding the other end, running. The rope is still on the wall, retired, knotted with one knot per graduate. There are knots nobody claims and the wall does not explain them. His line: *"The ground is not your enemy. The ground is your first repeat customer."*

**KETCH.** Current. Thirty years on the Driftline before his knees filed for retirement. His line, chalk, because he says paint is a commitment and chalk is a conversation: *"Flat salt and a following wind. Everything else is spare parts."* The chalk has never once blown away or washed off, which the wall also does not explain.

## Archivist's addendum

The nine names read together form a sentence in no language anyone will own up to. Little Reverb hummed at the door for a minute last season and reported it "hums back friendly, off by half a name." The archive files this under *unverified, hopeful*, alongside the rest of the garage.
