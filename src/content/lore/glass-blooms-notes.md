## What a bloom actually is

A *glass bloom* is a growth of fused, slow-running silica rising out of old conduit lines: teal at the core, amber at the petal edges, one to nine metres at first emergence and *taller the following week, like they heard you measuring.* Structurally they are petalwise — five to eleven broad plates furling out of a bright stem — but the petals are not decoration; they are *facing*. Every one of the nineteen first sites tilts the same way before noon and leans, by a hand's width, toward the gate past Windspine. Nothing on Kessa-9 leans toward anything. Note that. Underline it. I underlined it.

**Sketch, in words:** imagine an agave made of bottle glass, lit from inside by banked coals, singing very softly at a pitch your teeth feel before your ears do. Then imagine it *closing* for the night like a hand going to sleep, and glowing brighter for having done it. That close. That exact.

## The hum, and the hum with visitors

Alone on the shelf, a bloom keeps a single note — somewhere below a relay horn, above a headache. But bring a second bloom into sightline and the two find a chord, and the first time you ride the notch between three of them you will understand why the Choir files this under *gardening* and the Reclaimers filed for salvage exactly once and then very quietly withdrew the paperwork. The chord does something to a hover-bike: engine pitch slides toward it, instruments unclench, and my fuel gauge read a quarter-tank *better* than honest for six minutes. I wrote the machine's name in the margin of my permission slip, felt stupid, kept it.

**Storm behaviour.** Ahead of a wall, every bloom opens flat and hums *louder*, petals steaming at the tips. Do not shelter against one; the heat bloom off the stem is honest-to-the-bones kiln weather. Shelter *downwind*, behind, and count yourself lent, not saved.

## Riding rules, hard-won

1. **Petals point the way.** Use them as landmarks but fix your line on the ridge. Waypoints near the rows drift twenty, thirty metres *toward* the flowers between visits — the desert is tidying its own footpaths and your nav is a guest in the house now.
2. **Do not cut, chip, or sample.** The stump re-grows *better*, on purpose, at speed. That is not a plant behaviour. That is a *response*.
3. **Stop once.** Kill the engine at dusk. When the sun goes, the whole shelf hums in chord and the chord is *about* you the same way weather is about you. Then ride on, and tell the Bureau it was wind.
