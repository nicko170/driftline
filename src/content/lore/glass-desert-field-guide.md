*Driftline Training Annex 4 — issued to all couriers. Coffee stains original.*

**Salt flats.** The pans around Saltmouth look empty. They are not empty; they are *flat*, which is different. Flat means full throttle, no surprises, and the finest riding this side of the equator. Respect two things out there: the shimmer means heat-warp, not water, and the salt genuinely does keep tire-ghosts for weeks, so if an old track crosses yours at a right angle and stops — that was a courier who found the hard crust edge. Learn from it.

**Dune fields.** Crests are sharp on the lee side and lazy on the windward, always, it's the one promise the desert keeps. Climb the lazy side, hop the crest, land on the fall line. If you hop a crest blind you deserve whatever you meet on the far side — usually a rock, occasionally a Reclaimer, one time a wedding.

**The glassroad.** Reactor burns fused the canyon floor into teal glass a century before your grandmother's grandmother. It is the fastest road on Kessa-9 and the least forgiving: grip drops by half, and the walls were poured upward like fused candle wax. Brake before the bends, drift the long curves, and never — *never* — touch the bright veins. They hold heat longer than seems legally permissible.

**Boundary mountains.** The rangy violet walls at the map's edge. Not terrain you cross; terrain that files *you* under 'weather'. Turn around.

**Storms.** A wall of sand on the horizon means twelve minutes. A wall of sand that fills the sky means four. A wall of sand that makes your radio sing hymns means the Choir will want to interview you afterward, and the storm season chapter of this guide is not a metaphor.

Carry water. Wax your seals. Count your cargo twice. — *K.*
