*Notes of courier Hedd Kline, Driftline, requested by Ketch after Kline's third fairing, written during convalescence ("convalescence" is Ketch's word; Hedd's word is "bench time"), annotated by Ketch in the workshop hand. Read before your first canyon run, after your second, and in the ambulance both times.*

## Why glass is fast

Fused ground is the flattest surface on Kessa-9 that isn't a Guild plate. Skirt wash moves across it with almost nothing to bite, so a hover bike rides its own cushion with no drag debt — the old manuals say you gain a tenth of cruise speed on good glass for free. **Free is the trap.** (Ketch: *"Underlined in the original. Underlined again by me."*)

Glass is fast because it was melted flat by something that did not care about you, and it has kept the attitude.

## Heat, shimmer, lift

Glass at noon breathes. Stand off a hot pane and watch the air stack over it in layers — that's lift you didn't ask for. Your hover height lies to you on hot glass; the spring in the skirt rides the shimmer the way it rides a dune it can't see, and you'll find yourself a half-metre high exactly when the canyon asks you to corner. Brake *before* the bright patches. The bright ones are hot. (Ketch: *"The bright ones are hot. Hedd has written the word BRIGHT seven times. Hedd is healing well."*)

## Grip, or the negotiation

You do not grip glass. You *negotiate* with it, and the terms are set before you arrive. Across the cooling ripples the terms are bad. Along them they're decent. On the teal vein seams there are no terms, only heat, and I refer the reader to my third fairing. Brake later where the grain runs with you (THE CARVING IS CORRECT); brake never where the surface is wet with old dust, because dust on glass is ball bearings with a sky.

## The smugness problem

Here is the physics nobody publishes. A clean run across canyon glass produces — measurably, Ketch timed me — a 12% increase in throttle application on the following straight. The riders call this *the glass grin.* It is the leading cause of second runs ending on backs. Glass pays out small, early, and often, like a card table that owns the chair you're sitting in.

Counter-measure, proven on my instrument panel and my ribs: say the name of the lesson aloud when you feel the grin coming. Mine is *"fairing three."* Yours will write itself. It always does.
