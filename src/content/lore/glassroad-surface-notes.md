*From the Driftline bench book, Saltmouth garage, pages 41–44. Entries in several hands; oldest entries attributed to R. Davenant, newest unsigned but obviously Ketch.*

**The glass is not ice.** New riders brake for it like ice and skid like fools. Fused silica grips better than wet salt and worse than dry dune, and it grips *differently corner to corner*, because the old reactor burn rippled as it cooled. Learn the ripple direction. Riding with the ripple is a firm handshake; across it is a slap.

**Morning glass lies.** Dew sits in the ripple troughs till second bell and the surface looks dry from six metres up. It is not dry. Davenant's note: 'If the canyon shines like a promise, it's lying like one too.'

**The Slick Mile is honest.** No troughs, no dust, no excuses — a kilometre of glass smooth as a verdict. Fastest surface on Kessa-9. Also the only surface where you can hear yourself think, and the thinking is mostly *don't brake don't brake don't brake*.

**Boost off glass, not onto it.** The vanes bite air, not ground; glass doesn't care how hard you push, but the rim rocks at the runout *very much do*.

**The mid-span shrine shadow.** Canyon bends so the shrine's shadow lies across the run at dusk. Cold shadow, warm glass: the temperature seam will grab your skirt like a customs officer. Hop it, or square up and ride through straight. Never turn inside it.

**Night glass sings for the Choir.** After dark the surface cools and ticks like a settling roof, and the Choir says it's scripture and the Reclaimers say it's physics and both of them will fight you. Sound rises a whole tone before a storm. That's not scripture, that's a *warning*, free of charge.

**Final entry, unsigned, recent:** The glass is blooming near the chapel. Nobody taught it that. Ride gentle past the flowers; we don't know what they're for yet, and on Kessa-9 that always means *somebody does*.
