*Collected in the canyon over three seasons by courier Hedd Kline (between fairings), checked against the charter wall at mid-span chapel, and disputed on every note. Toll songs are ride instruments, not entertainment. Do not learn them from a page. Learn them at speed, the correct way, or do not ride the glass at all.*

## The tuning

The glassroad has no mileposts — the old reactor burn ate the road's furniture along with the road. What it has is *echo*. The canyon walls answer a sung line in just under a breath, and the answer comes back different from each stretch: flat off the slick mile, doubled in the fuse-narrows, swallowed entirely at the weeping seam where the teal veins run wet. A courier sings to hear the road answer, and the verses carry the braking points in their phrasing, so that a correct song *is* a correct run. You are not keeping time. The song is keeping you.

## Verse one: the gate toll

*Salt behind me, glass below, count the pillars as they go — three up, three down, hold your line, the fifth one leans, that's the sign.*

The leaning fifth pillar south of the gate-north stands off the road's shoulder by a hand's width less than it did last century, and everyone agrees it will outlive us anyway. You sing the pillar count to fix your line for the first true corner. Miss the sign and you meet the wall where the wall thinks it lives. Jett Marrows swears he sings this verse in his sleep and has the pillow scars to show for it.

## Verse two: the slick mile

*Hush now. Hush now. Hands like rain on a wedding pan — the mile can hear a heavy man.*

The slick mile is sung at a whisper because it is sung at speed, and at speed the throttle hand gets proud (see Kline's *Notes on Riding Glass*, addendum, the grin). The verse is short, so that the part of you that wants to add a flourish has nowhere to stand. *Hush now* is the entire braking instruction for the fastest stretch of road on the moon, and it has never once been wrong.

## Verse three: the weeping seam

*Teal runs wet and teal runs wide, cross her grain or cross her tide — never brake upon her bright, never argue with the light.*

The seam where the teal glass veins sweat old heat. *Her bright* is the shimmery lift-patch; *argue with the light* is slang older than the Delay for grabbing brake on hot glass and being negotiated into the scenery. Some crews sing *never court the light* instead. Both versions are kept, because the seam keeps both kinds of riders' fairings.

## The folklorist's minutes (excerpt)

Guild Sub-Factor Ines Bellamy rode the canyon in year 191 of the Delay to assess whether toll songs constituted *performances* under the entertainment tariff. Her minutes conclude: the songs cannot be taxed, because the singers are paying a toll already — in attention, in teeth, occasionally — and because any courier ordered to pay coin for a braking song will simply *not sing*, at which point the Guild would be taxing silence, which the canyon gives away free and keeps the change. The assessment is filed as closed. The hymn bill for the assessment ride is filed under medical.

## Last verse (unsung)

There is a last verse couriers do not sing, only mouth, at the spot above mid-span where the chapel's poor box hangs: the names verse. Every name in it was a courier the glass kept. The verse has no metre and no end, because the box is never full and neither is the canyon. You add to it or you don't. The road doesn't collect. The road just holds the account open.
