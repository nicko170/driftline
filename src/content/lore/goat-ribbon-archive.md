*Kept at the Saltmouth weather desk, beside the rail. The rail is old ship-wood, salvaged, and carries the forecasts of the desk's senior meteorologist in the customary medium: ribbon. One ribbon per verified prediction, tied by the factor on duty, entered here by hand. Instrument-wall score for the same period: twelve correct, of ninety-one attempts. The instrument wall costs more. The desk has notes on this.*

## The rail, catalogued

**Ribbon 1 (ochre, sun-faded to ghost).** First forecast: "The Big Stillness," three days of weapon-grade calm. Every mast on the ridge disagreed. The goat licked the air and held the bleat for nine seconds, a record. Stillness arrived on schedule. The ribbon was tied by the First Factor, who had bet against it and paid in oats.

**Ribbon 18 (rust, double knot).** Called a wall of sand nine days early, against the almanac, against the turbines, against Ogo the Second's explicit instruction. Eleven riders were home when it hit. The desk does not frame this ribbon because framing is pride, but visitors keep asking, so a small card has been provided.

**Ribbon 40 (teal, chewed).** Forecast: "wet from above, minor." Result: a condenser tower collapsed into its own tank; rain, technically, extremely local. Counted as correct under protest. The chewing is the author's own margin note.

**Ribbon 63 (bone-white twine, not ribbon).** The desk's only draw: forecast said "the horizon will lie today," instrumentation said clear, both were right — a superior mirage stacked the skydocks upside-down over the flats until noon. Twine is used for draws. The goat received half a ribbon's oats and accepted the bookkeeping without rancour.

**Ribbon 88 (amber, fresh).** Season-open call: first wall, named the day and the hour and — unsettlingly — the shape of it. The Guild weather desk (human division) has formally requested the method. The method is proprietary. The method is a goat.

**Ribbon 91 (ochre, unfinished knot).** Tied mid-incident: "north wind, Thursday," bleated with total conviction in the direction of the new barometer, which said calm. The desk then resigned at speed and had to be walked home past the exchange by a courier. The knot remains unfinished until Thursday, out of professional courtesy. Thursday turned out as advertised; the barometer has been re-evaluated.

## The drawer

Beneath the chart table there is a drawer, and in the drawer there is one white ribbon, untied, and it has been untied for eleven years. It is reserved for the forecast the desk refuses to give: rain. Real rain, sky rain, the kind in the old spotter's cards. Asked about it, the senior meteorologist stares at the horizon for a long moment and then eats something made of paper. The factors' official translation of this behaviour is: *the desk is thinking.* The unofficial one, whispered: *it isn't waiting to be asked. It's waiting to be right.*
