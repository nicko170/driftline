*Card 12 of the courier packet, ochre stock, kept behind the licence where the sweat can't reach it. Issued by the Office of Marks, Saltmouth Exchange. Footnote on file: "A courier who cannot read a seal will be read by one." Laminate if sentimental.*

## The ledger seal

One mark runs the Guild: the **ledger seal**, an open book struck through with a tally slash, ochre on bone. Genuine strikes show the slash crossing *left foot to right crown* — counterfeiters reverse it, every time, the way forgers always give the portrait a kinder mouth. Below the book, three dots: salt, water, weather. A seal missing the third dot is an inland copy and legal nowhere the wind owns anything.

On the obverse, hard to photocopy and harder to forge: a thumb-smear of Guild wax, ochre grade, which smells — consult Card 12, you are holding it — of *rain*. We are aware this is not verifiable. It is, however, binding.

## The flag grades

**Ochre square, square knot:** guild house; tariff applies; tea is compulsory. **Ochre pennant, one notch:** bonded cargo accepted. **Pennant, two notches:** bonded cargo accepted and the factor is armed. **Bone flag over ochre:** mourning; conduct business quietly and round up in their favour, the Office will square it later. **Amber streamer on any mast:** courier-class freight waiting; you may leave before the tea if you are very polite and very fast.

A flag flown upside-down is not a distress sign. It means the flag-keeper has died laughing at the tariff board, an event the Office recognises as *act of comedy* and declines to regulate.

## The stamp that means pay

The circular stamp, rust-red, reading CARRIED ON FAITH: your cargo was waived through ahead of payment. You will be found. The stamp is considered a courtesy; the finding is considered a certainty. Frame the first one. Everyone does. Tamsin Cho has three.

## The banner that outranked itself

In Year 174 the wayhouse at Gull Post flew a hand-sewn Guild banner — ochre field, ledger seal — stitched from memory by a keeper named Sadi Odh, who had seen the real thing twice. Twelve years later an audit found the stitching reversed the tally slash: a counterfeit, by the letter of the Marks Act. The Office prepared a fine. The keeper prepared tea and forty years of honest ledgers, every entry sealed under the wrong-way slash, every entry true.

Ruling of record: *a mark wardens what it marks; the wardening was done; the mark stands.* Gull Post's banner was certified senior over the surviving official issue, which had authenticates nothing but itself. Counterfeit flags may now, by covenant, outrank originals on proof of forty honest years. The Office notes this incentive is "manageable." The footnote adds, in a junior factor's hand, *it is the best thing we have ever accidentally done.*
