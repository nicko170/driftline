*Issued by the Office of Waxy Standards, Saltmouth Exchange. Reading requires no certification. *Sealing* requires Form 6-W. Breaking requires, at minimum, a witness and an alibi.*

## The Recipe

Guild wax is four parts salt-flat resin, one part rendered condenser tallow, one part ochre pigment, and one measure the Office declines to name in print. The unnamed measure is why Guild wax hardens in forty seconds, smells faintly of rain, and cannot be re-melted without smoking the liar's ledger-green. Junior factors are told the fourth measure is thunder. Senior factors are told to stop asking.

## Colour Grades (Memorise)

**Ochre** marks ordinary trade — grain, spares, honesty of the everyday sort. **Teal** marks bonded cargo: sealed at origin, sealed at heart, touched by no one between. **Amber** marks courier-class priority, which is to say *fast*, which is to say *the Guild will be sad if this is late*. **Violet** is restricted by covenant to the Archive, stickered twice, and spoken of in hallways only. **Black wax is not a grade.** Black wax is an enquiry. If you receive black wax, you are the cargo.

## The Counterfeit Teal Affair (Year 198, abridged)

A ringless binder in the Yard pressed seven hundred counterfeit teal seals from a cake of dyed tallow and sold them as bonded grade. The fraud was discovered not by assay but by weather: true Guild wax smells of rain, and Kessa-9 has had no rain to copy from in living memory, so the counterfeits smelled of *the binder's kitchen* — specifically lentils. Three marriages sealed in lentil-wax were annulled on a technicality of scent, which the Office maintains was correct and the Bench maintains was Tuesday.

## On Seal-Breaking

A seal may be broken by its addressee, by the Guild on warrant, or by nobody. Note that conjugal promises exchanged over a teal seal — sea-captain's weddings, dockhand elopements, two statistician proposals of famous dryness — inherit the seal's terms. Break such a seal *wrong* (cold knife, no witness, unkind thoughts) and the contract voids *entire*, marriage included. The Office has unmarried four couples this century and regrets that two of them deserved it.

## Closing Standard

Everything crosses. Everything's counted. The wax is how we pretend the counting lasts.
