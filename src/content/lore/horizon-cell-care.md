## What you own

A horizon cell is a piece of Verdantine Combine engineering from before the money stopped. It stores charge the way the desert stores grudges: efficiently, indefinitely, and with feelings about it. It will outlive you, your bike, and possibly the Guild. Treat it less like equipment and more like a very old dog: warm, particular, and capable of surprising violence only in stories you should take seriously.

## Charge cycles

Charge in shade. Charge in the cool of morning or the violet hour; the cell does not enjoy the noon flats and expresses this in your boost meter. A full cycle from flat takes four hours on a wind tap, six on a salt crank, and one memorable minute at Windspine if you have lost your mind and your insurance. **Never interrupt a cycle to 'see if it's angry.'** It wasn't. Now it has an opinion.

## Heat discipline

Warm is normal post-boost. Hot is a word for food. If your cell is too hot to lay a palm against, you have already missed three earlier warnings — humming sharpens, the field cage ticks, nearby goats leave — and should place the bike downwind and yourself elsewhere. This is not cowardice. The Guild's actuarial division (Sister Counterweight, entire) has certified it as arithmetic.

## The hum

Every horizon cell hums. Riders tune their day by it. A low hum is a content cell; a rising hum is a working one; a *harmonic* — a hum with a second voice in it — is reported to the garage, not the Choir, no matter what the horns do that night. Ketch's margin note on the master copy: *"It hums like a man thinking about lunch. This is fine. When it hums like a man who's decided something, come see me."*

## On 'slightly warm' cells

If a trader offers you a horizon cell described as **slightly warm**, **previously enjoyed**, **storm-found**, or **basically legal**, the Guild's position is: no. One paragraph cannot contain the incident reports of Brinn. The phrase "the grain." The phrase "mostly upwind." The phrase "we rebuilt the dock first because morale." Buy from the exchange, pay the tariff, and tell the trader the pamphlet made you do it. We sign these. He'll know.
