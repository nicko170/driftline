## So your cell hates you

It doesn't. It has never met you. You are a warm weight on the rack and occasionally a fist on the housing, and it files both under *weather*. The hum you take personally is a field coil doing arithmetic older than the road you're on. Read the Guild pamphlet (you have a copy; Ketch staples it to things) for what the hum *means*. Read this for what the hum *feels like*, which is: a very old god doing your accounts, badly, with love.

## Charging etiquette (the real rules)

One. Shade is a courtesy, not a kink. Two. Do not watch it charge. Watching is what hope does at a kettle and it works exactly as well. Three. If the neighbour's cell hums a half-tone up while yours is mid-cycle, that is *conversation*, and the polite thing is to pretend you heard nothing, the way you do at the bathhouse. Four. Never say the words "one more percent" out loud. The cell cannot hear you. Everyone else can, and they will tell the story at your wedding.

## Cold-weather sulking

Below frostline your cell will give you eighty percent and an attitude. This is not malfunction; this is *the molecule remembering that lithium was born in stars and has opinions about windchill*. Warm the housing with your hands before you ride. Yes, like a bird on an egg. Yes, the guild haulers will laugh. Their cells hate them too; they've just made peace with being hated by professionals. You are a courier. You are hated *amateurishly*, which is worse and therefore character-building.

## Why your boost dies at the worst moment

Because boost is borrowed against tomorrow and the cell is a better accountant than you. Because the moment was not, strictly, necessary. Because — and the zine stands by this against all engineering correspondence — *the cell can smell fear and considers it a rounding error*. Breathe. Ride the exit. The meter forgives faster than you do.

## Page seven

*Lithium, lightest of metals,*
*you were old when the sea was a rumour.*
*They dug you out and taught you to hold a grudge*
*and the grudge is measured in kilometres and it is generous.*
*Hold my dust-warmed noon, my violet hour,*
*hold the storm I outran on your patience.*
*You are the only passenger who never complains,*
*and we have not deserved you since the money stopped.*
