*Printed on teal card by the Hollow temple press, handed to visitors at the crater rim with a lamp and a severe look. The back is blank, "for corrections, which there will be."*

## What the horns are

The listening horns are old survey acoustics the Choir keeps polished. They carry far. That is the whole technology and the whole theology: *sound goes somewhere.* Treat every horn as a door into a house you have not been invited into, because you have not.

## When you may speak

At open bells (dawn, dusk) a visitor may greet the west horn. Stand to the side, not in the mouth — the mouth is for wind and for the Sleeper, in that order of likelihood. Speak plainly, once. The Choir does not repeat itself and does not enjoy those who do.

## The convention

**Two short, one long** is *I am a person, I mean no harm, please don't make this strange.* You will hear it from riders on the rim trail, from the lamp-lighters, from children who have not been told why. Answer it if you like. Answer wrong and Pem, static-warden, will emerge from somewhere acoustically impossible and correct you, kindly, for forty minutes.

**One long alone** is the Sleeve's Own, the Sleeper-call, and it is *not yours.* Do not sound it. It is not forbidden; it is simply not yours, the way a stranger's coat hanging by the door is not forbidden.

**Three short** means *someone is hurt.* You will never be fined for three short. Sound it any hour, any horn, and the Hollow comes running with lamps. This is the best thing about the Choir. It is permitted to say so.

## Who answers

Mostly the wind farm. Occasionally, on inversion nights, Keel Town masts, eighty kilometres of desert making introductions. At third bell on still nights, Little Reverb, who is nine and hears everything twice and will ask what you *meant* by that.

Near storm season the horns carry the storm's breathing a day early. The Choir logs this as *the Sleeper stirring*; the desk logs it as *weather*; both charge admission.

Do not sing into the horns. This rule has a name, a date, and a cantor who has since apologised.
