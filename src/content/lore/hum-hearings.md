*From the minute-book of the Salt Guild, Saltmouth exchange, recorded by the duty clerk and annotated by the Office of the Counterweight (see also Memo 7/Season, which this office will not be explaining). Convened in emergency session after a Driftline courier logged, in good faith and poor judgement, a crate that answered.*

---

## Hearing the First: whether a hum is cargo

**PRESIDING:** Sister Counterweight, Auditor-Titular. **PRESENT:** Factor Tamsin Cho; Quill, duty clerk; the crate (deponent, uncertified).

**FACTOR CHO:** The manifest field for sender is blank. The field for consignee reads *onward*. The payment field reads *settled*, which our books confirm is a lie, and our books have asked to go home.

**COUNTERWEIGHT:** For the record, describe the sound.

**CLERK:** It hums. When the pressure drops before a storm it hums; when spoken to it pauses, then resumes at the same pitch, which the clerk finds worse.

**RULING (1):** A hum is not cargo. A hum is a *property of* cargo, like weight or regret, and is billable at the standard surcharge for *unspecified audibility, third class*.

## Hearing the Second: testimony of the night dog

**COUNTERWEIGHT:** Call the exchange's night dog.

*The night dog (Lantern, eleven, good) was sworn by being told she was good, which she confirmed.*

**CLERK:** Lantern has stood watch beside the crate eleven nights. Transcript of testimony: [two barks]. [pause]. [one bark, lower]. Counsel for the manifest submits this constitutes *it depends*. Counsel is sustained.

**COUNTERWEIGHT:** Lantern, does the crate mean harm?

*Lantern lay down with her chin on her paws facing the crate, which the record will treat as* no, *rather than* yes, *on the grounds that in eleven years she has never once lain down facing the storm shutters.*

**RULING (2):** The witness is entered as *of reasonable character*. The crate is entered as *tolerated by Lantern, provisionally*.

## Hearing the Third: whether the hum can be insured

A Choir witness (lamp rank, name withheld at the witness's request, on the grounds that "names are for things that have decided") testified that the hum is *a question being kept warm*, and that the crate is a key, and that keys are not cargo but *intentions with handles*.

**FACTOR CHO:** If the intention detonates, who pays?

**WITNESS:** Everyone, eventually.

**COUNTERWEIGHT:** The Guild is asked to underwrite an intention. Requesting clarification: whose?

*At this point — the clerk notes, neutrally and at length — the crate hummed. Eight times. The eighth hum resolved a standing argument between two tuning forks in the survey cabinet, which now agree. The hearing adjourned. The word "adjourned" is doing, the clerk wishes noted, considerable work.*

**RULING (3), final:** Philosophy is uninsurable; faith is out of scope; *keys with handles* will be classified under existing statute as **freight, persuasive, do not stack**. The crate shall be carried, per the creed, by couriers in rotation, because the Driftline's seven stupid words turn out to be load-bearing: any crate, any storm, any door.

**MARGINALIA, Auditor-Titular's hand, added after the binding was closed:** *We held three hearings to decide what the crate is, and the only witness who answered plainly was the dog. Filed under: settled. The Office will take no queries. The Office is counting backwards.*
