*AIR CHECK is the Standard's speed segment, broadcast whenever the relay tower feels like sport and Vela Marlowe has a guest who can't sue. This one aired the week the canyon records went strange. — transcription circle*

**[theme: the tenor horn, faster, still flat]**

## The Record, With Asterisk

MARLOWE: Welcome to Air Check. My guest holds three canyon lap records, one of which is real — his words, his asterisk. Jett Marrows, welcome.

MARROWS: Vela. Flats. I'd bow but aerodynamics.

MARLOWE: Let's start with the asterisk. Undefeated.*

MARROWS: The asterisk is pending litigation, Vela.

MARLOWE: With whom?

MARROWS: The concept of losing. Case is ongoing. My counsel is confident.

**[laughter from the lamp-ring crowd; a goat can be heard declining to comment]**

MARLOWE: Talk to me about the training regime.

MARROWS: Rigorous. Scientific. I wake up, I look at the canyon, and I ask it: *are we winning?* And it always — always — says—

MARLOWE: It's a canyon.

MARROWS: It says *yes.* That's called mentorship. The full programme is: winning, then a light second breakfast, then winning again, sometimes at night, when the neon from Pell's noodle sign lights the last gate and a man can pretend the desert is applauding in amber.

MARLOWE: It spells NOODLES.

MARROWS: It spells however I need it to spell, Vela. Mindset.

## The New Courier, and the Third Gate

**[static; in the static, three seconds of something almost rhythmic]**

MARLOWE: Assess the field. New courier out of Saltmouth. Varga.

MARROWS: Who?

MARLOWE: The one with the crate.

MARROWS: Ah — Parcel. Yeah. Debt-bike, second-hand skirts, left turn like a customs form. Raw. Fast-ish. Humiliating to draft behind, because the box gets a better exit speed than the rider and *everyone can hear it.* If the crate ever gets its own bike I'll race the crate and lose to a parcel and retire to sell grip tape, and I want that on record so nobody can say the sport didn't warn them.

MARLOWE: Gate ethics. Riders say you cut the third gate at the Slick Mile.

MARROWS: I *negotiate* the third gate. My line through it is... optimistic.

MARLOWE: That's not a no.

MARROWS: It is a *statistically adjacent* yes. Next question.

## The Wall Question

MARLOWE: Last one. Storm season's on. Pemmy stamped four. You don't race the walls.

**[a pause; the horn in the background seems to hold its note]**

MARROWS: No. First wall of the year is Davenant's, that's just the truth of it — he took it on so the rest of us would read it right, and we read it, and we sit. You don't race a wall, you flirt and leave. Storms hate a clean exit and respect a clean witness. Cut that part if it sounds soft.

MARLOWE: It's staying in.

MARROWS: Then for the record: I'll beat any rider alive *after* the thaw. And Parcel — south gate, sunrise. Bring the box. It's the only training partner you can afford.

MARLOWE: Jett Marrows, everyone. Flat salt, following wind. Sleep moored.

**[sign-off chime; under it, an engine, far off, doing two short revs and one long]**
