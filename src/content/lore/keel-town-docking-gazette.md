*The Docking Gazette is posted at the winch base each Long-day by Dockmaster Shore's office and annotated by everyone before the ink takes. This copy includes the annotations, because removing them has been tried and the annotators have pens and patience.*

## Long-day schedule — week of the Third Slow Moon

**BERTH 1 — *Ceaseless Adjustment*, grain clipper, in from the north rim.** Two days late, blames weather, means cards. Whole hold smells of brine-shrimp. *(annotation: "Smells of brine-shrimp because it is hauling brine-shrimp, Sella, some of us read manifests.")*

**BERTH 2 — *Ferro's Answer*, tug.** Moored for refit. The Answer is forty years old and her crew claims she is the last honest weld on Kessa-9. The crew has claimed this while welding continuously for eleven years.

**BERTH 3 — VACANT.** Reserved for the *Pale Lark*. The *Pale Lark* has been expected for nine years. The berth is swept weekly. Do not discuss. *(annotation: "Discussed at length, twice, behind the winch house. Resolve: keep sweeping.")*

**BERTH 4 — *Minor Dispute*, charter skiff, Choir colours.** Offloaded glass vessels, onloaded lamp-oil and one passenger who stood on the dock rail for an hour listening to the moorings. Passenger declared "the tuning is almost right" and paid double moorage. Choir pays in exact change arranged by weight. Nobody knows why. Accept it.

**MOORAGE MAST — Driftline courier line.** Three bikes up, one down for the night. Ketch's girl with the second-hand machine made the mast climb in under a minute on a dare. The dare is now an entry in the moorage book. *(annotation: "It was fifty-one seconds and I have witnesses. — D.S.")*

**UNLISTED.** The crate passed by again.

That is the entire entry. It appears in the unlisted column the way unlisted things do, which is to say exactly often enough that the column exists. No berth, no colours, no carrier — the slate by the mast just showed it scratched in chalk at dawn, in handwriting the Dockmaster describes, when asked, as "familiar enough that I am not answering the question." The Gazette prints the dockings that can be seen. Some weeks that is a shorter list than the other list.
