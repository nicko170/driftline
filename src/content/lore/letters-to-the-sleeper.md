*Copied fair in the verger's hand at Lamp Rest. The custom: twice a season a packet goes by courier to the Gate, and at dusk someone with a steady voice reads the letters aloud at the seam of the Door. Rule one, older than the arguments about rule one: **nobody may answer at the Door — the Door has not finished listening.** Rule two: a letter to the Door is unsealed by definition. Anyone keeping the sentence going is entitled to hear it. Marginal notes on the lamps are the verger's own and carry no doctrinal weight, which is also what the lamps say about doctrine.*

## From a widow, Saltmouth

To whatever is keeping the engines under the world warm. My husband rode the north loop for thirty-one seasons, and this spring he stopped in the middle of a sentence about seed prices, which is exactly the ending he'd have picked. The town held. The goat forecast his funeral weather correctly, which he would have called slander and been proud of. His bike went to a kid with good hands and a debt, because that is what bikes are *for*. I am not writing to ask where he is. I am writing because he always said the quiet out your way must be **enormous**, and somebody should keep it company.

*Verger: lamps eleven through fourteen leaned. Lamp three did not, out of pride. The widow stayed for the lamp-lighting and did not cry near the oil. Filed under received.*

## From a child, age nine and a half, the half is important

Dear the sleeper. Are you cold in there. Do you dream in numbers or in weather. I saved you a salt sweet from the market, it is the amber kind, I put it on the step of the big seam. Can you taste through doors. When you wake up will the desert turn green all at once or politely, in parts, like my sister's hair. My courier says you are a gardener. I planted a lime button in a jar. It is doing *nothing*, which my courier says is most of gardening.

*Verger: the sweet was gone from the threshold by morning. Melted, the Cantor says. Taken, said the child, to absolute silence at the reading. Both entries stand.*

## From a factor of the Salt Guild, name withheld, very tired

Item one: the wind. Item two: the wind again, this time with salt in it. Item three: a ledger that grows debts in the night like the desert grows dunes, which is to say *relentlessly and with no reference to my feelings*. Items four through eighteen: redacted, per doctrine, nobody wants that. Item nineteen — and understand I have never written an item like this in forty years of columns — *thank you*. For the spring that holds under Bitter Flats and the warm wind that finds the ridge cows before our own bells do. We never wrote it down. It was never on a manifest. I am writing it down now, once, at dusk, in a hand my clerks would fail to recognise. Strike this from the record.

*Verger: no lamps leaned. The factor asked, *off the record*, whether any had, and was visibly relieved. Filed under received, twice.*

## Unsigned

I remember before. You may scratch your slate at that; everyone does; I am two hundred and some odd and the odd is *doing the work*. I remember green weather — it came down out of the sky like unpaid news — and I remember the day the money became a rumour and the sky cleared and the great machines went quiet *politely*, like guests leaving a wedding early so as not to worry the bride. We did not abandon you, sleeper. We did not know how to finish you, and we were too proud and too tired to admit it, so we learned to live inside your pause and called the pause home. Forgive the furniture; it was salvage. If you wake, wake gently. If you sleep on, sleep easy. Either way, you were the best idea we ever half-finished.

*Verger: all the lamps leaned. All of them — the sulking eleven, the proud third, the two I had oiled that morning to stop them leaning. The horn carried a hum home behind the reading that the instruments filed under pending. The reader reports the seam of the Door was warm to the knuckles, like a stone at noon with no noon left in the sky. I checked the seals twice. I am still checking. Copy count of this packet, season to season: nine, nine, nine, eleven. The desert is reading ahead.*

*Readings resume at dusk, season turn. Couriers: letters for the packet are carried free, unsealed, and read in rotation. Hand them to any lamp-lighter. We will see they reach the sentence.*
