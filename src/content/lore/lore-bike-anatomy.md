## The horizon cell

Your bike's battery is a **horizon cell**, so called because the first ones were salvaged from mothballed survey satellites that were built to chase a horizon forever. It does not charge so much as *agree to continue*. Keep it out of standing water, keep its salt-bridge contacts clean with a dry thumb, and never — this bit is underlined for a reason — never open one to see the horizon inside. There isn't one. That is the disappointment that powers the bike.

A healthy cell idles with a hum you feel in your teeth, about the pitch of a man thinking about lunch. If yours hums higher, it's scared. If it stops humming entirely, you are walking, and you should begin composing the apology to whoever owns the cargo.

## The gyro cages

Under the saddle sit two **gyro cages**, spinning in opposite directions so the bike leans instead of tipping. You will never see them. You will know them instantly when one fails, because the bike will develop Opinions about left. Rebalance is a garage job. The field fix is to lean the other way and make plans.

## Boost cells and why they are always empty

The **boost cell** is a separate reservoir that drinks the horizon cell the way a committee drinks a budget. It refills when you ride clean: long glides, drift exits, anything smooth enough to impress physics. Riders who boost everywhere arrive nowhere, slowly, on fire. Riders who boost *out of a drift* arrive first and pretend it was easy. Be the second kind.

## Cargo shield

The **cargo shield** is the padded field cage behind you, and it is the most lied-about component on the market. Sellers call it impact-rated. It is not impact-rated. It is *apology-shaped*. It soaks what it can and passes the remainder to your conscience, which is why fragile freight pays double.

## Closing notes

Wash your bike after salt-flat runs; the flats get into everything, including the upholstery of your future. Talk to it on long crossings; this is in the manual and I will not be embarrassed about it. And log your own maintenance. The best mechanic on the Driftline is the rider you were yesterday, leaving notes for the rider you'll be tonight.
