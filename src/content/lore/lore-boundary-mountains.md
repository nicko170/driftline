*Driftline Training Annex 20 — Boundary Mountains, lecture only. There is no practicum. The practicum is why.*

## The rim

Every map of Kessa-9 ends at the same place: a wall of violet-black peaks that catches the dusk an hour before the desert does and lets it go an hour later. The terraform charts call them the Boundary. The Guild calls them the Rim. Couriers call them *Weather*, as in "file you under," as in: some things are not obstacles so much as conditions, and you do not deliver to a condition.

They were never meant to be climbed. Old survey paint, flaked to ghosts on the lower scree, suggests they were never meant to be *there* — that the phase engineers raised a retaining range to wall the work-site and the work never arrived. The mountains outlived their own justification, which makes them the most Kessa-9 thing on Kessa-9.

## The three attempts

**Emmerich Dal, guild surveyor, year 141.** Crossed on foot with porters to chart a pass for a road that was never budgeted. His party's ledger survives; the last entries are prices for things the expedition did not have, sold to nobody. Dal came back alone, solvent, and silent, and his maps show the pass clearly until the page where the paper changes weight.

**The skyship *Ample Courage*, year 168.** A dockmaster's bet that altitude beat wall. The *Courage* cleared the first ridge flying fine and was found, by wind-scouts, parked neatly on the first ridge, crew intact, holding a day's worth of memories that all agreed with one another and disagreed with the calendar by nine.

**Courier Ysolt Barrow, year 201.** The Driftline's own. Barrow took a modified long-tail up the western couloir and her radio, from the moment she passed the snowline, began transmitting *tomorrow* — positions, weather, one grocery list — a full day ahead of her. She turned back at the false summit on the advice of her own voice, which is either the wisest thing anyone has done on the Rim or the reason we will never know.

She refuses to write the annex herself. "What would I put in it?" she says. "Leave it purple."

## What the air does

For the record, so the next ambitious rookie's next-of-kin can read it plainly: above the treeline-that-isn't, radios repeat, invert, or prophesy. Compasses point *in*. Horizon cells charge from nothing and discharge into nothing else. Engines run perfectly and report being somewhere else. The Driftline's position, ratified by every hub vote since year 202, is one word: **no.**

Ride to the foothills sometime, though. At dusk. When the rim lights violet and the whole desert holds its breath underneath. It's the only border in the world nobody guards because nothing needs guarding. — *K.*
