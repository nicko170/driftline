*Transcribed live by the full Lamp Rest circle plus two Guild clerks who filed it as Weather. This transcript circulated in two versions; we have folded them together and marked the seams. We are not sorry. — transcription circle*

## Part one: the waiting

**[theme: three notes on the tenor horn — tonight, for the first time in living memory, none of them flat]**

VELA MARLOWE: Evening, flats. Evening is the wrong word. It has been evening for nine hours; the sky has declined to commit. You know why. A courier — *our* courier, the one whose name I have mispronounced for four seasons as a matter of editorial policy — is past the far glass hills with a cargo the size of a breadbox and the weight of an era. Guild towers are lit. The Hollow is singing. The Yards have shut the crushers, which has never happened, not once, not for any wedding or funeral. Everyone on this frequency is pretending to do inventory.

**[a long stretch of wind; behind it, barely, the Choir's drone holding one note]**

MARLOWE: Updates as we have them. The storms stood down at the glass hills and *held formation* — Pemmy's desk has logged this as "staccato," which we're told means either mercy or arithmetic. Dockmaster Devanna reports every mast in Keel Town ticking in time with itself. Static-Warden Pem reports that the static is *listening back*, and Pem does not editorialize, so I will say it plainly: whatever's dormant out there has one eye open, and it's watching a second-hand hover-bike with a debt on it.

## Part two: the seams and the sign-off

**[version seam — the transcripts diverge for exactly one sentence]**

MARLOWE: —and I'm told the first drop has fallen on the salt, listeners, the first *rain*, two hundred years late and right on time— *[Version B reads instead:]* —and I'm told the door has closed soft as a book, listeners, and the desert keeps what the desert earned—

**[the versions re-converge]*

MARLOWE: Either way: somebody carried it. That's the whole news. Everything else is weather.

Keep your lamps lit and your ledgers honest. This is the Standard, signing off moored — *[a pause]* — no. Tonight, sign off *unmoored*.

**[the horn, in tune; then, from every dead radio on Kessa-9 at once, one chord, held]**
