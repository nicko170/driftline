*Recorded off the water-tower antenna and typed up by volunteers at the Lamp Rest exchange. The Standard broadcasts at dusk, every dusk, weather permitting and sometimes not. — transcription circle*

**[theme: three notes on a cracked tenor horn, one flat]**

## The Civic Part

VELA MARLOWE: Good evening, flats. You're with the Evening Standard, I'm Vela Marlowe, and the season is *on*, so let's do the civic part fast. One: storm signs are not dares. Two: if your roof is loose, your roof is cargo — strap it or lose it. Three: when the wall comes, you shelter *down*, not scenic. The overlook is for sunsets and bad decisions and this week it is only for one of those.

## The Guild Meteorological Desk

Now the weather. The Guild meteorological desk — which is a goat named Pemmy standing on a brass chart — has issued its seasonal forecast, and I am contractually obliged to read it as science. Pemmy stamped *four times*, listeners. Four walls before the thaw. The Guild's acting intern attempted to correct the figure to three, at which point Pemmy ate the correction, the intern's sleeve, and — I have this from two witnesses — one (1) pencil of office. The Guild denies any insubordination at desk level.

**[paper shuffling; a pause long enough to notice]**

## One More Item

And one more item, because the board won't stop talking about it. A courier out of Saltmouth ran the season's first wall this week — outran it, if you believe the garage, which you shouldn't entirely — carrying sealed cargo through winds that folded a Guild crawler like a napkin. Name on the manifest is— *[static]* —Vargas? *Axle* Vargas. New on the line. The cargo, I'm told, hums. The Guild tells me it does not hum, and would like me to say so, which I just have.

Folks, when I was young the storms were weather. This one moved like it was *counting* what crossed it. Cross politely.

**[a dead station bleeds through: three seconds of something almost rhythmic, then the horn, still flat]**

MARLOWE: That's the Standard. Flat salt and a following wind. Sleep moored.

**[sign-off chime; under it, very faint, a bell that has been dead for sixty years, ringing twice]**
