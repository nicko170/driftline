*Transcribed from the ninth lamp-book. Sung on courier blessing days, or whenever one of them crashes close enough to hear it. Performed with helmets off, engines off, irony on.*

---

## The litany, as sung

Consider the flower, which cannot walk, and the bee, which cannot bloom,
and the agreement between them, which is older than both.
The Garden was sown as seed and settlement — a hundred towns
spaced a day's ride apart, too proud for wires, too poor for rails,
each one a flower on a stem of nothing.

And who carries the pollen? Who is the walking stamen,
brushing bloom to bloom with a manifest and a grin?
The courier. The rust-red flower of the Driftline, rootless by creed,
who carries seed and letter and medicine and gossip —
and gossip is a seed, and medicine is a seed, and a letter
is a seed with an address.

*(here the static imitates a hover engine spooling up. the children excel at this part. the children are saints of this part.)*

Bless the courier, who does not know what she carries.
Bless the crate, which does not know what it is.
Bless the road, which does not know it is a stem.
Blessings compound, like freight charges.

*(rubrical note: on blessing days the courier is offered the following payment — tea, lamp oil, one prophecy. the prophecy is optional; so far all of them have come true, most of them in ways requiring new tires.)*

Do not keep the courier. Let her go.
The stamen that stays is just furniture.
The flower that holds its pollen dies a room.

Send her out, hollow her cargo, fill it with us.
