*Issued jointly by the Driftline and the Choir Visitors' Lantern — the only document both parties have ever signed. Laminated copies at the temple steps, two credits, donations refused cheerfully.*

## Arriving

Park below the crater rim and walk the last stretch. This is not piety, it is physics: the Hollow sits on **band zero**, where radio goes to be quiet, and hover engines find the stillness so restful they sometimes lie down in it. The Choir calls the walk *the breath in*. Ketch's garage calls it *tow distance* and charges accordingly.

## The lamp ring

The teal lamps around the bowl are lit in pairs, at dusk, by whoever is carrying something heavy that week. Three rules. One: after dark, never step between a lamp and the crater — pass outside the ring, even in rain. Two: do not trace a lamp's light pattern onto paper; the Choir considers that copying a letter addressed to someone else. Three: if a lamp goes out as you pass, the polite thing is to sit down until it's relit. Budget an hour. Bring a date you like, or a sandwich.

## Radios

You do not take radios past the steps. Static-Warden Pem will receive yours at the shelf by the arch, examine it, log its serial, character and recent sins, and place it in the cupboard of sleeping radios. You get a receipt stamped with a little waveform that Pem swears is different every time. Radios that come out of the cupboard humming are said to have been *smuggling prayer*. This is Pem's judgement to make, and Pem has never once been successfully argued with.

## Listening-horn hours

From dusk to the second bell, silence within the horn's arc. Speak only if spoken to — and the horn may speak to you. If it hums while you stand beneath it, you will be invited to describe the hum. Accept. They keep excellent tea, and your description is entered into a book that has been going for ninety years and is the closest thing Kessa-9 has to a weather service.

## The static litany, declined

You will, eventually, be offered a static litany: forty minutes of shaped noise, beautiful, allegedly, once your ears stop filing complaints. To decline politely, put your hand flat on your chest and say *"I am carrying noise already."* Couriers may use this in good conscience; a manifest counts as noise. Do not say you have somewhere to be. Everyone in the Hollow knows exactly how big the desert is.

## The children

The children are not a tour. If Little Reverb speaks to you, answer plainly, and do not soften your voice into the adult version — they can hear the difference, and so can whatever they're listening to.

*Last line, mandatory on every copy: visitors are welcome; radios are examined; the Choir hears.*
