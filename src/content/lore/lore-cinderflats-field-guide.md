## Piles and the Crusher

**The Flats are a scar that never finished healing.** A reactor-fed industrial plant half melted into its own footprint a century and a half back, and the Reclaimers have been carefully, profitably, taking it apart ever since. Everything useful leaves on a barge or a bike. Everything dangerous gets a flag, a nickname, and — if it's very dangerous — a small shrine, because Aunt Vertex believes in redundancy.

**Sorted piles, official categories.** **Walkers** — structural, safe, carry-hard stuff: girders, plate, rail. **Wishers** — parts that will work again if wished at correctly: pumps, vanes, valves, one payroll clock that still asks for a shift code. **Widows** — things that look fine and aren't; the pile is behind a fence and the fence is behind a *bigger* fence, and the crane operator who names them gets naming rights for life, which is the closest thing the Union has to employee benefits. Trade from Walkers, browse Wishers, never so much as *wave* at the Widows unless Vertex waves first.

**The Crusher.** A four-hundred-tonne hydraulic monument to the principle that everything fits in a shipping crate eventually. It predates the Union, runs on a converted smelter feed, and has moods. Mornings it sings. Evenings it sulks. If it clangs twice and stops, walk away and find Pyke; that's the jam-clearing bell, and the person nearest the feed chute is about to have a story with a moral. Nobody works the Crusher alone. Nobody has ever *successfully* worked it alone; the two people who tried agree on this from adjacent memorial plaques.

## Alarms worth believing

**Alarms that matter vs. alarms that are just opinions.** The plant still powers a few of its original klaxons, and the Union keeps them because a dead thing that still warns you about the weather deserves employment. **One long tone:** real; move away from the stacks. **Two short tones:** real; move away faster. **The rising wail:** old reactor-bay weather siren, triggers on barometric drops; it has cried wolf at every approaching storm for nine decades, but it has also never been *wrong*, so the Union's official position is that the wail is 'just an opinion with a perfect attendance record.' **Bells in the night, no pattern, from the sealed bays:** nobody's authorized to call those an opinion. Log the time. Tell Vertex. Sleep elsewhere.

**Closing chalk, small letters, new:** If you wake something up down here, you get to name it and you're responsible for it. That is not a metaphor. — *S.*
