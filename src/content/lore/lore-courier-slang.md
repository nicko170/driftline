*Compiled by dispatcher trainees at the Saltmouth garage from Ketch's chalk wall, Rill Davenant's radio transcripts, and a Guild copyist who kept writing margins during the boring manifests. Example sentences are as overheard. The Driftline runs on seven words of creed and about nine hundred of this.*

## The lexicon

**flat-salt** — adj. Everything's fine, nothing wants money. *"Windspine run was flat-salt. Two drops, one dust-devil, no paperwork."*

**following wind** — n./blessing. Luck, specifically the kind you ride. *"Following wind, kid. Bring her home loud."*

**knot-happy** — adj. Over-tied, over-secured, worried. Cargo, straps, or people. *"Cho's got that crate triple-waxed and knot-happy. Either it's worth a fortune or it's nobody's."*

**glass-sick** — adj. The wobble new riders get staring down the glassroad's shine; also, chronically, people who can't leave the canyon because it's beautiful and fast and hates them. *"Third lap and she's glass-sick again. Somebody wave a sandwich at the finish line."*

**hummer** — n. A crate that hums. This season: specifically *the* crate that hums. Before this season: a myth dispatchers told rookies to watch them check their cargo twice. *"Manifest says kitchenware, box says hummer. Which do you bill the Guild for? Both. Bill them for both."*

**count it twice** — v. Guild loan-phrase, fully naturalised. Double-check, or: I'm serious. *"Storm line's early, count it twice."*

**doormat** — n. A settlement with a job board but no landing pad. Dismissive, affectionate, accurate. *"Delivered to a doormat past the cairns. Lovely people. Landed on their pump house, gently."*

**long salt** — n. A run with no shade, no shelter, no talking. *"Drowned Array round-trip is long salt and shorter answers."*

**wax-reader** — n. A courier who judges a job by the seal instead of the pay. Traditionally wise. Frequently broke. *"He's a wax-reader. Saw that ochre seal and his whole face did accounting."*

**teal-eyed** — adj. Choir-touched; hearing the static a little too fondly. *"Pem's teal-eyed but her storm calls are better than the instruments."*

**scrap-grateful** — adj. The Reclaimer virtue of thanking anything about to fall down. *"Tower came apart polite. Scrap-grateful all round. Vertex took a knee."*

**brand-new** — n./mild insult. A rider on their first season. *"Easy, brand-new. The brake's the other lever and the sky's still up."*

**shelf it** — v. To officially lose a problem. Guild origin, deployed ironically everywhere. *"Where's the debt paperwork?" "Shelved it." "Beautiful."*

**bite the ridge** — v. Take the high line through windspine to save time and spend courage. *"She'll bite the ridge. She always bites the ridge. Somebody warm up the stretcher-biscuits."*

**quiet** — n. Dead air where a courier's beacon used to be. The one word nobody jokes about twice. *"Board shows quiet on the north loop. Helmets on. The line goes looking."*

*— plus: burner dust (storm speed), mule-cell (borrowed horizon cell), chandelier (the skydocks at dusk), church money (Choir tips, always exact), the mathematics (Ketch's word for consequences), and tourist (anyone who says 'it's just sand').*
