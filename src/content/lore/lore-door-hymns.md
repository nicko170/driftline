*Copied from the slate lectern at the cradle approach, Choir Hollow cantor's office. The lectern is cleaned daily. The door, the wardens report, prefers the singing to the silence, though they admit this is "a vibe-based measurement."*

## Hymn of Approach (processional meter, slow)

*We do not knock, for knocking claims.*
*We do not crowd, for crowding names.*
*We bring the lamp. We bring the load.*
*We stand as weather stands the road —*
*not asking, Mother, to be heard,*
*but keeping warm a sleeping word.*

Sung on the last kilometre. The wardens time the walk to it: eight verses per kilometre, breathing on the off-beats. Pilgrims who hurry the hymn are sent back to the ridge to walk it kindly.

## The Counting Hymn (work chant, call and answer)

*One: the seam that isn't there,*
*Two: the glass that keeps its care,*
*Three: the arm the mountain lends,*
*Four: the cold the black face spends,*
*Five: the key that travels far,*
*Six: the door that knows you are.*

Cantor Ilex insists the numbers are devotional. Static-Warden Pem, after eleven years of listening, notes in the margin of the lectern slate: *the numbers are also true.* The seam reads zero on every instrument. The glass holds its temper within a hair. The mountain's fused ring bears load like a cradle-arm. The door-face radiates cold in a steady six-count. Either the Choir hid an inspection checklist in a hymn, or the inspection checklists everywhere have been hymns all along.

## The Fatigue Verse (sung to the door itself, weekly)

*Hold, hold, your temper long,*
*no sudden load, no closing wrong;*
*rest in compression, wake in chord —*
*stress in cycles, not in the sword;*
*a hinge is grief repeated slow —*
*open once, and gently go.*

This is the secret one. A decommissioned terraforming manual, era-authentic from the cadence, would call each line by another name: thermal cycling tolerance, torsional load limits, crack propagation, controlled decompression. Aunt Vertex of the Reclaimers heard it once by accident, stood very still for a verse, and said *that's a service schedule with a chorus,* and the Choir, graciously, in writing, agreed.

## Lullaby of the Door (unmetered; hummed)

No words. The wardens hum it at dusk in the doorway's lee, one note longer each night, because Little Reverb says the door hums back *one note shorter,* as if every evening it saves the difference, note by note, toward the sentence it means to say when the key comes home. Nobody laughs at this. The lamp-oil log shows the dusk humming has never once been missed in sixty years. The Choir counts everything worth counting; that was the Guild's joke. The Choir heard it, and took it as a compliment, and wrote it on the lectern: *we count in verses. Someday the door will answer in kind.*
