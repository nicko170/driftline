*Driftline Training Annex 15 — Drowned Array, intermediate and up. Half of this region is an old solar farm; the other half is a salt pan that is eating it. Both halves are honest about it.*

## The cairn code

**Stone speaks.** The Array is webbed with courier caches — sealed tins of water, horizon cells, medwrap, once a whole birthday cake in stasis wrap — and every cache is marked by a cairn. Read them top to bottom:

- **One flat stone on top:** cache is stocked, take what you need.
- **Two flat stones:** stocked, but the next courier through should restock — the tin keeps a tally slate, sign it.
- **An upright stone:** empty. Do not curse the cairn; the cairn did not drink the water.
- **A black stone anywhere in the stack:** hazard nearby — soft brine, live panel, or a reclaimer crew's tripod claim. Circle wide.
- **No cairn where last year's chart shows one:** the pan moved. Trust the pan; it has seniority.

Building a false cairn — marking a cache that isn't there — is the one offence that gets a courier's knot cut at every hub on the line. It has happened twice. Both names are still spoken the way you speak of weather that killed someone.

## Riding the pan

**Panel-field shortcuts.** The standing panels lean in rows like drunks holding each other up, and the gaps between rows are the only straight lines in the region. Panel shade is ten degrees cooler; panel shadow at speed will grab your skirt where the frames still shed current. Ride the *lit* side of a row or give the dark side a full skirt's width.

**The caretaker.** Someone maintains the substation at the pan's heart — lights on, pumps coughing, a mail slot that empties weekly. Nobody admits to knowing who. Driftline policy is a parcel a month, addressed CARETAKER, ARRAY, left at the drowned substation door. The parcels always go. Once, one came back with the stamps licked and a note: *thanks — the cake worked.*

**Dusk shine.** At dusk the whole pan lights up amber — brine so shallow and still it mirrors the sky and doubles it. It is the single best place on Kessa-9 to see your own bike from above. It is also when the brine crust is coldest and most brittle. Take your photograph from the pan edge. The Array has drowned prettier machines than yours, some of them recently. — *K.*
