*Compiled for the Courier's Rest shrine wall from three contradictory civic accounts. The compiler requests that readers stop arguing about this on the shrine wall itself. The paint is tired. — Compiler's note, Year 209*

## The shape all three versions share

In the first years of the Delay, when the money was freshly gone and the settlements still believed the ships were coming back, a rider crossed the glass flats ahead of a closing storm, carrying an unmarked crate to a sealed door in the far hills. The crate arrived. The door opened. Whatever came out of that room — medicine, weather, news, three grapes — is why we are alive to argue about paint.

That is the whole legend. Everything else is civic property.

## The Saltmouth account

Saltmouth insists the rider was a **dockhand named Vess** and the crate was payroll — the last honest wages on Kessa-9, delivered out of spite. The door was a Guild vault, which unlocked, audited the coins, and paid everyone. Saltmouth children recite: *the first courier carried money and the desert has been billing us ever since.* The Guild teaches this version. The Guild would.

## The Keel Town account

Keel Town insists the rider was a **skyship pilot, name withheld from record**, which Keel Town finds far more dignified than "Vess." The crate held soil — living soil from the last greenhouse, carried at storm-speed to a sealed seed vault. The door opened, swallowed the soil, and Keel Town maintains that every green thing on this planet is technically *delivered mail.* Their version ends with a chime. There is always a chime in the Keel Town versions of everything.

## The Lamp Rest account

Lamp Rest, which is to say the Choir, insists the crate held a **name** — that some lost machine begged a stranger to carry its name to its cradle so it would not sleep nameless. The rider said the name at the door. The door opened *because it was pleased.* The Choir version has the fewest facts and, annoyingly, the best ending.

## Compiler's verdict

Three settlements, three crates, one rider, one storm, one open door. The Driftline's own position is the creed, which refuses to specify: *any crate, any storm, any door.* Riders say that's not evasion, it's the point — the first courier didn't know what they were carrying either. They carried it anyway. That's the entire job description, two hundred years running.

*Final note, same compiler, added later: the shrine wall now has a fourth account, in fresh paint, unsigned, that says the crate hummed. Nobody has painted over it. Nobody will.*
