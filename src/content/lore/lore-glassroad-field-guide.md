*Driftline Training Annex 7 — Glassroad, advanced. Do not read this appendix until you have fallen off somewhere cheaper.*

## Grip, veins and the fast line

**Grip, honestly.** Fused glass holds about half the bite of dry salt and lies about the rest. The old reactor burn cooled in ripples, so the canyon floor is effectively corduroy the size of a riverbed. With the ripple: a firm handshake. Across it: a slap. Read the grain with your eyes at speed and with the seat of your suit the rest of the time. A hoverbike barely touches the ground, but your skirt wash, your dust line, and your pride all follow the grain, and two out of three of those matter.

**Vein heat.** The bright teal seams running through the floor are not decoration. They are where the burn ran hottest and they hold that heat like a grudge — two centuries later, a vein at noon will still boil spit. Crossing one is survivable. *Riding along* one warps fairings, cooks a horizon cell to two-thirds life, and once, memorably, set fire to a wedding crate. The Guild paid out. The couple eloped anyway. Kessa-9 endures.

**The drift line.** Counter-intuitive, so it's underlined in the original: the fast line through the long bends is a held drift, not a clean carve. Glass rewards a slide you commit to and punishes a correction you flinch into. Boost *into* the slide, not out of habit, and let the exit hand your speed back to you like change from a bartender who respects you.

## The shrine and the legend

**The mid-span shrine.** Strut-shrine, tank-shrine, or 'mother's sconce,' depending on which faction you ask within earshot of which other faction. It sits where the canyon bends hard enough that its dusk shadow falls cold across warm glass, and that seam of temperatures will grab your skirt like a customs officer with a quota. Hop the shadow or square up through it. Never, ever turn inside it.

**The Slick Mile and Sel Ondray.** The legend, as sworn to at three bars with three different endings: courier Sel Ondray, forty years back, entered the Mile behind a storm front and drifted the whole kilometre — one continuous slide, vanes screaming, hands off the bars for the last hundred metres, *conducting*. Whether Ondray beat the storm or died doing it depends on the bar. What's carved at the Mile's far end, nobody disputes: *BRAKE LATER.*

The carving is not sarcasm. Probably. — *K.*
