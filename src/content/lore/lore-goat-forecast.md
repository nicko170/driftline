*Filed by the Guild Bureau of Meteorology (acting head: vacant; acting intern: resigned) in response to Form 91-C, "Request to Justify a Desk." Verdict attached. The verdict is a hoof print.*

## Statement of the desk

The Guild meteorological desk forecasts storms for the whole Saltmouth basin and has done so for eleven seasons with a loss rate of zero couriers, zero caravans, and one (1) pencil of office. Its method is as follows. A brass chart of the basin, engraved with wind roses and storm doors, is laid flat in the weather loft each dawn. **Pemmy**, a salt-white goat of unimpeachable seniority, is admitted. Where Pemmy stands, that region is fair. Where Pemmy stamps, a wall is coming — one stamp per wall before the thaw. Where Pemmy lies down, all riders shelter; the desk calls this *the velvet forecast* and couriers call it *the goat says no.*

The Bureau submits that this is a *sensor array with opinions,* and is cheaper than the satellites, which are dead.

## Appendix A: Dr. Ilse Vane, atmospherics (consulted)

"I spent a season in the loft with instruments. The goat beat my barometer by six hours, eleven times out of eleven. My hypothesis — micro-pressure detection via the inner ear — failed inspection because Pemmy began stamping *before* the pressure moved, as though the pressure moved because she stamped. I have stopped publishing. I now hold the chart steady. It is honest work."

## Appendix B: Brother Decibel, the Choir (consulted)

"The static over Windspine changes texture before a wall; any warden knows it. Pemmy knows it without ears on the band, which means she hears it in the world itself, the way Little Reverb hears HER. The Choir does not claim the goat. The Choir wishes to be very clear: the goat is not scripture. The goat is, however, *right more often than scripture,* and we are sitting with that."

## Appendix C: Boss Pyke, the Reclaimers (consulted)

"Brought her down the yards to sniff the old turbines before storm season. She ate a disarmed detonator cap and stamped twice on a hull we were cutting that afternoon. Hull toppled in a gust, empty, money saved. Invoice her. We tried. The Guild said she is salaried in pencil-of-office and dignity. Fine. She's the best sensor on the flats and I'd strip my own grandmother's skiff before I'd argue with her."

## Appendix D: Fen, age eight (unconsulted; appended anyway)

Fen, a child of the loft stairs, submitted — on the back of a ration chit, in crayon, uninvited — the only field observation the Bureau considers complete: *"Pemmy checks the sky first. Then she checks the doors. Then she checks who is riding. THEN she stamps. She is not predicting the storm. She is deciding whether to warn us."*

## Determination

Form 91-C is denied. The desk is justified. Couriers are reminded: never race the goat's window — the goat knows — and those who ignore a stamp do not get to appeal to the goat afterwards. Pemmy remembers. Pemmy files nothing. Pemmy does not need to.
