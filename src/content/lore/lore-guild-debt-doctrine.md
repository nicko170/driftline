*Distributed free by Guild exchanges. Nowhere in the Desert is anything else free, which recipients are encouraged to reflect upon.*

## Doctrine

**Why the Guild holds debt.** Salt keeps you alive, water keeps you longer, but *obligation* is the only commodity that travels both directions at once. A debt is a thread between two people who would otherwise be weather to each other. The Guild does not lend money. The Guild lends *reasons to come back.*

**Why we tally honestly.** A debt recorded high is cruelty; recorded low, contempt. Your factor weighs what you owe the way a brinesmith weighs the harvest: to the grain, in your presence, with the scale where you can see it. Anyone may ask for a reweighing. This has never once changed a number and has saved four hundred friendships, which is what the scales are *for.*

**Why we forgive at a hundred years.** Charter, Article Six: debts are honored, tallied, and occasionally forgiven on the debtor's hundredth anniversary, whichever comes second. It is not mercy, and doctrine forbids calling it mercy. It is housekeeping. A ledger that outlives every name in it stops being an account and becomes an *opinion about the dead,* and the Guild does not deal in opinions. (The Choir does. They are welcome to them.)

**On paying down.** Debts fall at whatever pace honest work allows, and doctrine recognizes no dishonest pace, including the heroic kind. A debtor who kills themselves working pays the Guild nothing but funerals, which are *expensive feelings.*

## The Ambition Balance (the famous footnote)

**The Ambition Balance.** Occasionally the Guild extends credit that no sober factor should approve: a courier with no collateral, a second-hand bike, and a look. Standard practice since Year 88, formalized as the Ambition Balance, after Guildmaster Pell, asked why she'd lent eight thousand credits to *that kind of person,* answered: *"The Desert takes gamblers and leaves pilgrims. Lend to the pilgrims."* Current doctrine requires only that the factor write, in the margin of the debt, what the debtor hopes to become. If you are such a debtor — and you are, we can tell, you have the bike and the look — know that your margin note is already drafted, in excellent penmanship, and it says something kinder than you'd expect.

Pay it down, courier. Take your time. **Come back.**
