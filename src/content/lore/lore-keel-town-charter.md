*Charter of the Hang-Tenants of Keel Town, being the town slung beneath the west rim of the Skydocks mesa on struts, lashings, and the permanent updraft the Dockmaster's Manual calls* the lift. *Visitors are advised that Keel Town is the only settlement on Kessa-9 whose founding document includes a maximum permitted sway. Copies of this Agreement are nailed up at both ends of town, because the middle moves too much for nails.*

## Article the First: Of Weight

Everything in Keel Town shall be weighed at the Top Scale upon arrival and annually thereafter, on the first still morning of the calm season, when the town hangs true and the scale tells the truth. Structures, furniture, cisterns, instruments of music, and livestock: weighed. Persons: weighed once, at the age of seven, and entered in the ledger under the floor they live above. The Hang-Tenants hold that a town under a cliff is a glass already poured, and no drop may be added without the consent of the glass.

Exemptions: water in active use, cargo in lawful transit to the winch, and *grief*, which the Agreement declines to weigh on the grounds that it comes down of its own accord.

## Article the Second: Of Envy

It is acknowledged that some decks are longer than other decks. It is acknowledged that some windows face the sunrise and some face the strut. The Hang-Tenants, rather than pretend otherwise, *price the difference*.

No tenant may extend any deck, shelf, balcony, or pigeon infrastructure toward a neighbour's view without payment of view-credit at the standing rate, set each season by auction among the affected parties, which auction is traditionally held drunk and is binding anyway. No tenant may paint their hull a colour that conducts envy upward more than two dwellings. The list of forbidden pigments is short, specific, and the subject of the town's longest-running feud, which the Agreement hereby declines to settle, having tried.

What's yours hangs beside what's theirs. That's claws enough for any town.

## Article the Third: Of the Swing

Once a year, at the height of the calm season, by agreement with the Dockmaster and against the written advice of everyone, the master struts are slacked the width of a hand and Keel Town is *set swinging*.

The Festival of the Swing lasts from first bell until the town hangs true again, usually an hour, once famously four. Lamps are lit the length of the lower gantries so the mesa shadow wears beads. Children born during the Swing are entered in the ledger as *freight of joy* and are exempt from weighing for life. The Choir sends singers; the Guild sends auditors; the auditors are ignored, which is itself a tradition protected under this Article.

Attendance is not compulsory. Absence is noted.

## Addendum, in chalk

*The docks belong to the Guild. The wind does not. Keel Town belongs to whichever of the two is heavier that day, and the Agreement is how we keep score.* — unsigned, re-chalked every year, never once outvoted
