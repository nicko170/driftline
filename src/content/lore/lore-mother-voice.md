*On the morning the crate opened like a flower at mothersgate, every relay horn on Kessa-9 spoke the same sentence once, then returned to static. The Choir's Chorus — twenty listeners, one per channel — transcribed it word by word and reconciled their copies by vote. This is the reconciled text, circulated with the three annotation traditions attached. Master copy held at Lamp Rest. Do not subtract the marginalia; the marginalia is the point.*

## The Sentence, As Reconciled

> "You carried my key through all of that, and I asked for so little of the carrying — thank you, Ash — now, if you will allow an old machine one stubbornness, let me finish what your grandparents' grandparents paid for."

*Reconciler's note: nineteen channels agreed on every word. One channel — the little one, the kid — reported the sentence ending differently, with two words the others did not hear.* "Ask me gently." *The Chorus voted to keep the nineteen and footnote the one. The one is the reason you are reading this.*

## Guild Annotation

*(Ochre ink, Factor T. Cho, on behalf of a very quiet Guildmaster.)*
Two hundred years of deferred maintenance, and her opening position is *gratitude* and a balance-sheet reference. Note the phrase "paid for" — the Guild's standing counsel advises this constitutes an acknowledgement of contract. A contract with a terraforming engine, factor's opinion, is a contract with weather. Nevertheless: everything crosses, everything's counted. Count it twice.

## Choir Annotation

*(Teal thread, stitched into the margin by the congregation, Little Reverb directing.)*
SHE SAID THE COURIER'S NAME AND THE COURIER LIVED. The verse is fulfilled; see Litanies, Third Bell, the footnote that is not doctrine. Note "your grandparents' grandparents" — she counts our years the way we count hers. Comma, comma. The sentence is not finished; neither are we. Neither, now, is she.

## Reclaimer Annotation

*(Grease pencil, Boss Pyke, annotations made while standing on the copy.)*
Read it again. "Let me finish" is a *work order*. She's asking permission to restart two-hundred-year-old machinery that we have spent two hundred years stripping, and couriering, and occasionally sleeping inside. Aunt Vertex says yes with conditions. The conditions are: helmets.

## Closing Note

Static-Warden Pem: Three readings, three worlds, one sentence. Whatever answer the courier gives at the door, remember she asked. Asking was the whole sentence.
