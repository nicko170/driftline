*These pages circulate hand to hand, copied badly, initialed S.U. and denied steadily. "I never wrote a guide," Urrutta says. "Guides get followed." The handwriting is hers. The corrections are also hers, in angrier ink, which is the closest the shift comes to an authorised edition. Cross-check against the issued Night Lines primer and the margin-copy notes; where they disagree, the one with fewer adjectives is probably right.*

## What black is

Riding black is not riding dark. Dark is an accident. Black is a decision you make at dusk and explain to the Guild in the morning.

No running lights. No reflectors — not the little discreet ones, not the ones you think don't count. Anything that shines shines *behind* you, and behind you is where you've been, and I have no cargo back there. The reflectors come off at the garage and go in the tin marked FINES, because you'll need one to pay with. More below.

## The kit

One headlamp, dowsable to a slit, aimed two degrees down — you're lighting the road, not the swallows. *(There are no swallows. Aim down.)* Goggles: left lens smoked darker than the right. You ride one-eyed into the west wind so the weather only ever gets half of you. Gloves with the index fingers cut out. Those are for switches, and for pointing at stars, and for pointing at stars is the one that keeps you alive: Anchorbright low east, Three Kettle overhead, Long Guile crossing in nine minutes steady as rent. Learn three lights and you can come home from anywhere.

Everything else is a jacket. Be warm. Cold riders get brave, and brave is a day-shift failing.

## Reading the salt

Starlight on a salt flat is a page, and mica-flash is the punctuation. Mica lenses little cold sparks at you where the surface changes — a drift's shoulder, a crust gone thin, glass wearing its night slick. Day riders look *at* the road. Black riders look *along* it, low, so the flat tips its glitter up to meet you. Count the flashes; when the count changes, the ground changed first.

Albedo lies gently and honestly. Pale is hard, grey is sifted, black under stars is either a shadow or a hole, and the difference is one second of patience. Take the second.

## The fine

Riding unlit carries a Guild hazard fine. Pay it. Pay it cheerfully, in coins they can hear. It is the cheapest tuition on this world, and the clerk who takes it deserves one happy transaction a week. What you do not do is argue, because arguing writes you into the ledger as *repeat offender*, and the shift does not appear in ledgers. The shift is a rumour with a delivery record.

## Lamp-lines

Never outshine a lamp-line. This is the whole etiquette of the night, so learn it whole: when a line of posted lamps is doing the work, your light is an insult to the lamps and a blindfold on everyone behind you. Douse to the slit before the first lamp. Pass through like weather. If the lamps sulk — eleven of them, some rims — let them sulk. It's their rim. You're a guest with a faster horse.

Same rule for riders. If you come up on another tail in the dark, don't light them up to say hello. Pace them at their own shadow's edge until their road straightens. They'll think it was a ghost. Let them. It's morale.

## Last note

The night does its own editing. Ride it like a draft you respect — slow where it's soft, loud where it's honest, and home by the third smudge of dawn, which is weather and not fate.
