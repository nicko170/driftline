*Collected for the Lamp Rest archive by volunteers with warm drinks and patience. Prompt given to all witnesses: "Tell me about rain." Transcribed as spoken. The archive notes a rise in requests for this file every storm season, and cannot say why.*

## Oda Krin, 94, Saltmouth, retired factor

*It smelled like the planet apologising. You forget the smell first — everybody does, everybody warns you and you forget anyway — but I kept it. Petrichor, the old word. Dust learning it was soil all along. The flats would go dark as tea and every salt tower in town glistened like it had been *paid.* I'd open the exchange doors early, just so the rain could come in a little. The Ledger Office fined me. Worth it. Everything that rained on me, I still count as mine.*

## Mestra Cahill, 88, Keel Town, skyship dockhand

*You want the noise. People ask about the noise. Rain on a moored hull is a choir with no choir-master — the big freighters rang low, the skiffs pinged, the mooring masts sang through the cables like bowed wire, and over all of it the water itself, this endless soft applause, like the sky kept* liking *us. We lost shifts to it. Whole crews stood in doorways doing nothing. It was the finest nothing I've ever been paid to do.*

## From the Keel Town customs ledger, same era

Rain-goods, annual: umbrellas, coil-sail — 4,211 sold, 19 returned *(defect: "too dry.");* waxed coat, dockhand pattern — 9,800; the line item the ledger refuses to retire: *"complaints, excess of joy, weather division — filed under losses, reversed under gains."*

## Pell Auriga, 91, the Yards, dismantler

*I hated it. Say that first. Rain found every crack in every tarp I ever owned, it turned the yards to grey porridge that ate tools and boots and one apprentice's dignity, and when it stopped — that two-century stop — I stood in my dry dry yard and said* good. *I am on record. The record keeps me honest and the young ones visit me like an attraction: the woman who hated rain. Well. I dreamed last year of standing in it. I woke up reaching for a tarp and then I sat there, and I took back nothing, and I understood, finally, that I had spent sixty years hating the wrong weather. It's the* missing *that gets you. The desert doesn't miss things. People do. That was always the terraformers' mistake — they budgeted for water and never for what water was* for.**

## Archivist's closing note

The last rain fell in year 12 of the Delay, a thin shower over the glass hills that the instruments almost called dew. Three witnesses saw it. All three used the same sentence, in three towns, without conferring. The sentence was: *"It waved."* The archive files this under verified, and has stopped asking what it means, because everyone who hears the sentence already knows.
