## Excerpts from the paper log

**Year 1, quarter 1. Carver, G. (Chief Mechanic).** Unit CR-9 commissioned. Crush drum aligned. Jaw pressure within tolerance. Still operational.

**Year 3, q2. Carver, G.** Drum bearing replaced. Unit accepted the new bearing without complaint, which the previous unit never did. Still operational.

**Year 7, q4. Carver, G.** Jaw jammed on a piece of hull plating that the schematics say came off nothing we own. Removed. Politely. Still operational.

**Year 11, q1. Okonkwo, P.** G. Carver retired; the unit was fed his old gloves at the ceremony, as is now apparently the custom. It hummed for an hour afterward. Tolerances fine. Still operational.

**Year 16, q3. Okonkwo, P.** Noted: unit runs sweet on storm mornings, sluggish on still ones. Either condensation or preference. Logged as preference. Still operational.

**Year 19, q4. Okonkwo, P.** Unit refused a load of Choir lamp-glass. Jammed twice, then gave the glass back through the feed chute *by way of the reverse cycle, which this unit does not mechanically have.* Returned the glass to the Hollow with apologies. Still operational. See also: manual, Rule Two, re: load-bearing historically.

**Year 22. Transitional entry, unsigned, in two handwritings.** Okonkwo gone to the Boundary survey and not back. The new chief is a child. The child has grease in her eyebrows and no fear. Still operational. (Both of them.)

**Year 24, q2. Vance, I. (age 14).** Machine's fine. Machine's great. Machine didn't eat my sandwich, which the older mechanics say is a sign of respect. Taught it the first verse of the pull song by running the drum slow. Still operational.

**Year 29, q1. Vance, I.** Gearcase rebuilt. Found inside: forty-one gloves, nine wedding tokens, one (1) beeswax seal from an Appendix we do not discuss, and something small and warm we agreed to leave where it was. Still operational.

**Year 33, q4. Vance, I.** Aunt Vertex asked the unit's opinion on the west gantry pull. This is now procedure. Unit said yes (one jam) instead of no (two jams). Gantry came down clean. Vertex says the machine has "stakeholder status." Still operational.

**Year 38, q2. Soder, T.** Boss Pyke has ordered — in writing, so it's true — that no one say the word "obsolete" within hearing. The unit hums back now when you hum first. Union rates for hums TBD. Still operational.

**Year 40, current. Soder, T.** Quarterly inspection. Crush drum aligned. Jaw pressure within tolerance. Somebody left it a butterscotch on the feed plate and it jammed happy for ten minutes, and honestly so would you. Two hundred years of machinery in this yard and this one eats problems and hands us back openings. When the new crates started humming last season, the whole yard got quiet — and the crusher hummed *with* them, same note, like answering a relative's knock. Logged as preference.

Still operational. Stars help the poor soul who ever logs otherwise.

*— See also: the Field Manual's Rule Three ("Never alone (see Crusher, re: moods)") and Sparks, the work song the drum now keeps time on, unasked.*
