*Transcribed at the Yards by a Guild insurance assessor, who filed it under "noise pollution" and was thereafter served butterscotch with visible disappointment. Annotations by Solder.*

## The verses

**CALL:** *Drop the line and hold the slack —*
**ALL:** *Sparks fall up when the dead comes back.*

*(Standard pull opener. "Sparks fall up" because cutting torches arc upward when a hung beam finally lets go — Solder says it's also just true about mornings.)*

**CALL:** *Girder's a wishbone, wish it wide —*
**ALL:** *Old girl holds for the ones who tried.*

*(Verse for the Big Bench collapse, third week of the year, every year. Thirty-one held, after. Nobody works Bench Day. The wishbone thing is fiddly engineering I will not explain sober.)*

**CALL:** *Count the cutting, count the cost —*
**ALL:** *Feed the Crusher nothing lost.*

*("Nothing lost" means fingers, mostly. The Crusher gets no fingers. It is a point of professional pride that the Crusher has been on a strict no-fingers diet for nineteen years.)*

**CALL:** *Windmill, windmill, end your turn —*
**ALL:** *Turn's over. We've got it from here.*

*(The turbine words, sung. Retiring one on Windspine takes six people and the song carries over the ridge. The Guild once complained the singing "devalued the silhouette." The Union's response is Appendix F, burnt, sung, unforgettable.)*

**CALL:** *Salt in the bearing, salt in the bone —*
**ALL:** *Nothing out here breaks alone.*

*(Solidarity verse. Also literally true: everything out here is one load, chained to the next thing. Physics being honest, like the foreword says.)*

## The seventh verse

**CALL:** *¶ ¶ ¶ —*
**ALL:** *¶ ¶ ¶ ¶.*

*(The seventh verse. The Guild assessor was given cocoa and a very long story about a dog by way of explanation, which the astute reader will note explains nothing. Vertex says the verse "isn't ours to sing in front of strangers." Pyke says it's a work song like any other. Rill Davenant — before — used to hum it while checking their cargo straps. It scans like the others. It has the same number of feet. The feet are the problem, that's all anyone will say.)*

**CALL:** *Bank the smelter, gate the flame —*
**ALL:** *Night good, wall good, we did it same.*

*(Closing verse, said to Anchor Seven, who is a wall, at the last bench, every shift. "We did it same" means: nobody died today who didn't have to. In the Yards, that's a whole hymn.)*
