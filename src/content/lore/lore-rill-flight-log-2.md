*These are the missing pages from Rill's logbook — torn out, not burnt, and folded into an oilskin square inside the storm gauge's service hatch, where she must have known a courier would eventually go looking. She was right. She usually is. It is infuriating. — K.*

**Season's edge. The ridge.**

The gauge bell rang twice this morning and the gauge bell has been dead since before the Yards were hot. I rode up there expecting kids with a rope. No kids. No rope. The striker was *inside* the housing, wrapped in sixty years of its own rust, and the bell was still warm. I've put my hand on a lot of machines. You learn warm-from-running and warm-from-sun. This was warm-from-*attendance*.

**Note, pinned to the margin in different ink:** if you're reading this, wrong, but keep reading. Logbooks are for the living. Yours is whichever page you're on.

**Three days later. Survey camp.**

The Choir says the static's started *scanning* — their word — like scripture being read by somebody who keeps losing their place. The Guild says the gauge is Guild property and has filed it under Weather, Pending. The windfarm kids say the turbines have been saluting *back*, and I'd call that a tall story except I taught them the salute and they say the pitch is right.

The hum's found me. Same register as the crate-you-don't-talk-about, junior — yeah, I clocked it on your rack in the garage, I clock everything, it's my whole personality. Ours are the two loudest secrets in Saltmouth. Wave if you see them holding hands.

**Last entry, no date, written hard enough to emboss the next page.**

Route: survey camp to turbine row, then the pylons past the crest, because something out there is keeping count and I want to see its face when it reaches me. Fear is data. *Ketch's voice in my head, my hand, same as ever.*

If I don't write the next page, it'll be because the desert finally answered the salute. Don't come alone. Cross politely. And if a bell that's been dead since your grandmother was a rumour rings twice — that isn't a warning, junior.

It's an *invitation*, and I've never in my life let one go to waste.

*The page ends in a ruled line drawn perfectly straight for nine centimetres, like the pen kept going after the hand stopped. — K.*
