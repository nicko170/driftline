*Recovered from the kitchen ledger at the Last Lamp (provisions, lodging, opinions), Saltmouth. Pages are annotated in at least six hands. Recipes of the Delay are not instructions; they are truces between the cook, the larder, and the weather. — transcription circle*

## Salt-baked lichen (serves: whoever stopped)

Take window-ledge lichen, the pale kind only — the teal kind is glass-fed and will outlive the meal. Brush, don't wash; washing is for towns with four tower shifts. Pack in a fist of coarse salt per head, into the hollow of last season's pan bread, and set the whole loaf on the engine cowling for the length of one honest conversation. Crack the salt. The lichen tastes of whatever the wind carried that month, which is why old couriers can date a meal.

*[Margin, Ketch's hand: "Tasted of following wind, year eleven. Of rain, never. Ordering it anyway, season after season, on principle." — K.]*

## Storm bread (the dangerous one)

You do not bake storm bread. Storm bread bakes *itself*, and you are its audience. Mix a slack dough at dusk when the fronts stack on the ridge; set it, covered, on the highest grounded platform you own; go inside. The static that crawls before a wall will raise the dough and scorch the crust in patterns the Choir reads like scripture and the Reclaimers sell as *pre-burnt, saves you the trouble*. Slice only after the wall passes. Eat facing the direction it went.

*[Margin, Pem's hand: "The loaves from the night the gauge bell rang all scored themselves in fours. I have them under wax. Don't ask." — P.]*

## Counted stew (ceremonial — Guild audit week)

The Brinemaster's stew, served once a year, by rule: every ingredient must be counted aloud as it enters the pot, by the person who carried it. Salt, counted. Water, counted. Rootstock, counted. If two diners disagree on a number, the stew waits, simmering, until the ledger of the room agrees — which is the entire point: nobody on Kessa-9 eats alone, we only eat at different speeds. The stew is ready when the room is.

*[Margin, Tamsin Cho: "Year forty-one it waited six hours over whether a tuber was one tuber or two. We ate at dawn. Best stew I ever had. Do not tell the tuber." — T.C.]*

## Courier's constancy (field ration, no ceremony)

Flatbread, salt, one dried thing, one smug thing (a condiment you are inappropriately proud of), water counted twice. Eat at speed. Compliment the desert between bites; a complimented desert is a slower desert.

*Flat salt, full pan, following wind.*
