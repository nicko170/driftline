*From the private record of Sister Counterweight, auditor-titular of the Saltmouth exchange. Not for circulation. Not for the Ledger Office. *Especially* not for the Ledger Office, who would file it, and it must not be filed, because some things you count by holding.*

## Entry the first: on scales

Mine is a brass beam the length of a coffin, older than the exchange, older than my order, accurate to the weight of a breath if the room holds still. I have weighed salt, water-shares, fused glass, three deposed guildmasters, and — by the tradition no one will explain — the souls of the newly contracted. The scale has never been wrong. It has been *inconvenient*, which is how you know a thing is honest.

I tell the apprentices: everything real has a receipt. That is the whole religion. I no longer tell them that.

## Entry: the crate

A courier brought in a sealed crate — no manifest, no sender, manifest wax stamped with nothing, which is not a seal so much as an *absence kept fresh.* On a dare, mine, I put it on the scale.

The beam did not settle. It *considered.* Forty years I have read that beam the way other women read scripture, and I have never once been read back. When it finally came to rest, the reading, in the old notation, in my own brass notation, was: **one promise, undelivered.**

I did not sleep. The scale slept fine. I checked it at dawn out of spite and it said the same thing, calmer, as if the overnight shift had already been told.

## Entry: on counting backwards

The apprentices noticed first. I take inventory backwards now — spoilage, surplus, stock, and only then what is actually on the shelves. It horrifies the Ledger Office, which pleases me, but that is not why I do it.

I do it because of what the beam showed me in its considering. Every audit I have ever run counts forward: what was, what arrived, what remains. The crate runs the other way. It is not a thing that exists; it is a thing that will have existed. Auditing it forward, you always find the column empty and the total correct. Auditing backwards — from the total, down through the remains — there it is, in every column, patient as interest.

It is coming by courier. Of course it is. That is how it gets *delivered into being.* Somebody has to carry tomorrow; that is the creed; I wept about it for an evening and then I wept no more, because weeping is spoilage and I am not spoiled, merely reconciled.

## Final entry, for whoever inherits the scale

When the beam balances by itself one afternoon, do not recalibrate it. Put on your good robe. Something real is about to arrive, and it will want to be counted, and counting it twice will not be enough. Count it backwards. Count it like it counts you: with the door held open.
