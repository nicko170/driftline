*Salt Guild Mooring Manual, Public Extract 3 — abridged for ground traffic. The full manual costs forty credits and answers more questions than most visitors can think of.*

## Winds, winches and ships

**The mesa wind.** Air strikes the great mesa and climbs it like it's late for something. The result is a permanent standing updraft on the west rim and a permanent rotten downdraft on the east. Dockmasters call the west side *the lift* and the east side *the ledger*, because of what each one does to your cargo and your accounts respectively. Approach high and west, always, or book a room and try again in the morning.

**Winch etiquette.** The cargo winch runs from the valley floor to the dockmaster's hut and it runs on *turns*, not on hurry. You flag the base, number your load, and wait for the bell: one ring, loading; two rings, haul clear; three rings, somebody has done something expensive and the line is closed while the Guild counts it. Never lean over the cable. Never light a flare within twenty metres of the cable. The list of people exempt from these rules is one name long and she is the winch.

**Skyship classes, for ground observers.** **Keels** — slab-sided Guild freighters, slow, unkillable, painted ochre at the waterline so the ledgers match. **Gulls** — slender courier-rigged schooners, the only ships cleared to ride the crest storms, crewed entirely by people with opinions about couriers. **Tenders** — small, grubby, indispensable, and privately owned; do not call them boats. A ship that arrives with its lanterns lit in daylight is carrying a bereavement. The whole dock goes quiet for it, Guild or not.

## Keel Town, and why west

**Why Keel Town hangs off the west rim.** Everyone asks. The west rim is the windy rim — why build a town there, lashed under the mesa's lip on struts and prayer? Because the lift is updraft, and updraft is *free cargo capacity*. Goods winched up the west face arrive lighter; ships moored over the west hang cheaper. Keel Town has the workshop smell of a thousand small economies making use of one big wind. Also the sunrise is better and the downdraft rim is frankly haunted by accounting errors.

**Last line of the extract, mandatory to print:** *The docks belong to the Guild. The wind does not. File complaints accordingly.* — approved, Brinemaster's office.
