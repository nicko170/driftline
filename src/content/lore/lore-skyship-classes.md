*Dockmaster's office, Keel Town. Print run: continuous. If this card is torn the apprentice replaces it. If this card is *lost* the apprentice files Form 22-B. — D.*

## The silhouette

Every skyship is two arguments that learned to cooperate: the **envelope** (the lifting cell, war-surplus gas canvas, patched in faction colours) and the **gondola** (everything else — crew, cargo, grudges). Read the envelope first, the gondola second, the paint never: paint lies, canvas doesn't.

**Spindle envelopes** — long, narrow, fast. GULL class. Courier-rigged schooners, cleared to ride crest storms. If the envelope wears a gap-tooth patch pattern, that's old reactor-burn canvas; those ships are older than the Delay and prouder about it.

**Drum envelopes** — fat, slab-sided, slow. KEEL class. Guild freight. An ochre waterline stripe means the cargo is *audited*; fly steady, do not wave, the manifest is watching.

**Blimp-and-a-half** — one cell and a stub, asymmetric on purpose. TENDER class. Private. Indispensable. Do not call them boats. They will hear you. They're close; tenders are always weirdly close.

## Moorage grades

**Grade I** — mast-moored, nose-only, engine-warm. Can slip in ninety seconds. Costs more than your house.
**Grade III** — cable-set, the honest working moor. Most of Keel Town.
**Grade V** — *stacked*. Hull lashed to hull, three deep, off the west hang. Legal, barely, and only because the Yards wrote the math on a bulkhead in chalk and dared anyone to disprove it.

## Famous hulls

- ***Following Wind*** — the Gull that outran the season's first wall forty years back. Retired as a wind vane on Mast One. Still technically moored. Still technically faster than you.
- ***Bureaucratic Reminder*** — the Guild Keel that audits the *other* auditors. Arrives unannounced. Its approach is the only sound that silences a skydock faster than a bereavement lantern.
- ***Ample Courage*** — see the Boundary Mountains file. Found parked on a ridge, nine days unaccounted. Flies now with an extra chronometer and no comment.
- ***Kestrel's Debt*** — ask the Dockmaster about her own ship at your own risk. She'll tell you. It takes an hour. It's worth it.

## Etiquette (the whole reason for the card)

Wave **anything but white**. White cloth at a skyship means *distress*, and distress at altitude means every winch crew on the mesa drops everything, including, on one occasion, lunch *into* an open envelope. Groundlings: salute hulls you admire, two fingers, off the brow, nothing thrown. Ships remember.
