*[Recovered document. Source: prospectus, Verdantine Combine, "KESSA-9: A WORLD ON SCHEDULE." Annotations in three hands, dated variously. We have kept them. They are the honest part.]*

---

## Phase One: Sky-Fixing *(years 0–8)*
Orbital mirrors shepherd the sunlight. Atmospheric processors — thirty thousand strong, self-replicating — thicken the sky to breathing weather. Guaranteed or your colony fee refunded.
> *margin note, earliest hand: "The processors replicated. The sky stayed thin. Nobody got a refund because everybody who could process one was breathing Phase One air."*
> *second hand: "Two mirrors still work. We call them the double dusk. Children call them the old suns. Free of charge."*

## Phase Two: The Salt Mercy *(years 8–30)*
Where ice cannot yet be seeded, engineered brine beds will store heat, anchor dust, and prepare the loam of tomorrow. Temporary by design.
> *"Temporary," writes the first hand, "has now lasted longer than the company that coined it. The flats are beautiful. Saltmouth sits on one. We salt fish in tomorrow's loam."*

## Phase Three: Rains *(years 30–45)*
Seed ships deliver the ocean package. Rivers within a decade of first rain. *Phase Three* is where your grandchildren swim.
> *The margin here is crowded. "Never shipped." "Shipped somewhere. Ask Verdantine's receivers. Oh — can't." And, in the third, youngest hand: "My grandmother paid a swimming surcharge. We display the receipt."*

## Phase Four: Greening *(years 45–80)*
Hardy pioneer flora, then meadow, then forest. Cafés.

> *"Cafés," says the second hand, "is doing a lot of work in this sentence."*

## Phase Five: Handover *(year 81)*
The caretaker intelligence — designation **MOTHER** — having tended every phase, gifts the finished world to its people and powers gently down, her work complete.

## Addendum: Read It Backwards

*Found clipped to the last page, in the third, youngest hand, the one that writes like a courier:*

"Read it front to back and it's a con. Read it back to front and it's a lullaby. Five, four, three, two, one — the machine powers *up*, the children swim, it rains, the salt was always a mercy, and somebody is watching the sky for you.

"The Choir says MOTHER is asleep under the glass hills at the Gate, waiting on a Phase that never shipped. The Reclaimers say she's scrap with good PR. The Guild says whatever sells.

"The Driftline says: she was built to *caretake* a world on schedule, and the schedule died, and she hasn't. Something still moves the weather out past Windspine. Something answers the Choir's horns — static, sure, but static in *patterns*. Two hundred years past handover, half-done, all alone, and the caretaker is still caretaking.

"Frankly, that's more than Verdantine ever promised. — *K.*"
