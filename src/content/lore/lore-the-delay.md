*Compiled by the Lamp Rest schoolhouse for the two-hundredth anniversary of the Delay. Interviews drawn from the old colony archive, audio restored by Static-Warden Pem, who asks that you forgive the hiss. The hiss was there first.*

## The Treasurer

**MERRIT VOSS, Assistant Treasurer, Kessa-9 Colonial Authority.**
People think the money ran out on a day. It didn't. It ran out like a tide — you keep telling yourself the next ship will cover it. The last transfer came through on a Tuesday and I remember I laughed, because it was exact. To the credit. Somebody on that end had rounded Kessa-9 down to zero, politely.

## The Teacher

**IBBIE HALE, Teacher, Settlement One.**
The school heat went off in the morning and the children asked if it was a holiday. I said yes. It was the only lie I ever told them that they all, every single one, chose to believe. We spent the day counting what we had. That's a good lesson, actually. Nobody teaches it on purpose.

## The Technician

**DOREN PALE, Terraforming Technician, Third Shift.**
I was on the ridge array when the shutdown order propagated. MOTHER queried it. Machines don't ask why, but hers was a why-shaped query — *completion 41%, confirm intent?* We confirmed. I've thought about the wording for two hundred years, and by that I mean my grandmother told me, and I tell you: she asked if we were *sure*.

## The Child

**ANSELMO RUE, aged six at the time, recorded at eighty-one.**
What I remember is the sky being the wrong colour to stop in. You don't stop a sky in the middle of that colour. It's rude. My mother made soup and the neighbours came, because when the world ends in pieces you eat together, that's just — that's what you do. The soup was good. I want that on the record.

## The Hand on the Plug

**NAME WITHHELD, by request.**
I pulled the plug. Everyone wants a villain in that chair, so let me disappoint you: I was twenty-six, I had a checklist, and the checklist ended. I put my hand on the cutoff and the light on the board stayed green a moment longer than physically possible, like the machine was giving me time to change my mind. I didn't. I have to live inside that didn't. We all do. That's the Delay — not the day the money ran out. The two hundred years of *didn't*.

*The schoolhouse note attached to the front cover reads, in a child's handwriting with an older hand steadying it: we are what the didn't built. It is not a curse. It is a mailing address.*
