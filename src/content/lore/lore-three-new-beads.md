*Transcribed off the water-tower antenna by the usual volunteers, with two cups of tea stood on the receiver to stop it drifting. For context: two hundred and eleven lamps had hung lit on the terraform Strand since before the Delay was a Deliberation; the old survey chart shows four hundred and four planned. On this night, an hour after dusk, three more came on — all at once, unannounced, where the lit arc used to stop. Marlowe went straight to open line. — transcription circle*

## Cold open

**MARLOWE:** Good evening, flats. Look up. I'll wait.

*[Eight seconds of broadcast silence, which the circle notes is four seconds longer than station policy and exactly as long as it needs to be.]*

**MARLOWE:** For those indoors: the Strand is wearing two hundred and fourteen beads tonight. Not two hundred and eleven. The sky has changed count. I have checked three times and once with the harbour glasses, and the new lights are steady, amber-white, spaced like they were always meant to be there — which, the survey office tells me breathlessly down a second line, they were. Open line. Somebody tell me what we do now.

## The callers

**CALLER, Saltmouth, Guild register by the sound of the abacus:** You do *nothing*, is what you do, until somebody establishes who *insures* a mirror that was not lit yesterday. Flats, I have children. The children have an actuarial horizon.

**CALLER, Cinderflats, definitely a Reclaimer:** Salvage law, sister. First touch. If the sky's fixing itself, I claim the third one for the Yards, sight unseen, in the spirit of —

**MARLOWE:** The sky can hear you, love.

**CALLER, Cinderflats:** I hope it *can*. Bead three. Yards. Dibs.

*[Here a caller from Lamp Rest comes on the line uninvited — the circle is certain of the register — and, without preamble, begins to sing the Held Breath, the long slow note the Choir keeps for the dark gap east of the Strand, the one parents hum to children instead of lullabies with words. Marlowe does not cut it. Nobody cuts it. It runs forty-one seconds, and the volunteers note that all three new beads held steady for the whole of it, and that the harbour rigging went quiet like a congregation.]*

**MARLOWE:** *[gently]* Open line stays open.

## The naming

**CALLER, age unclear, young, up past bedtime:** Are they *lost*? The other lamps. Or were they just off?

**MARLOWE:** Flats, that is the best question asked on this station in years. Off, the survey office says. Not lost. Waiting to be finished.

**CALLER, young:** Then the new ones should have *names*. Like bikes get.

*[The circle notes seventeen seconds of suggestions arriving faster than the switchboard could buffer. Ruling, by Marlowe, at the top of the hour:]*

**MARLOWE:** Bead one, by acclamation: **FINISHED** — because the flats like a promise kept. Bead two, with apologies to the Yards and none to the abacus: **DIBS** — you know who you are, and the Choir is still laughing. And bead three — the last caller of the night was a garage-keeper who would not give his name and does not need to: *"Every bike I've ever fixed had a name, and every one of them was somebody's old favourite."* So the third bead is **OLD FAVOURITE**, flats — which means if you're out there under it tonight on a second-hand machine with a second-hand name, the sky has just taken yours, with thanks, and it will give it back every clear night from now on.

That's the hour. New sky over old water, and both of them holding. Weather at eleven, kindness at all times. Marlowe out.

*[Circle note, appended: the three beads lit again at dusk the next night, and every night since. The Guild has opened a file. The Yards have opened a betting book. The Choir has opened nothing; they say it was already open, out there where the breath is held.]*
