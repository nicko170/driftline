*Posted on the Saltmouth water tower, weather-side face, under glass, behind another layer of glass. Reading time: one thirst. — Ledger Office*

## Article one: what water is

Water on Kessa-9 is not weather. Weather is a rumour with ambitions. Water is *counted moisture*, and counted moisture has an owner, and the owner, by the grace of arithmetic, is the Salt Guild. This is not greed. Greed implies appetite. This is *custody*.

## Article two: condenser law

Every condenser, dew-trap, and fog-sail on the flats is registered, and its nightly yield enters the ledger at dawn. Unregistered condensation — moisture that forms on private surfaces — remains the property of the surface, which is why the Yards run their crushers at night for the sweat, and why the Guild chooses, publicly and annually, not to notice. Choose not to notice your neighbour's small water: it is the cheapest kindness and the dearest, and the Guild prices it at zero, *which is a gift.*

## Article three: the tower shifts

Saltmouth's tower keeps four shifts — dawn draw, market draw, dusk draw, and the **night shift**, which draws nothing and is therefore the most important: somebody must sit with the water while nobody is watching it, or the debt between a town and its thirst goes unwitnessed. Night shift is a position of honour. Applicants must be able to stay awake, and to explain, on request, why. Both skill sets are rarer than rain.

## Article four: fair price

The fair price of water is the price at which the seller can sleep and the buyer can walk away unbending. Any price that requires a eulogy at either end is theft wearing a ledger. The Guild has tested this definition in arbitration, twice, and won, because we wrote it honestly the first time.

## Article five: the moorage exception

Keel Town's moorage crews — the people who hold skyships against the wind with their hands while the winches think — are paid one shift in ten *in stories*: a witnessed story, told to a clerk, entered in the ledger under DIVIDEND: NARRATIVE. Inspections have asked why. Because a winch crew that has been *heard* holds a hull eighteen percent longer in gusts — we measured — and because when the money ran out two hundred years ago, stories were the only currency left circulating, and the Guild does not default on old instruments.

Count your water twice. Drink it once. Share the rest of the counting.
