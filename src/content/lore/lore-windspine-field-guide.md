*Driftline Training Annex 9 — Windspine appendix. Transcribed from the survey camp's communal notebook, which is chained to the table because of the wind, not the company.*

## The ridge and its manners

**The ridge makes weather.** Understand this first. Windspine is not a place that *has* storms; it is the machine that *manufactures* them. Air comes up the southern foot warm and lazy, hits the crest, and gets ideas. Everything else in this appendix follows from that single fact.

**Turbine etiquette.** The turbines are older than the Guild, the Choir, and most of the rocks. Rule one: park downwind of a nacelle, never under, unless you enjoy replacing fairings. Rule two: a blade turning slow is working; a blade turning fast is working harder; a blade that has *stopped* in wind is thinking about something, and you should leave before it finishes. Rule three: the hum is normal. The hum going quiet mid-gust is the only alarm on the ridge with no override.

**Tap the tower before dawn.** Climbers and surveyors strike each pylon twice with a wrench at first light and listen. A clear ring means the struts are tight. A flat note means a bolt walked out in the night — the ridge shakes loose one to four bolts per storm, always the ones you'd least like to lose. Nobody remembers who started the tapping. Whoever it was is owed more lives than the Guild has counted.

## Duties at dawn

**Storm-gauge duty.** The gauge at the far crest is a brass drum, four anemometers, and a telegraph key wired straight to Saltmouth. Somebody reads it at dawn and dusk, every day, storm season or not. If nobody has read it, the nearest courier inherits the job. Yes, even if you're late. *Especially* if you're late — the gauge log is how Saltmouth knows the difference between a bad hour and a bad week.

**What the dawns tell you.** Rose sky, ordinary day. Copper sky, keep the run short. A sky the colour of a bruise over the far crest means the season is writing your name on something. Cancel the job. The board will be unkind; the storm will be worse, and it does not accept apologies.

**Parting note, unsigned:** The ridge does not hate you. It is simply busy, and you are made of exactly the kind of silence it spends all day noisily replacing. — *K.*
