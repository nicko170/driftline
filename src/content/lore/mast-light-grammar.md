*Driftline Training Annex 6 — night riding supplement. Mast lights are not decoration. They are the desert's correspondence, sent slow because nothing urgent survives the dark. Learn to read. Learn to be read. Your headlight is a pen; write carefully.*

## The letters

Masts speak in four letters; everything else is style.

**STEADY AMBER** — *anchored and open.* Wind ordinary, ground held, shelter berths probably available. The most beautiful sentence in the language.

**DOUBLE BLINK, then hold** — *company ahead.* Another rider in the mast's shadow, lantern-lit, gossip pending. Ease off your approach; a mast that announces you announces *to* you.

**SLOW PULSE, long and low** — *wind coming up.* The mast breathes before the ridge does. Check gauge reports. Check the goat, if the goat is local. Make peace with your schedule.

**FAST, THREE TIMES, then nothing** — *warned.* Not danger yet — *warning*. Enter its light at riding speed, not hero speed; the mast has put the kettle on and is annoyed at why.

## Greeting versus warning

A greeting mast brightens as you cross into its ring: *come in, I see you*. A warning mast dims — one breath, two — like someone lowering their voice in a doorway: *I see you, and I see what's behind you.* Same lamp, opposite hospitality. Night riders say the difference is obvious at two hundred metres, at which point you are already in the sentence and should finish it politely.

## Your own light, an involuntary literature

The spotlight on your nose cone is a word in this grammar whether you like it or not. **Swept slow left-right at approach:** *friendly, arriving.* **Held high:** *clear passage, intending.* **Cut dark well outside a ring:** *sleeping town — do not wake, do not prove anything.* Flashing twice at a mast that greeted you is *thank you*; there is no word for *you're welcome* because there has never needed to be.

## The pattern nobody teaches

Riders in the far pans report — separately, reluctantly, always in the tone of people returning a borrowed umbrella — a fifth letter: a mast that goes dark *in sequence*, one ring at a time, as if counting you past. No blinks. No pulse. Just a slow, exact dimming that walks the array like a fingertip along a shelf. The beacon manual does not cover it. The long-static archive lists the accompanying band as *unmonitored*, which everyone understands to mean *his*. The Drowned Array crews call it *the stamping* and say it is the sound of mail being resolved, helmet-side. Ask TALLY-9 and you'll get a ticket opened under your name, classification *transient*, priority *adequate*.

Rule, unnumbered, the one the annex makes you repeat back: **a pattern you cannot name is still a pattern.** Dim your lamp, match the slow count, and be — in the grammar's only imperative — *readable*.
