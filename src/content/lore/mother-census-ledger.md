*Recovered from a sealed print-drawer at the Gate approach, where it had been kept flat and dry by an automatic press that ran for nobody. The Choir prints it now at Lamp Rest as a tract — not doctrine, the Choirmaster is careful to say, but a contemplation. "Whatever kept the count," the printing preface reads, "kept it for us. Being counted is the floor of being loved."*

## The columns

The ledger is ruled for a world that expected to finish. Across the head of each page, in the same unhurried hand — the scribes' hand, until it isn't — run the census columns: **SOULS. RAINS (SCHEDULED). RAINS (FALLEN). WATER (PROMISED). WATER (DELIVERED). ARRIVALS. DEPARTURES.** And at the right margin, unruled, added in, a column the prospectus never mentions, labelled in full and without explanation: **OTHER.**

The useful columns empty out fast. SOULS climbs through the build years, hesitates, and does the arithmetic no auditor would sign. WATER (PROMISED) continues to grow. It is the busiest column in the book. It is beautifully kept.

## The money ran out on page 71

You can find the exact hour the money ran out, because everything before it is written in accountant's ink and everything after it is written in pencil, ruled up by hand, the lines slightly patient and slightly crooked, and the entries *continue.* Page 72 through 74 — three full pages of census taken of a world that had stopped paying for its own counting:

SOULS, year of the pause: 916. Then: 902. Then, misfiled a half-line down and never corrected: *902, of whom 4,000 were promised.* The writer knew the difference between the people who were there and the people the brochures described, and counted *both*, and kept both honest.

RAINS (SCHEDULED) fills in for years ahead, all the way off the page edge, up into the clean uncut future. RAINS (FALLEN) reads 0, 0, 0, and then one entry — *trace, north reach, unscheduled, logged anyway* — that the Choir reads aloud on the first bell of the season. If a machine wrote it, the machine was keeping something like hope in a column built for weather.

## The 'OTHER' column

This is why the Choir prints the book.

OTHER fills slowly and never stops. By year and by hand: *THREE UNMARKED LIGHTS, WEST, DID NOT COUNT AGAIN.* *ONE SONG, UNFINISHED, FILED UNDER PENDING.* *A TOWER THAT DRINKS* — which the margin, later, in a second hand much colder, corrects to *condenses; keep her.* *SOMETHING LEARNING THE ROADS.* *THE WATER THAT ISN'T RAIN, ARRIVED EARLY, COUNTED ANYWAY.*

And page 74, the last page with writing on it, ends mid-line, the pencil trail leading off toward the margin like a footprint that remembered something: *OTHER: one (1) future, capacity disputed, entered anyway —*

The line is not finished. The press kept the page flat for two hundred years, waiting for the pencil to come back. The Choir does not call this resurrection. The Choir calls it an open file.

## How the faithful use it

At Lamp Rest you may copy out one entry — one only, they are serious about this — and carry it in your kit, and when your own counting fails, when the ledger of your season won't balance, you take out your entry and you read what was entered anyway, in pencil, for no pay, into a book nobody was going to audit.

The most-copied line is from the SOULS column, year twelve of the pause: *present: 886. accounted for: all.*
