*Taken down at a relay fire from Ook, dusk-farmer at the boundary shadow, who does not write things down because paper is loud hours thinking. He allowed the writing down on condition that nobody grow from a book. "A book has no shadow," he said, "and neither does a fool." — Transcribed by a courier who stayed past dark on purpose.*

## What it is

Night-mirror lichen. Pale green, low to the stone, feeds on starlight and glass dust and patience, and the third one is why the colonies failed and the farmers didn't. It does not make light. Understand that first. It *returns* light — takes what touches it, waits, and hands it back. A cairn smeared with mirror crop answers your headlamp from a klick out, like it was glad of you. It is the only thing on Kessa-9 that reliably is.

## Sowing by shadow

Sun people sow by calendar. Out here the sun is unreliable, so you sow by shadow: wait for the season when the boundary cliffs throw their long evening line, and set the spores where the shadow of your own hat brim falls at the second hour of full dark. The lichen roots where shadow *grows*. Seed in shrinking shadow and you have planted a shortage.

Wet the stone first with brine-weak water — the lichen reads salt as an invitation, being homesick for seas it never saw. Cover with shade-cloth. Do not look at it for nine days. It can tell. Everything out here can tell.

## Harvesting by moonrise

Cut in the hour after moonrise, the hour the crop is fullest of what it drank. Cut earlier and it sulks — a sulking row goes dark for a season and holds a grudge the way wet ground holds a wheel-print. Cut later and it keeps the light for itself, which is its right, and you may stand in the dark and think about your timing.

Cut with a bone blade. Metal startles it. The glow you bring home in the tubs is *borrowed*; it spends down over weeks, and when a tub gutters out you do not curse it, you thank it and plant the lees at the row's end. Full circle. It remembers a circle. It forgives a sale.

## What the glow is good for

Cairn-marking, mostly. A mirror cairn turns your own lamp back at you, which means the marker shows brighter the closer you are to lost — which is exactly when you need it, and exactly what a plain lamp can't do. Second: wake-reading. Smear a strip on your own tail fin and at night you can see your wake, how wide you throw, how honest your line is. The crop does not lie. Third: the shelter-line lamps at the Gate approach run on it and have never once been relit, which is not a miracle, it is maintenance performed by weather.

What it is not for: food, fever, fortune-telling. People ask. The crop is a mirror. It returns what you bring, including the question.

## The standing rule

Never sell a mirror crop to someone in a hurry.

Not cruelty. Practical. A person in a hurry is being followed, or following, and either way they are about to learn that a mirror shows you what is behind you. Set a hurrying rider on a bike whose tail flashes back its own wake, with ten klicks of mirror-marked cairns behind them lighting up like witnesses, and you have not sold them a lamp. You have sold them an *audience*.

Hurried handling kills the crop besides. It bruises black where a rushed hand grips it, and black mirror returns nothing, which is the closest thing to grief a plant on this desert manages. If a customer can't slow down for three sentences of soil talk, send them to the Guild. The Guild sells things that don't care. Mine do.

## The unplanted row

North row grows what I didn't sow. Wrong green — more like the Hollow lamps than like crop. It grows toward the Gate at a handspan a day and the rows lean with it like bread dough toward the oven. Garden rule says: I decide what grows. The row disagrees. We are negotiating. Whatever is gardening in her name plants without asking, and it plants *patiently*, and a farmer knows that handwriting.

Don't buy it. Don't touch it. It wasn't sold. It was *planted*.
