*Annotations found in a courier's copy of* Night Lines: A Dark-Hours Riding Guide, *left as collateral at the Saltmouth exchange and never reclaimed. The courier's tab was settled in full by persons unknown; the book stayed. The guild clerk filed it, read it twice, and began riding nights. Notes transcribed in order of page, in the courier's hand — quick, sloped, written by lamp.*

---

## On "the desert is honest after sunset"

Honest like a card sharp who tells you the deck is clean. True and insufficient. The desert at night doesn't lie about where things *are*. Lies about how far, how fast, and whether it's pleased to see you. Trust lamps, not distances. A lamp is a promise somebody has to keep refilling.

## On the second stillness — pages 4–5, underlined twice

Twenty minutes, they say. I've counted eleven. I've counted forty. The stillness lasts exactly as long as you need minus what you deserve. Do your crossings in it and thank nothing out loud.

Addendum, different night: the stillness has a sound. It's the sound of everything deciding.

## On the headlight

The guide says nothing about the light itself, so I will. The headlight at night is not for seeing. The flats give you starlight enough once your eyes cash the cheque. The light is for *being legible in return*. The desert reads you back: cold glass throws it at the sky in sheets, couriers' beacons answer it, ridge keepers log it. You are writing with that beam whether you mean to or, so write something boring. Boring riders get old.

What reads the light besides keepers, in order of frequency: night haulers (want to race), beacon tenders (want to chat), dust-devils the size of buildings (want nothing; take it anyway), and once — once — something pacing me out on the far salt that matched my line for an hour, lamp-free, and left me at the Relay 9 turnoff with my nerves arranged like luggage. The guide calls her Sable. The margin is where we keep the truth: I called her *ma'am*, out loud, in an empty desert, and I've done it ever since.

## On the stars that keep their promises

The Hook is real. The Water Load still rises over the Drowned Array like an apology that keeps arriving. But the Broken Coin owes me a note: it *swims* on still nights, not just in fog. If the Coin swims and no fog follows by second hour, the fog isn't brewing — something high is moving in the wind that hasn't arrived yet. Ride your planned line, not your fastest one. The coin is never wrong about what; it's just occasionally rude about when.

## On rule 4 (the lullaby)

The guide says it's fine. It is fine. But write down the tune anyway. The Choir pays real credit for verses of Channel 0.4 nobody's logged, and the dead channel keeps a larger library than the codex does. I've sold them three. I hum one back at the radio on long dark flats, and whatever it is, it keeps better time than my engine.

*Last page, last note, no guide text beneath it:* Night riding is the only time the desert lets you feel how big the unfinished parts are. Don't fill them up with noise. You're a guest in a house that was never roofed. Mind the furniture. Leave the lamps better than you found them.
