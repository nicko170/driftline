## The Violet Hour

Ride out at sundown and watch the flats change hands. Daylight holds the desert like a landlord; at dusk the colour drains out of the salt and the sky fills violet to the zenith, and for about forty minutes the whole world is neither managed nor pretending. Experienced riders schedule around the violet hour the way kitchens schedule around the soup. New riders catch it by accident and pull over, which is correct.

## Cold-Snap Handling

Cold glass is honest glass. As the chill sets, your hover field grabs the flats a stride firmer, braking comes a half-beat sooner, and drift exits bite instead of glide. The first cold-snap of the night arrives fast — riders call it *the floor remembering*. Trim your hover height down one notch at full dark or the first frost-flat will trim it for you, loudly, through the seat.

Boost behaves in the cold, all right. Boost *behaves*. Watch the heat gauge instead: engines drink harder after dark and a tired horizon cell will lie to you warmly.

## Trust Teal

Navigation after dark is mostly lamps, and lamps have politics. Trust **teal**: Guild beacons, relay lamps, the chapel arch on the glassroad — teal light is posted light, maintained by somebody with their name on it. Trust **amber** that's bolted down: mooring lights, ridge markers.

Do not follow **amber that moves wrong**. Mirage-lamps, skiff-kites, scav smudge-fires mocking a convoy pace — all of it swings a little too slow, bobs a little too high. Rule of the night routes: posted light doesn't *sway*. If the lamp you're aiming at dips when you brake, you were aiming at a bait fire, and somewhere behind you somebody has started their engine.

## Beacon Etiquette

One short horn at a lit beacon means *passing through*. Two means *coming in cold*. A long one means *I'm out of ideas* and it is everybody's business — night riders answer the long horn even at a loss, even Reclaimers do, and that is the closest thing the desert has to scripture.

Never put your lights out near the Hollow. The crater keeps count, the lamps answer back, and the Choir has opinions entitled *harmony*.

And if something out in the dark hums in the same key as your engine: ride your own line home. It doesn't want your cargo. It's practising.
