## Why ride at night at all

Because the desert lies by day. Heat mirage, glare off the salt, horizon that wobbles like a bad witness — daylight is full of opinions. After sunset the flats go quiet and *flat*, and what you see is what is there. Distance means distance. A lamp is a lamp. Experienced night riders describe it as riding inside an agreement.

Also: the Guild pays a night premium on supply-window contracts, because nobody likes the dark and the water doesn't care.

## The second stillness

Learn the desert's daily trick. At dusk the wind dies — completely, suspiciously, for about twenty minutes. Old hands call it the second stillness (the first is at dawn, and nobody has a good name for either; the desert declines to answer). Then the cold wind comes down off the ridge line, steady from the west, and holds till morning. Plan your crossings for the stillness. Plan your speed for what follows.

## Star navigation, for people who forgot their compass

Storm season scrambles radios and compasses both, so learn three stars and stop apologising:

- **The Hook.** High, hooked asterism, points west the way a shopkeeper points at the till. Find it, you've found Windspine.
- **The Water Load.** Two bright stars with a smear between them — the old crews swore the smear was the ocean they were owed. It rises over the Drowned Array and keeps rising, which is more than the ocean managed.
- **The Broken Coin.** Sits fixed-ish over the south masts. The skydocks keep it lit with reflections in season. If the Coin is blurred, fog is brewing on the flats; ride high or ride home.

The Choir's lamps at the Hollow are teal and arranged in a ring; visible at thirty klicks, and the only landmark that *meant* to be one. Thank them silently. Loud gratitude is proselytising-adjacent (see: *Choir Hollow: A Visitor's Manners*).

## Who else is out there

Night supply pilots, Guild-beaconed, predictable, will race you and deny it. Beacon tenders on their rounds, wreathed in spare lamp-glass, always stopping exactly where you needed someone. And **Sable**.

Sable delivers nothing you ordered and everything you needed. Sable's bike has no running lights and makes no wake, which are both possible and neither is legal. Stranded riders report being paced for an hour through bad dark and left, unbilled, at shelter. The Driftline roster has no Sable. The relay fires have plenty. If a silent rider matches your line some night, don't chase, don't hail, just follow at a distance and be worth rescuing. Some of the creed isn't written down because it's still out there doing rounds.

## The rules survive translation

1. Lit beacons mean tended ground. Dark beacons mean yesterday's ground. Ride for yesterday's ground only if you liked being born.
2. Slow in the dips. Night cold pools there like water pretending.
3. The weather desk closes at sundown. Pemmy sleeps. Anyone forecasting after dark is guessing, including you.
4. If your radio hums a lullaby, that's Channel 0.4 and it's fine. If it hums *your name backwards*, that is storm-static being a comedian. Check the sky behind you.

Night is not the desert being kind. It's the desert being legible. Learn to read it and you'll wonder how anyone rides blind in the day.
