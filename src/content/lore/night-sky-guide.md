*Print of the flat-chart nailed in Ketch's garage, beside the tea tin. His note on the margin: "Learn three lights and you can come home from anywhere. Learn the rest and you'll want to." Day riders don't need this card. Everyone night-rides eventually. Storms don't check the clock and neither does debt.*

## The Strand

Look up on a clear dark and the sky is *cut* — a shallow arc of lights, evenly spaced, rising out of the west and stopping, dead stop, a handspan short of the zenith. That's the terraform strand: the weather mirrors MOTHER was hanging when the money and then the mind ran out. Two hundred and eleven lamps lit. The chart in the old survey office shows four hundred and four.

The unlit stretch is there too. You can fly it sometimes — your eye fills in where the lamps *should* continue, the way your tongue finds a missing tooth. The Choir calls the lit half *the promise* and the dark half *the patience*, and for once the Guild's ledger agrees with somebody's scripture: the manifest for the unhung mirrors exists. It has been audited. Everything crosses, apparently, except the sky.

Rider's use: the Strand runs south-southwest to zenith. In the flats with no landmark and your compass sulking, line your bike under it. The lit end points home-ward of Saltmouth by a comfortable lie of about four degrees.

## The faithful

The moving ones. Old orbit-keepers from the planting years, still on their rounds two centuries past their funerals — the same breed of stubborn your horizon cell was salvaged from, which is why Ketch calls your battery *a descendant.* Learn three:

**Long Guile** — steady amber, crosses in nine minutes, never varies, the metronome of the sober. **Pair-of-Sleeves** — two lights in harness, tumbling; if you've lost the east, wait for them. **The Deacon** — blinks once a minute, slow as a name being remembered. The Choir stands quiet for the Deacon when it crosses, and the relay band goes courteous for its whole transit. Nobody knows when that started. Ketch says it started the first time somebody outrode a wall by its light, and nobody wanted to be rude to the candle.

## The Breath Between Stars

East of the strand there's a gap — a palm-sized dark where nothing shines and nothing crosses, blacker than the space around it, like the sky leaning in to listen. The Choir's name for it is the good one: the Breath Between Stars, held for two hundred years, not yet let out.

Static-Warden Pem logs it as a dust lane, camera-shy. Pem also logs that the gap *breathes* once a year, on the night of the first big storm — dims and returns, all in one watch — and files that under weather, the drawer with the fewest consequences.

Rider's rule, older than the filing: if you're out at night and the gap seems closer than the horizon, you are not lost. The desert is just holding its breath to let you hear your own engine. Sing if you like. Commit if you sing. The sky up there is half-built, half-lit, and still keeping time — which makes it, Ketch says, closing the garage, the most honest road on this world.
