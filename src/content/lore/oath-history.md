*Read to new couriers at their first relay fire, in whatever order the room decides. The reader holds the lamp while reading, which is why the names get slower toward the end. — Driftline Training Annex 1, of which this is the whole document; the bike is Annexes 2 through 40.*

## Who wrote it

Seven words: *any crate, any storm, any door.* The Guild claims a factor dictated them to the first courier as terms of employment, and produces a ledger page that has been carbon-dated to *embarrassing.* The Choir claims a listener heard them in the static before any courier rode, which would make the creed a *delivery the desert made to us*, and honestly the Choir's version pays best as a story. The line's own tradition, third-hand and counting, says a woman wrote them in paint on the inside of a shelter during the second year of the Delay, when everything else on the route had failed. Her name is contested by three bars and one thesis. The paint is real. The handwriting is mostly doors.

The short answer, which riders give new couriers so they can get on with the riding: *nobody wrote it. It arrived. Somebody has to carry tomorrow.*

## Who broke it (the short list)

**Coulter Wenn,** year 31 — sold a manifest to storm-season raiders, delivered nothing he was paid to, and was struck from the numbers. Note the order: the line stopped answering him *before* the raiders did. Last seen riding east with no name on the relay band. East has no relay band.

**The Selby pair,** year 58 — opened the mail. Not for loot; for *news*, they said, in a bad season when news was short and hunger shorter, and opened letters told three towns what two other towns were saying about them. What followed took a year off the basin's life. The pair served it back, every day of it, and their names are read at fires with the honest footnote: *broke it once, kept it after.* The list of breakers is short because the line forgives restorers in full and by name.

**[redacted],** this season — asked the crate to hum *for them, specifically.* It did. The subsequent events are under review by everyone. The name stays redacted until the humming stops. The line is patient, not careless.

## Who died keeping it

The reader slows down here. The lamp is passed hand to hand down the list.

Sel Ondray, who held the Slick Mile line in a storm so a wedd— *versions differ; the point stands.* Rill Davenant's mother, who ran fever-salts through a laid-down day before the days were called that, and whose route number the Windspine crossings still wear. Nine riders in the year the Glassroad burned, who carried *people*, against manifest, under codicil one — the codicil was unratified then; it wasn't that night. And Davenant, whose name the fire holds last and quietest, because the line does not say *died* about a rider the desert hasn't given back. It says *gone quiet,* and per codicil four, unratified and universal:

*The line goes looking. All of it. Still looking.*

New couriers answer here, by rote and then by heart: **the desert decides what crosses. We decide it arrives.**
