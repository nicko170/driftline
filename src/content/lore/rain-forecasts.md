## The ledger view

**DESK:** Tonight's modest enquiry, sent in by, conservatively, everyone: *what if it rained?* Not the last shower of year twelve, the one that waved goodbye. Real rain. Returning rain. The gardener-wakes-up-and-finishes-the-garden kind. Economist first.

**ECONOMIST (Guild desk, name withheld per trade agreements):** Catastrophic. Wonderful. Both, in that order, for about nine fiscal years. Salt is what we *are*, and rain dissolves our inventory, our road surfaces, and three of our five natural jokes. The glass flats soften. The mooring tariffs treble because hulls rust and, my friends, rust is just rain keeping its promises. And then — and here I depart from the posted position of my own desk, at some personal cost — everything else gets *cheaper*. Water stops being a charter and becomes a *climate*. Crops children have only seen pickled grow standing up. A courier's horizon cell lasts a season longer because the storms lose their tempers. I ran the model. The model says we are ruined, and beneath the ruin, in smaller print, it says *richer than anyone's grandmother*. Make of that what your ledger permits.

## The lamp-line view

**DESK:** Theologian.

**THEOLOGIAN (Hollow of the First Note):** We keep a door in the hills that an inscription calls a plan. Behind it the gardener sleeps, and the question is not whether she *can* bring the rain but whether anyone may *ask*. I will say the unfashionable thing in both directions. To my own: waking her is not worship, it is a favour asked of a neighbour, and neighbours forgive what worshippers never could. To everyone else: letting her sleep is not cowardice. The desert we have is not the failure of the desert we were promised. Two hundred years of children have been *born into this one*, sand-mad and salt-rich and kind. If she sleeps, we are not the unfinished world. We are the finished people of an unfinished world, and there is no shame audited anywhere in that. Either way the inscription is obeyed: we carry what we cannot count.

## The poem, and the price of the dry world

**DESK:** Note for listeners: the Choir panellist has, characteristically, blessed both columns of the budget. Poet. Last word.

**POET (Keel Town, unrepentant):** I have nothing to add about rain arriving that the old accounts didn't say: it rang the hulls like a choir with no choirmaster and everyone stood in doorways being paid to do nothing. So let me price the other future, the dry one, the one where we keep the door shut and the desert keeps us. In that world the rain stays a story we tell so well it still waters something. Children draw it in blue they mix from glass dust. The couriers still cross. The door stays a door instead of becoming a wound we named *weather*. I have stood at the lamp-line in storm season and watched the whole horizon walk toward us like a debtor, and I thought: we chose this. Every evening we choose it again by lighting the lamps.

Rain, if it comes, will find us ready and embarrassed, like guests arriving during an argument. If it never comes, it will find us the way it left us: waving.

**DESK:** The Long View thanks the panels of both futures. Markets close unchanged. The lamps are lit on schedule. That's the Standard. Good night, Keel Town. Drink your water — whatever it's planning.
