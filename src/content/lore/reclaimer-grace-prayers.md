*Transcribed at the Cinderflats Yards mess, Local 3, with the crews' permission, which was given on the condition that we write down the funny ones and the true ones in that order. A reclaimer grace is said standing, hats on (yard dust does not observe religion), before the evening meal on any day a crew cuts decommissioned works. Aunt Vertex presides when present. When absent she is considered present, which the crews describe as "the comfortable option."*

## The standard grace (short form)

*Forgive us, old works, for the taking. You stood longer than your makers and quieter than your keepers. What we strip, we honour in the using. What we break, we own. What we owe — put it on the Guild's tab, they'll bill the desert.*

Said fast, over food going cold. The last line is newer than the rest and is recited with particular care on days when a Guild factor is eating with the crew, which is often, and with particular volume when one isn't.

## The long apology (cutting day)

Before torch meets hull on anything that ever moved under its own power — a hauler, a lattice crane, once memorably a ferry — the crew repeats the machine's service record aloud, names included where the plate still shows names, and then:

*You carried. Now we carry you. Rest is not surrender; it's the longest shift, finally clocked. If there's a ghost in you, the kettle's on and the ghosts of this yard eat free — that was true while you worked and it's true tonight.*

Kettles are, in fact, kept on during long cuts. Boss Pyke calls this morale. Aunt Vertex calls it *hospitality with a fire permit.* Solder calls it nothing, but refills the kettle.

## The torch-holder's absolution

Spoken by the crew *to* whoever holds the torch, before the first cut, in call and response:

*Whose hand is on the torch?* — **Mine, and trembling honestly.**
*Who chose the cut?* — **The crew chose; the machine allowed; the yard will judge.**
*And if it's the wrong cut?* — **Then it's on all of us in the log, and on Mags in the song, forever, amen.**

The last line names Mags Delver because Mags wrote the odes and therefore controls posterity. Crews maintain that blame shared per the log is paperwork, but blame immortalised in verse is *ornamental*, and the distinction has settled more disputes than the Yard Rules.

## Grace after a bad cut

When a charge goes early, a strut falls wrong, or a thing that was logged dead hums — the meal is eaten in silence except for one line, said by whoever stood closest:

*We heard you. We're sorry. We're still eating. Teach us slower tomorrow.*

The crews insist this is not fear. It is *manners toward the flammable dead*, which they admit is a fear-shaped manners. The Choir calls the whole liturgy heresy in the shape of a family dinner, and sends soup. The soup is accepted, per the comfortable option.
