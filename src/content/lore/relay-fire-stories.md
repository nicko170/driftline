*Collected over nine storm seasons at nine relay fires. Methodology: I sat, I listened, I wrote, I bought the next stampede of water. Annotations in two hands. The tidy hand belongs to Sister Counterweight, who does not believe in ghosts and logs them anyway. The other hand is Ketch's. — Sera Vann, keeper (ret.), relayline*

---

## 1. The Pale Courier

A rider in unpainted salt-white comes up behind you on the long flats at dusk, matches your speed exactly, and will not pass. If you look back, the flats are empty and your mirrors hold nothing but your own face a half-second late. The story says the Pale Courier is the ghost of the first courier, still making the first delivery. The story says if you let it draft behind you until the next cairn, your boost will run cool for a season.

*True,* writes Old Bahro, who let it draft and reports a season of suspicious mechanical fortune. *Nonsense,* writes Ketch. *"Boost runs cool if you maintain the vanes. Maintain the vanes. Also don't look back, it's bad posture."*

## 2. The crate that delivers itself

Any courier can tell this one now: a sealed crate, no sender, no manifest, freight prepaid in a currency nobody mints. Left at the depot before first bell. The story says if you open it, the desert charges you interest. The story says it hums on storm nights in a key the Choir's horns recognise.

The newer tellings are worse, because they're specific. A crate matching the description rode a second-hand bike out of Saltmouth with a courier named Varga, and half the factions on the map suddenly remembered wanting it.

*True,* writes Sister Counterweight — about the crate, not the ghost part. *Nonsense,* writes Ketch, which the keeper notes he wrote three times, once underlined.

## 3. The beacon that answers

Out past the last wayline there is said to be a relay lamp that lights itself when a rider is going to be lost, and if you cross it it speaks — one word, in static, your name. Salt Singer Ila says she knows a courier who heard it, turned back, and lived. Quicklime Kid says they *are* that courier and charges two credits to elaborate.

*True,* writes Ila, and adds a bar of music. *Nonsense,* writes Ketch. Then, smaller: *"If it says your name, turn back anyway. Cheaper than a funeral."*

The keeper's own note, final page, no annotation after it: *Every relay keeps an empty chair at the fire. Nobody remembers when that started. Nobody is in a hurry to stop.*
