*Copied off the inside of the Local 9 mess-hall door, where it is painted in rust-red and amended in chalk more often than the tariff board. The pulling song "Sparks" is the noon song; the graces are for dinner; this is for first light, the morning a crew stands in front of a dead thing and decides, together, to begin. Aunt Vertex says a reclamation dawn is the only service the Union has that starts with an outcome. "We're going to take it apart," she says. "The least we can do is say good morning first."*

## The waking calls

Three calls before the sun clears the ridge, sung to the yard, not the machine — this distinction is insisted upon.

**Call one, the yard-answer:** *"Who's upright?"* — answered by name, every member, no shortcuts, including apprentices and the kettle, which is answered for. A name sung is a person accounted; the day cannot be lost more than one at a time.

**Call two, the footing count:** *"What's under us?"* — *"Slab, sand, slag, and the patience of both."* Sung facing down. Vertex says you thank the ground before you ask it to hold a fell thing. Solder says it's so everyone looks at their boots once, on purpose. Both chalkings stand.

**Call three, the door knock:** struck on the derelict's casing with a knuckle-wrapped wrench, never sung. Metal gets the morning word *spoken*. Song is kept for people until the machine's status is known: dead, retired, or — the third ledger column, rarely used — *occupied*.

## The working measures

Through the morning, the soft chants. **The haul-measure**, four lines, paces the cable teams; it is deliberately dull, because hauling is dull and dull is alive. **The look-out's round**, one voice, rotates the watch: *"I see the sky, I see the pan, I see my crew, I'll see them again."* The measures may be re-worded by any crew, any season, except for the **keeps** — one line per chant, underlined in red on the mess-hall door, which may never be altered: *held the line for a living crew* (haul), *see them again* (look-out), and the door knock's spoken *saying good morning, no more*.

Vertex on the keeps: "Songs wear. Keeps don't. The day the keeps wear out is the day we find out what they were holding shut, and I'd rather die curious." Pyke's chalk beside it: *she won't.*

## The long rule

Never — this is the only *never* the cycle contains — never sing the door knock's greeting *in full*, all three calls in order with names, over a machine that still hums. Humming indicates charge. Charge indicates a listener. The full greeting is a *waking*, and the Union does not wake what it cannot feed, house, or answer to.

It is not fear. It is rostering. A woken engine goes in the duty book, draws a keeper, gets a name, ruins the schedule for a year. The yard book records four full greetings given in error across ninety-one years: two pump rigs, a lattice crane, and a relay mast that answered in *three-part harmony* before the second call was finished. That crew went home. That entry is initialed V and underlined twice.

So: knock once, speak the short word, listen the full count of nine. If the thing hums back in the same key as your kettle — and kettles, per the appended note, are tuned flat for exactly this reason — you close the panel, sing the retirement measure *softly*, and go ask Vertex what the crate in Saltmouth has been teaching the desert.

She will say *nothing*. She will say it in chalk.
