*Pages recovered from the waxed satchel in the ridge cache at the turbine row's south foot, still dry, still in order. Rill logged her runs the way she rode them — fast margins, no wasted strokes. Read it in order. It changes handwriting before it changes anything else. — S.W.P.*

## Day one — ridge, morning

North loop. Staples for the survey camp, mail for the row, nine kilos and a *hat*. Sky is honest today — that crisp sand-gold that means the wind is working somewhere far off and politely. I let a dust-fox finish crossing. Three minutes, one small perfect morning, worth more than the fee.

Camp took delivery, fed me beans, complained I complained about the beans. Standard contract fulfilment.

## Day one — crest, 10:41 (gauge mark agrees)

Crossed the crest line on the gauge's clock and my own, which almost never happens — usually I beat the tower by a breath out of professional vanity. Today we matched. I take that as the desert being *accurate at me.*

The hum started here. Not the bike. Off the left fork, past the gauge, down where the ridge drops its shadow. A note held too steady for a machine, too patient for a storm. I have been storm-rated nine years and I know every sound heat makes. This was heat's *opinion.*

If it happens again tomorrow I am noting it as weather. If it says anything, I am noting it as mail.

## Day two — same line, later than planned

It said something.

Ketch, when you read this, and you will, because you read everything with that guilty archivist face — the wind said its own name and the name had *knots* in it, like Ferro's tally rope, like weather that keeps count. Four-one-seven, the turbines hum, four-one-seven, over and over, and then the hum *blushed* — I don't have another word for a sound changing its mind — and it was four-one-six *point nine*, and the decimals tasted like rain.

I am not lost. Lost is a logistics problem and I am excellent at logistics. I am *being addressed.* There is a difference. Address has a return fee.

## Day three — the notebook may be making me honest

Weather note, per protocol: the gusts are sand-gold with an iron underside, which is wrong for the season and right for something older. The almanac would call it a Grazer moving early. The almanac is a fine book and it has never heard this.

Liturgy note, per nobody's protocol: *the count is the cargo. the count is the cargo.* That rhymes in a way I did not do on purpose, and I am leaving it in, because crossing it out would be a lie, and a courier's log is the only scripture we get.

I tied the jacket to the cache marker facing the hollow. If you find it, the knot is not a distress knot. It is a *direction.* Read the knot-side.

## Undated — the ink changed

Four-one-six point nine. I checked it against the teal line and the teal line *agreed before I finished checking,* which means either the desert reads my log or my log reads the desert, and I no longer remember which of us is the courier.

Don't follow the ink, junior. Follow the count. It's warm. It has been waiting two hundred years to be *delivered.*

*— R.D., storm-rated, still rated*

*(final marginal, Pem's hand: the log ends here, but her transponder went on walking the ridge for another day. Some deliveries complete the courier. File under weather. File under mail.)*
