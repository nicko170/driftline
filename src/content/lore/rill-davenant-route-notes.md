*Found in the map tube of the second-best bike on the Driftline, which means they were filed by hand and read at speed. Bearings are true-north, 0° at the pole-star cairn, the way the old logs do it. Handwriting steady until it isn't. — K.*

---

## The scraps

**Scrap one — ridge line.** 042° off the gate, hold to the leaning turbine, then 010° until the ground goes *musical*. Almanac says take the south shelf. Almanac has never drifted the shelf with a crosswind from the burn scar. Almanac is a coward and I have drawn a little face on it.

**Scrap two — weather.** Hunch: the wall season opens early this year. The goats know — Pemmy has been standing at the gauge with her ears flat, and Pemmy's ears outrank the Guild desk. Second hunch, written smaller: the static on the dead channel is using my *cadence*. Three long, one short. That's my knock on the relay doors. Nobody taught it that.

**Scrap three — margin of scrap two, later.** It's a joke. I've decided it's a joke. Rule for the desert: anything that copies you is either weather or company, and either way you salute and keep the line.

**Scrap four — bearings, underlined.** Gauge camp to turbine row: 355°, twelve minutes at cruise, *not thirteen — thirteen puts you in the dead air where the compass swims.* Then due 090° to the pylons. The pylons past the crest hum a fourth below the cells. Nice note. Found myself holding the bike's hum up to match it, like tuning. Wrote it down so I remember it was *me* who did that.

**Scrap five — the running joke, instalment.** Voice on the channel knows my name now. Not the callsign — the *name*, the one my mother shortened. Joke structure: I say "you'll have to be more specific, the desert's full of Rills," and the static pauses like it's considering. Punchline pending. Fear is data. The data's getting chatty.

**Scrap six — clean card, folded twice.** For whoever rides this tube after me: the salute is not a superstition, the gauge bell is not dead, and if the channel calls you by the short version of your name, that isn't a warning. Check which page your logbook is open to. Then go and meet it *dressed*.

Don't come alone. — R.D.

*The tube smelled of ozone and cold mornings. The little face drawn on the Almanac entry has been preserved. It is, objectively, a good likeness. — K.*
