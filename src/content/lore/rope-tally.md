*Keel Town moorage, mast three, rigger's shelf. The tally-line is forty-one metres of braid, frayed by hands, kept coiled in a crate stencilled NO (a complete inventory system). Transcribed by a Guild clerk under protest — Ferro's, not the clerk's. Commentary by Dockmaster Devanna Shore, and by Ferro, separately, in that order only on paper.*

---

## The line

**Knots 1–88 (the apprentice years).** Plain overhand, tarred line, each one a hull caught and made fast. Ferro tied these left-handed, before the winch took two fingers of the right. He says the left hand was the better rigger all along and the fingers were a fee.

> *Devanna: He re-tied all eighty-eight after the accident, with the left, in one evening. We chalked it to grief. We were wrong about which grief.*

**Knots 89–402.** Figure-eights, graduated by ship class — a skiff gets half a hitch, a grain hauler gets a doubled bight, and the three true skyships of the old register get a splice worked into the line itself, because *a splice is a promise the rope believes in.* Keel Town has hosted three true skyships in Ferro's tenure; captains lie about their tonnage to earn one, and Ferro prices the lie according to how much he enjoyed it.

**The storm-hitches.** Back-hitched, doubled, set proud of the line so your fingers find them in the dark — every storm big enough to earn a name. Sixty-one of them. Knot 341 is the season the west mast went over; Ferro tied that hitch *during*, at the top of the mast, in the wind, because the tally is kept where the work is done. It's blackened. Nobody asks about the blackening — the lightning did it, or Ferro did it, and either answer is a storm story and the Guild pays the same rate.

**Knot 402½.** The *Kestrel's Debt* — the hull that slipped her far mooring, crossed thirty klicks of dark, and came home by herself. Ferro tied her a full knot, then tied a half beside it, *for the trip she made alone.* Moorage pay: one knot, one half-knot, and a standing tab the kettle still owes him.

**Knots 403–1,190 (the union years).** Reclaimer salvage barges, Guild tithe-floats, Choir lamp-barges that arrive without being scheduled and leave without being cast off. The Choir vessels get cow hitches — *easy to undo, no argument with the tide* — which is the closest Ferro has ever come to a theological statement.

## The knot he invented

**The Lio bend.** Knot 1,191, and after it, the line changes: same braid, new knot, one Ferro invented. Quick to tie dying-light, holds any load, comes free with one pull of the tail when you *mean* it and never before. The whole moorage uses it now; every rigger in Keel Town ties the Lio bend daily and says the name daily, and Ferro hears it daily, and has never once said who Lio is.

Recorded attempts to find out: nine formal (Guild audit, two visits from family, one marriage proposal structured as an enquiry), uncounted informal. The tally itself is the only answer he'll give. The half-hitch before knot 1,191 is slipped — tied to come undone — the only slipped knot in forty-one metres of keeping.

> *Devanna, closing: He says a tally is a ledger of things that didn't get away. Note that the slipped knot stays on the line. Note that he checks it first, mornings.*

*Ferro is semi-retired, which means he does everything but admit it. Ships moored to date: 1,847. Storms: 61. Names he answers to: Ferro, Old Ferro, and — once, at a relay fire, not repeated — Cap.*
