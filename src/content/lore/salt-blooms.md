## What Is a Salt Bloom?

A salt bloom is a garden that grows out of the flat when the desert is feeling generous, which is rarely, or showing off, which is always. After the right dew — the kind Pemmy the goat can smell three days early — the flats push up crystals in fans and towers and little stair-step castles, white and bone and sometimes pink if the deep salts are in a mood.

A small bloom is knee-high and takes one night. A grand bloom is taller than your father and takes a storm season of quiet. Nobody has ever watched one grow. People have tried. The blooms refuse to be interesting while observed, which scientists call "a mechanism" and grandparents call "manners."

## Are They Alive?

No. Also, do not tell them that.

They are made of salt that remembers being water, which the Choir says is close enough to a soul to sing at, and the Guild says is close enough to a resource to weigh, and everyone else says is very pretty, please stop arguing in front of the crystals.

## What Eats a Salt Bloom?

Nothing! Nothing on Kessa-9 eats salt blooms. Birds land on them. Beetles live in the shade of them. Storms break them and the flats grow them back, which is the closest thing to being eaten a bloom will ever experience. Mrs. Halloran says a world where the prettiest thing is also the least edible is a world with its priorities correct, and she is the head teacher, so it counts.

## Rules for Visitors

1. Look all you want. Sketching is encouraged.
2. Take one (1) small fallen piece, never from a tower. Towers are load-bearing.
3. If you break one, apologise aloud. Nobody can prove this helps. Nobody has stopped doing it.
4. Never lick a bloom. Korbenn did, last year, and he still talks about it, which is the punishment.

## Why Couriers Name Them

A courier's route is marked by towers, turbines, and graves, and it helps to have friends in between. The blooms that come back year after year in the same place get names, the way landmarks do when the people who pass them are always a little lonely and always a little fond. Ask any courier on the east flats who "Margerie" is. They will point. It's rude to ask why it's funny. The bloom leans.

Old couriers say some blooms hum faintly before big storms, the way the deep crystals remember weather that hasn't happened yet. New couriers say that is a superstition. Riders on the Driftline say check whose crate is humming when the flats hum back.
