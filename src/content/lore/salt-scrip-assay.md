*Card 4 of the courier packet, printed on salt-board, issued at the Saltmouth exchange with your licence and a look that says the look is also contractual. Credits are ledger entries; scrip is what the ledger wears when it goes outside. Learn the coat before you trust the man.*

## What scrip is

Pressed salt, food-grade and up, struck at the exchange into **beads** (eighth-tile, market money) and **tiles** (the working unit, four the loaf at fair rates). Guild credits are numbers under seal; scrip is numbered weather. Both are real. On the flats, the one you can hold is realer — hold it *correctly*:

**The assay.** A true tile carries three rim nicks — salt, water, weather, same three dots that stand under the ledger seal (see the recognition card, Card 12, the one you laminated like a sentimentalist). Nick one is square-cut: purity passed. Nick two is round: weighed on the public beam, in the open, because a weighing nobody watches is merely a rumour with scales. Nick three is knife-scraped by the duty factor, fresh, *at handover* — the scrape is the sound of the Guild standing behind the money, and forgers have never once got the sound right.

## The clipper's art

To clip is to shave a tile thin, keep the difference, pass the rest. The punisable arts, in ascending order: **edge-shaving** (detectable by the beam, fined in kind, tile for tile, off your rations), **re-nicking** (detectable by the scrape's *shine*, fined in reputation, which out here is the larger wallet), and **sweating** — warming a stack so beads weep brine for re-pressing — which is technically theft from the tile, physically a puddle, and punished by assignment to the very desk that catches you, for one full season, under supervision, in a chair that squeaks. Recidivism after a season at the desk is nil. The desk has opinions. Ask it.

## Why the Guild weighs in public

Not trust — theatre. The public beam is the exchange's sermon: anyone may watch any tile proven, and the watcher is paid in certainty, the only dividend the Guild has never taxed. The water tower counts cups for the same reason the beam sings on market days: a count nobody can witness is a secret, and secrets, on a drying world, are how you get a riot with a manifest.

## The senior counterfeit

File under Card 12's lesson — *a mark wardens what it marks*. Year 171 a ring pressed the **Bell Series** tiles, ring-down pure, rims nicked to a standard the Guild itself did not then meet. Caught, of course; the Office weighed the evidence and found the forgeries *heavier than issue* — more salt, truer nicks, honest scrape. Ruling of record: certified senior, bought in at par, and the Guild quietly adopted the Bell standard, which it now cites as its own, which, per the ruling, it is. The clippers were assigned to the desk. Two stayed on. One still scrapes the third nick, and the scrape is perfect, and everyone knows, and that, courier, is the sound you're listening for.
