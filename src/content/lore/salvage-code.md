*Standard-issue pocket edition of the Salvage Code, Local 9, Yards of the Cinderflats. Stamped on a strip of crawler deck-plate because paper burns. Annotations in Boss Pyke's hand; she maintains the annotations are "the actual code". — recorded for the Driftline neutral-courier file.*

**Article One.** A machine that can no longer do harm is a machine owed a wake, and a wake has a guest list. *{Pyke: Means you don't strip a thing while it's still dangerous enough to be interesting. IDENTITY OF INTERESTING IS DECIDED BY ME.}*

**Article Two.** The desert takes; the desert does not own. What the storm drops, the storm drops *to somebody*, and priority goes: crew, owner, claimant with tools on site, claimant with paperwork, in that order, when convenient. *{Pyke: "When convenient" is doing a lot of work in this article and it KNOWS it. See: the crawler business, season of the first wall. We had tools. They had a form. The form called my winch crew an armed infestation. We are, at worst, a polite infestation with excellent signage.}*

**Article Three.** Salvage is rescue performed late. *{Pyke: The Guild reads this as sentiment. The Guild's mistake. A stranded crawler is a crew-sized weather event that hasn't finished happening. Strip it fast and fair, and the pan keeps nobody's bones.}*

**Article Four.** No reclaimer strips a door, a seal, a cradle, or anything that hums of its own accord, without a vote, a firewatch, and somebody's aunt present. *{Pyke: Vertex volunteers to be everybody's aunt. This clause exists because of what Anchor Seven did BEFORE it had a vote about it. Point being: some machines deserve a second life and some deserve a long supervised nap, and the crate everyone's so excited about is pending a decision.}*

**Article Five.** When two claims meet, a neutral courier carries both, and the order of the countersign is the law of the day. *{Pyke: Oldest article in here. Older than the Union. The couriers were carrying claim-tokens before there was metal to stamp them on. The Driftline doesn't pick sides; it picks up — both, mostly, at once. Remember that the next time you price a courier, Brinemaster. I know your factor reads these. Hello, Tamsin.}*
