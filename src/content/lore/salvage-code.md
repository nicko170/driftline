## Article One — What salvage is

Salvage is the love of dead machines expressed as labour. A thing that cannot finish its work is owed two services: *the stripping*, by which its good is moved forward into things that still can, and *the thanking*, which is free and which nobody has ever regretted in front of witnesses.

> **P.** — Wrote this myself. Still right.
> **V.** — You wrote the first sentence, Pyke. The thanking clause is mine, and it's the load-bearing one.
> **S.** — The thanking clause once got an entire bulldozer decommissioned without a single bang. Just saying. Historical precedent.
> **V.** — Apprentices do not get *precedent*, Solder. Apprentices get *cordite*.

## Article Three — The first-touch law

**Whoever reaches a wreck first, with tools, and puts a line on it, holds the claim.** Tools means tools: a camera is not a tool, a lawyer is *provably* not a tool, and a flag thrown from a moving crawler at sixty metres is enthusiasm, not contact. The line must carry load. The claimant must be *present at the wreck*, not orbiting it in a fast skiff shouting *dibs* — this amendment was added after the Mesa Incident and is non-negotiable.

> **V.** — The Guild calls this "weather-adjacent squatting." The Guild can eat a winch brake. Politely. With garnish.
> **P.** — Article Three beat their counter-claim in front of the dockmaster *once*, the time the courier ran it honest. The order the tokens cross the desk still decides it, mind. That's the old custom and we don't cheat couriers. Couriers remember.
> **S.** — Everyone remembers. That's the desert's whole job.

## Article Five — Hazard shares

A wreck pays in proportion to what it tried to do to you. Humps, colleagues, hot seams, biting edges: weighed at the scale, on the day, *before* tea. Shares of the result go one part to the hands, one part to the spotter (the sky-watch is a hand), one part to the widow-or-worse fund, one part to whoever wrote the pull song that season.

> **S.** — Question: does the wreck *intend* harm, or is it only weather with loading issues?
> **V.** — A structure has intent the moment it drops a beam and aims. I've had beams *introduce themselves*. The audit position is we don't interrogate the beams, we bill them.
> **P.** — Side note the auditors hate: if a wreck is still *warm*, more care, more shares. "Warm" means still powered, still moving, still pretending. Ask Vertex. If Vertex hums while looking at it, double shares. If Vertex goes quiet, *leave the valley*.

## Article Nine — "Abandoned" (definition; four pages; items omitted here)

A thing is abandoned when its owner has left it, intends not to return for it, AND the desert has *started its paperwork* — rust, drift-sand, birds, in that order of legal weight. Sub-clause 9.4(π): **a door is only abandoned if its hinge has voted** — that is, if it swings free and is not held *shut* from the other side for any reason, including interior phenomena the pull team declines to name. *See Hymns for a Door for the thanking service. See also: Article Five, last note — if a door goes quiet, leave the valley.*

> **V.** — I wrote 9.4(π) after the Lighthouse Affair. It has never once been wrong.
> **P.** — It's four pages about *doors*.
> **V.** — And yet you are alive to complain about them, Pyke. Curious.
> **S.** — I love it here.

*Code ends. The back cover bears the union stamp and, below it, in grease pencil: "The code is a door. Hinge votes are ongoing."*
