*Notices pinned to the Saltmouth job board's underside (the top side is employment; the underside is survival), collected and copied by courier consensus. Stars are awarded in MINUTES — the reviewer's estimate of how much longer they would have lived inside than outside. Ten minutes is a palace. Negative ratings exist. Ask about Culvert Nine outdoors, where it can hear you.*

## The Cellar at Beacon 12 — 9 minutes

Doors seat true, sand seal holds, and somebody — name withheld, we all know it's Old Bahro — leaves sealed water and a rude note about couriers who don't reseal the water. Bench sleeps two if they like each other or one if they don't. Minus a minute because the bell overhead rings all night in a wall and you will dream you are already dead and queueing. Still. Nine minutes. Ride for it.

## Canyon Slot, Duskward Slalom — 6 minutes, asterisk

Flood-carved slot with an overhang lip, fits one bike and one rider and one apology. Asterisk: in a true wall the canyon winds *accelerate*. It is not shelter so much as a very fast room. Six minutes if you wedge the bike crossways, which you will only learn to do by failing to, which is the canyon's entire teaching method. Reviewer's note: "held, somehow. never again. five stars, no stars, six minutes."

## Choir Hollow Vestry Annex — 4 minutes, complicated

They will take you in. The Hollow takes everyone in; that is the whole pitch. The annex is dry, the mats are clean, and the harmonics through the basalt will gently unhook your thoughts one at a time, which in a storm is arguably a service. Four minutes because you must listen to a novice explain the Sleeping Garden while the world ends outside, and some of those minutes feel like someone else's. Bring earplugs. They think earplugs are funny. They'll get you earplugs.

## Culvert Nine — minus 40 minutes

Condemned. The door opens INWARD, the ceiling is the road, and the previous tenant is still there in the meaningful sense. The Board has ruled the minus rating "legally confusing and experientially accurate."

## Shelter South of Choir Hollow — the highest rating on the Loop

Door: one hinge, holds by faith and a cargo strap. Water: a tank, unlabeled, do not ask. Amenities: none. This is the complete notice, as pinned, in the hand of an anonymous courier, since cosigned by forty-one others:

*has a dog*

The dog is small, grey, old, and storm-proof. When the wall hits he climbs into your lap, puts his chin on your wrist, and watches the slot in the door with total professional interest, as if the storm is a program he has seen before and found good. Forty-one couriers have attested that they cannot account for the hours the storm took. They sat down scared and stood up safe, and in between there was a dog.

No minutes rating has ever been assigned. The Board considered the question and adjourned. Some shelter isn't measured in minutes. The dog does not rate you back. You are welcome anyway.
