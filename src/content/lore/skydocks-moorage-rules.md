*Posted at the winch base, both copies, top and bottom, the middle being subject to sway. Revisions one through six are lost to weather, fire, and litigation respectively — twice to litigation, which is why there are seven. Annotations in the dockmaster's hand are binding where initialed and advisory where legible. — D.S.*

## Rule 1: Of the bells

Approaching vessels ring **once** for intention, **twice** for cargo, **three times** for apology. The mast answers with its own count and the counts must agree before any rope moves. Vessels ringing four or more times are showing off and will be moored last.

*Annotated: Three for apology was added after the* Ferro's Answer *arrived with the wrong bride. The rule has since prevented, by my count, one war and two marriages, neither being the Answer family's fault exactly. — D.S.*

## Rule 2: Of windshear

The vessel higher on the rim has right of way; the vessel lower has right of *complaint*. In crosswind both rights belong to whoever is heavier, as weighed at the Top Scale, whose ruling is final per the Hang-Tenants' Agreement and whose keeper is not to be bribed with anything under a whole cake.

*Annotated: Heavier-vessel rule stands since year 40 when two clippers argued into a steward, who is fine, and into my good winch, which is not. The wharf-master's cake price is CHARITABLE and I will countersign the letter. — D.S. / Below, different ink: Cake remains the price. Cake remains achievable. — H.F.*

## Rule 3: Of ballast

All ballast is taxed by the ton and by the story. A vessel unloading ballast must declare what is being left behind; a vessel *taking on* ballast must declare what is being carried out of accountable reach. Water in active use is exempt. Grief is exempt. Unlisted goods are neither exempt nor taxed. They are *noted*.

*Annotated: "Noted" has ended more tariffs than the Guild knows. The Summit years proved that what Keel Town notes, the desert receipts. — D.S.*

## Rule 4: Of the Balloon Incident

No vessel may be lighter than its own paperwork. Any vessel found to be lighter than its own paperwork will be **lashed to its manifest** and lowered until honest.

*Annotated: The Incident is not to be re-litigated, re-enacted at festivals, or taught to children as a game. The child in question is grown, flies couriers out of Saltmouth now, and tells it better than the rules do. — D.S.*

## Rule 5: Of the night moorings

Lamps to double at dusk; the mesa shadow to be beaded by the lower gantry lanterns on still nights for the town's morale, which is a cargo class. Night docking is half rope, half faith; faith surcharges apply and are refundable only in song.

*Annotated: Shipping line crews keep trying to pay the faith surcharge in verse. The verse keeps being accepted. My office is not a temple. My office keeps receipts, and the receipts are couplets. — D.S.*

## Rule 6: Of the unlisted column

What is not listed is not moored. What is not moored is not subject to these rules. This clause is its own punishment. Vessels chalked in the unlisted column are to be swept around, never swept *at*.

*Annotated: Chalk appears at dawn, six words, hand familiar enough that I am not answering the question. The slate was bargained for in good wax and the column was thrown in free, the way storms are. — D.S.*

## Rule 7: Of the Swing

Once yearly, calm season, master struts slacked a hand's width, per Agreement, against my written advice, which I file and then enjoy like everyone else. Berth 3 is held for the *Pale Lark*, expected nine years; swept weekly; **do not discuss**.

*Annotated: Discussed twice behind the winch house, resolve reported in the Gazette, which I also write, so: resolved. Keep sweeping. — D.S. / Counter-annotated, chalk, no initials: The Lark is expected the way rain is expected. Berth stays swept either way. That's the whole religion up here, Dockmaster, and you sweep.*
