## The walls, by name

Couriers name storms the way farmers name weather vanes — affectionately, and wrong. The working taxonomy, refined over four decades of being chased by examples:

**The Walker.** Slow, wide, polite. Crosses the flats at a pace you can outrun in second gear while composing your story for the moorage. The Guild pays full shift for a Walker and nobody has ever complained.

**The Sprinter.** Narrow front, obscene speed. Comes off Windspine like it owes someone money. Outrunning a Sprinter is a boost-management exam; the trick is to drift the exits and never boost into the face of your own fear.

**The Grazer.** Sits on a route for days, eating visibility. Not fast. Just *there*, like a debt. Reroute. Accept the reroute. Do not race a Grazer out of spite; the logbooks of the spiteful are short and well-punctuated.

**The Choirwall.** Any wall whose static band sings in patterns. The Choir logs it as liturgy, the Guild logs it as an obstruction, your radio logs it in your bones. Courier rule: it isn't singing. If it is singing, that's the Choir's problem, and they are welcome to it.

## Shelter etiquette

Any roof, any wall, any overhang: storm right takes precedence over property, grudge, and queue. You share the lee with whoever got there first, including Reclaimers, including the person who shorted your pay in Pelican Season. You do not discuss either fact until the wall passes. Ketch's addendum, adopted as canon: *"The person who arrives wettest gets the good spot. The person who arrives on fire gets it regardless of moisture."*

## The annual correction (printed in red)

Rule 12, the twelve-minute rule, is **not thirteen minutes**. This sentence is reprinted every year in increasing type sizes because every year someone learns it personally. The 44th edition uses the largest type the press owns.

## When the wind sings

Hold speed. Hold line. Write down what it says *after* you are indoors. The almanac does not adjudicate whether the desert talks; it notes, drily, that Rill Davenant wrote down coordinates, and Rill Davenant is a chapter of someone else's almanac now.
