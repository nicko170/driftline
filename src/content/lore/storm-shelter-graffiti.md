*Transcribed by the rostered keeper of the Windspine shelters "because the walls are the only book that gets longer every season." Copy donated to the Codex with the keeper's note: walls first, transcription second, both outlast the roof.*

## The useful

**WATER CACHE BEHIND THE THIRD BUNK, I REFILL IT, REFILL IT TOO** — dated across forty years, six hands, the last adding: *still true, check the seal.* This is the wall at its best: a slow conversation between strangers who met only the storm.

**IF THE DOOR HUMS, THE WALL IS CLOSE. IF THE WALL SINGS, THE DOOR IS THE LEAST OF IT.** Unannotated. Nobody dares improve it.

**PUMP HANDLE IS LEFT-THREADED. YES, REALLY. LOVE, EVERYONE.**

## The true

**I WAITED OUT THE WALL NEXT TO A GUILD FACTOR. WOULD AGAIN. — R.C.** Answered below by a different hand, presumably the factor: **HE SNORED. RATES DOUBLED. — S.G.**

**CARRIED A STRANGER'S CRATE HERE WHEN MINE FAILED AT THE DOOR. YOU KNOW WHO. CRATE WAS CAKE. STILL RIGHT.** Two centuries of shelter cooking owes that crate an apology it will never get.

**THE GOAT CAME IN BY HERSELF, STOOD ON MY BED, ATE MY SPARE GOGGLES, AND THE STORM PASSED. I DON'T KNOW WHAT CHURCH THIS IS. I'M STAYING.**

## The unprintable

Nine columns of it, increasingly historical. The keeper transcribes only the closing entry, from year 19, which has become the unofficial shelter toast: **"SEASON'S WORST WALL, AND STILL BETTER COMPANY THAN THE BOARD OF —"** [remainder lost to a repair patch; the keeper maintains the patch was load-bearing in every sense.]

## The chalk mark, Shelter Six

Low on the south wall, in soft white chalk that has never flaked, never been scrubbed, never faded: a small neat hand printing **SIX**, and under it **YOU'LL WANT ROOF HERE.** The survey says Shelter Six was poured in year 2 of the Delay. The pre-Delay build sheets say the concrete for that wall came off a terraforming barge that never arrived. The chalk, the youngest dating says, is forty years *older* than the wall it's written on. The Choir has lit a lamp beside it. The keeper dusts it first. On storm nights, keepers of a certain humour swear the chalk is a shade fresher, as if someone has been by, checking their work.
