## The rule under the rules

Water costs. Heat costs. Time costs. Tea is what happens when you spend all three on somebody else, and that is the entire religion of the flats, everything else is liturgy.

## The teas

**Salt tea.** The daily cup. A pinch of pan salt in the brew, which sounds wrong and tastes *correct* — the salt pulls the bitter forward and sends it back changed. Served to everyone, including debt collectors, *especially* debt collectors. Refusing salt tea is legally an insult in Keel Town and spiritually one everywhere else.

**Storm tea.** Brewed strong and drunk fast when the wall is visible, borders on the superstitious except that it works: the ritual is to pour one cup for each person in the shelter and one extra, set by the door, *for whoever's still out there*. The extra cup is drunk by the last arrival, warm or cold, and woe betide the shelter where the pot ran short. Shelters count their cups the way the Guild counts water.

**The apology brew.** Weak, over-steeped, deliberately imperfect. You bring it, you pour it, you say nothing while it's drunk. The awfulness of the tea is the apology sized correctly; a good tea would be showing off. If the injured party refills your cup afterward, you are forgiven. If they fill it to the top, you are *family*, and must now attend all their storms.

## On cups

Never fill a guest's cup to the top. A full cup says *you will not be here long*. The space at the rim is the length of the welcome.

## What to bring a weather desk

The Weather Desk drinks whatever it's given and forecasts accordingly, so bring the good leaves. The caprine division (see: Pemmy) takes tea in a shallow pan, and the posted sheet confirms the goat's accuracy improves 4% on oolong. Guild factors pretend not to stock oolong. Guild factors stock oolong.

## The courier's cup

Driftline custom: when a courier doesn't come back, their cup is hung outside the relay post, upside down, handle east. It comes down when the desert gives the courier back, *whichever form the giving takes*. Rill Davenant's cup is down. This pamphlet has nothing further to say about it and neither should you.
