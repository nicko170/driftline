*Recovered from the chart-table at the Windspine survey camp. Two documents, pinned together with a rosette pin and a note that says only: STOP LAMINATING THESE TOGETHER, IT CONFUSES VISITORS. — S.K.*

## Exhibit A: The Prospectus Map (Year 0)

Printed on bonded weave, meant to last fifty years and outliving its warranty by a century and a half. The oceans are marked in a confident blue at Sea Level (Projected). Rivers run where the gravity models said they would run, in the polite, well-spaced way of rivers in brochures. Forest belts follow the thirty-ninth parallel with the exactitude of a hem. Kessa-9, in this map, is a garden that has already agreed to be one.

Settlements are labelled in optimistic present tense: Orchardhold. Rivermeet. Lake Merit. None of these places ever existed. Two of them technically never *stopped* not-existing: the survey file lists both as ACTIVE (PENDING), a status that has been pending since before my grandmother's grandmother was surveyed herself.

## Exhibit B: The Honest Chart (Year 214)

My copy. Salt flats where Ocean Prime was meant to sit, dune fields over the planned forestry, the Glassroad where Reactor Burn Two wrote its signature down a canyon that wasn't in the brochure. The settlements have real names now, the kind people earn: Saltmouth, Keel Town, the Hollow, the Yard. The wind farms run the ridge the prospectus reserved for, and I quote, 'agreeable meadow'.

## Marginal Notes, Transcribed

*On the blue oceans:* "Water table did read twelve metres here once. I checked. Then I checked twice, because you don't get to be right about an ocean very often."

*On the forests:* "Found four trees. They know who they are. Cordial relations."

*On the change:* "The prospectus promised us the planet it *meant*. This chart promises the one we got. I've stopped being angry about the difference, because the second one is where everyone I love actually lives, and you cannot laminate a home into a lie, no matter how the printers beg you."

*Pinned in the corner, in different ink:* "The weird part isn't that the money ran out. The weird part is that the desert kept the appointment anyway. MOTHER was paid to finish this place and never got to. Six days from the gate and the whole basin *leans*, like something out here still wants finishing. Keep the maps pinned together. I like the neighbours."

— K., Surveyor's Guild (unaffiliated), Windspine Ridge
