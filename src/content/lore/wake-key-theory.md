*Working notes, Bench One, the Yards. Union rules apply: all speculation logged, all tools accounted for, all casserole invitations honoured. These notes concern the courier's crate — the sealed one, the hummer, the one the Guild can't weigh and the Choir won't stop singing about — and what it would take to open it. Compiled by Solder. Annotated by the boss. Obey the annotation.*

## Assessment

The crate is not a container, chief. I've cut open two hundred years of sealed terraforming stock and I'll tell you what a container does under a probe: it *resists where it's thin.* This thing resists where it's *going to be thin.* Probe the lid and the base stiffens. Warm the seam and the hum comes up a half-tone, pleasant, like it appreciates the attention. That's not armour. Armour gets scarred. That's *occasion.* It wants the right opener, not the strongest one.

Union position, preliminary not final, hold my apron: it's a **wake-key.** Terraforming yardscraft — old cradle gear — used to ship dormant machines as paired halves: a sleeper and a nudge. The nudge wandered the grid until it found its sleeper's handshake. We stopped finding keys a century back. We are finding one now, and it is on a *courier route*, which is either a coincidence or the most efficient delivery arrangement in Yards history.

## The quotes

**Quote one (Solder, bench-rate, honest):** Full disassembly, union supervision, documented. One hundred forty hours, three torches I would not get back, and a signed waiver for "events of meaning." Forty thousand credits. Firm.

**Quote two (Aunt Vertex, sight unseen, from her anchor):** "Bring it to me and I will open it the way Anchor Seven opened — all at once, in a light the sky remembers. Free of charge. Price afterwards." Vertex is quoting *ceremony*, not labour. Declined on principle and on flooring.

**Quote three (anonymous, slid under the yard gate, Salt Guild wax):** "Open it. We will buy whatever falls out, at any price, in perpetuity. If nothing falls out, we will buy the nothing." The estimate says *nothing* like a quoted weight. Declined on superstition, which is Local 9's word for *experience without paperwork.*

## Casserole (Bench One, serves a crew)

While the quotes were out I made the working casserole, per rule: layer of chipped-root, layer of salted scrap-beans, cheese if the Guild's been paid, crushed pan-bread on top, baked on the housing of a machine *that was not yet disassembled*, because the boss says dinner should sit on the future and warm through. It fed six. Nobody spoke during the first serving. This is logged as a finding.

## Ruling

**[In Boss Pyke's hand, grease pencil, underlined hard enough to emboss:]**

*Not yet. I have spent forty years waking things that deserved a second go and one waking that the scar still whistles about. The hum on this one is not a machine asking to be on. It is a machine asking to be MET. You don't open a knock, sprout — you answer the door or you don't, but either way you do it dressed. The crate stays sealed until the desert sets a table. Stocktake the torches, stand the crews down, and somebody find me that courier. I want to look at who it's humming FOR.*

*— P. Shift bell. Casserole at seven.*
