## Article I — Rain

Rain belongs to no one. **AMENDED, year two:** rain *in collectors* belongs to the collector's owner, less the tower's tithe. **AMENDED, year four:** rain that *misses* the collector by less than one standard cup's width (see Article III) is deemed *aimed* and also tithed. **AMENDED, year four, later that same afternoon, in different ink:** prior amendment suspended *pending a definition of aim*. It remains suspended. Rain, technically, is still getting away with it, and the tower keeps an unofficial ledger anyway, in pencil, where the rain can't see.

## Article II — The concept of thirst

Thirst is a *state* and not a *claim*. A body may be thirsty without being owed, and may be owed without showing it, and the tower serves both equally *at the window* and neither in the street. **Provision, unamended, marked with a star:** *no one asks the tower for water and goes dry. The asking is the price. Non-negotiable since the founding, which is why it's a clause and not a toast.*

## Article III — The standard cup

One standard cup: the brass measure on the tower chain, dented, warm at night. Everyone on the flats can go thirsty by exactly one cup without complaint, and exactly two cups with *pointed* complaint, which the town calls "the civic interval." **AMENDED, year twelve:** cups loaned between houses are not water transfers, are not commerce, and absolutely do not enter the ledger. **Repealed, year twelve, Guild hand.** **Reinstated, year twelve, knife hand, deeply scratched:** HOUSE CUPS ARE NOT LEDGER ITEMS. The scratch is legible forty metres off, which paradoxically is what makes it enforceable.

## Article IV — The tower

The tower is its own legal person (see *Water Rights and Other Wells*, article three, re: the night shift). It holds the town's water in the manner of a lighthouse: *not for itself, but for the watching.* Any resident may inspect the level gauge at the civic interval. **AMENDED, year forty-one, Guild hand, small, apologetic:** inspections during storms are deferred to the first clear dawn. **AMENDMENT NOTE, unknown hand, small, *not* apologetic:** the town inspected anyway. It was fine. Add it to the thanking service.

## Article VII — The hydration provision

Whereas the Salt Guild holds custody of the mains, the condensers, and the arithmetic; and whereas custody is not communion: **the Guild GRACIOUSLY PERMITS the daily hydration of Saltmouth, its crews, its couriers, its guests, and one (1) goat of disputed employment**, subject to the fair-price doctrine and the civic interval. The provision is re-read aloud at the dusting festival each year, in full, with feeling, *and the Guild stands through the word "graciously" without flinching, which everyone agrees is the true annual event.*

**AMENDED, year sixty:** the goat is named. The goat's name is withheld from this record *at the goat's request*, and yes, the request is on file, and yes, it's binding. The Guild checked. The Guild checked like it was a debt.

*Filed under: Charter, Standing. The knife is chained to the kiosk. Amendments by prior arrangement and one witness. — t.c.*
