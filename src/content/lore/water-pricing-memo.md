The charter it hangs beside has knife amendments; this one has pencil, because nobody dares.*

## The Arithmetic

Water on Kessa-9 is not *found*. Water is *manufactured*, nightly, by condensers that breathe the thin humidity off the salt and comb it into litres, and like all manufacturing it has inputs, and inputs have prices. A condenser drinks power. Power comes from the wind or the array. The wind belongs to everyone, which is to say the Guild maintains the lines for everyone, which is to say the lines have a maintenance schedule, and the schedule has a rate column, and you — valued customer, valued *settler*, valued party to Article VII of the water charter — are at the far end of a long and honest chain of arithmetic.

Drink slowly. It spoils the curve if you gulp.

## The Tower Fee

The tower at Saltmouth is not merely a tank. The tower is a *promise of height*. Water that has been lifted possesses what our billing department calls *positional dignity*, which is to say it will flow downhill on command to any tap in the grid without further pumping. The fee covers the lifting, the promise, and the small brass plaque. The plaque alone, we are required to note, cost more than a season of lifting, which is why the fee is calculated per litre and not per plaque.

## The Surcharge (Not a Sun Tax)

Between first bell and last, all grid deliveries carry a Daylight Operational Surcharge. It has been suggested — in the margins of this very document, in a hand the Auditor recognises and declines to prosecute — that this is "a tax on the sun." It is not. The sun is free. The sun has always been free. What is billed is the *increased evaporation risk, increased rivet expansion, and increased staff discomfort* experienced by water while the sun is acting upon it. The water suffers. Someone must be compensated. The Guild, holding the water on your behalf during its suffering, is the natural party.

Night delivery is surcharge-free. Nobody requests night delivery. The Guild does not speculate as to why.

## Why the Goat Signs

Per the methodology documents of the Guild weather desk (caprine division), all seasonal water forecasts affecting the bulk rate must be countersigned by the duty forecaster. The duty forecaster is Pemmy. Pemmy is a goat. Pemmy signs with a dipped hoof, and the hoof-print is legally a signature, because in year 141 the Ledger Office attempted to amend the requirement and the amendment is *still in committee*, and water, counsel ruled, cannot wait for committees. The goat reads the sky better than the machines did, and the machines are the ones saying so. If you would like a cheaper signature, the Guild invites you to breed a better goat.

## A Note on Fairness

Since the last wire went quiet (see *The Last Wire*, framed, left-hand wall), the Guild has carried the arithmetic alone. Nobody else volunteered. When the day comes that the sky delivers water for free, the Guild will of course adjust its prices to remain competitive, and looks forward to the challenge.

In the meantime: everything crosses. Everything's counted. And the counting is priced to move.
