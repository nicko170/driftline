*Brass plate, bolted to the winch base at the Skydocks, one metre up so you read it at chest height like a confession. The original Guild text is engraved. The annotations are in three hands: the First Rigger's neat script, the Second's broad pencil, and the Third's grease-pencil capitals, each generation answering the last.*

---

## As Engraved (Guild Issue, Rev. 7)

**Article the First.** The line is loaded before it is asked. Trust it accordingly.

**Article the Second.** No hand touches a moving line. The line does not know you. The line does not want to.

**Article the Third.** The knot holds what it is tied to hold, neither more nor your excuses.

**Article the Fourth.** A frayed line retired with honours outranks a sound line spent in vanity.

**Article the Fifth.** The load does not care about your schedule. The schedule has been notified.

## The Knots (as sung)

*The engraved diagrams wore off in year six. The riggers replaced them with rhyme, because a rhyme survives a storm better than a picture and can be shouted over wind.*

Over and under and kiss on the crown —
what rides up gentle comes gently down.

Twice around timber and tuck her head —
a line that lies is a line that's dead.

Bowline, belay, and bless the throat —
she climbs like a lark and she lands like a boat.

## The Annotations

*First hand (script, devout):* Rev. 7 omits the Blessing of the Drum. We say it anyway. *Drum, hold what we gave you. We will ask how it went.*

*Second hand (pencil, pragmatic):* The Blessing is not regulation. The Blessing is *why we're alive*. Article the Second, addendum — the line DOES know you. It knows your timing. Give it bad timing and it will introduce you to the sky.

*Third hand (grease pencil):* WHOEVER RETIED THE GUIDE LINE WITH A GRANNY KNOT OWES THE YARD A NEW GUIDE LINE AND THE CLINIC A APOLOGY. RHYME THREE, PEOPLE. SHE CLIMBS LIKE A LARK. A LARK.

*First hand, afterthought, tiny:* The winch is the oldest machine in Keel Town and the only one that has never dropped a soul. On quiet nights the drum ticks in fives. Count them. That's the catechism too.
